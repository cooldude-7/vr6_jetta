# Pick the BLV, keep the Jetta's bones

For a manual 3.6 VR6 swap into a 2015–2018 Mk6 Jetta GLI, the parts that go together are Ninety4co's Mk6 GTI recipe with Jetta substitutions in three places (radiator and lock carrier, A/C discharge line, axles), and the engine should be the **2006–2010 B6 Passat BLV on Bosch MED9**, not the 2012–2018 NMS Passat engine this project has been calling "CDVC". The NMS engine is **CDVB** on Bosch MED17.1.6 (ECU family 03H 906 023); CDVC and CDVD are the 2018+ Atlas/Teramont codes, and that label is used throughout this report. The CDVB is the better engine on paper (newer, factory one-piece oil-pump sprocket, same electrical generation as the Jetta, same subframe and mount part numbers as the Jetta), but **no tuner sells a manual-transmission or swap calibration for the MED17 3.6**, Ross-Tech's own administrator is "quite skeptical" the ECU will accept manual coding, and no CDVB has ever been documented running in a PQ35 car. The BLV has a published swap-tune product (United Motorsport's "3.6L FSI VR6 Passat '06 to '08"), a tuner who has done this exact job (Reflect Tuning), and a pin-by-pin wiring sheet. Mechanically the Jetta is a Golf at every mount interface: the VR6 engine mount **1K0 199 262 AR**, trans mount **1K0 199 555 AP**, the Jetta's own dogbone, shift cables, axles and concentric slave cylinder all carry 1K0 part numbers shared with the Mk6 Golf, so "bolts in" rests on catalog equivalence plus the GTI precedent. The gearbox side (used Mk4 24V 02M plus a South Bend clutch) is about **US$1,100** and is the most settled part of the build; wiring is still a **200–300 hour** re-pin job that must be re-derived from factory erWin diagrams for both VINs; and the listed vendor prices for the chassis, gearbox, cooling, exhaust and ECU-service items sum to roughly **US$3,100–3,900 before the engine, the tune, bearings and machine work, hoses, and the custom A/C and exhaust sections**. Every claim below keeps its VERIFIED / REPORTED / INFERRED label; anything REPORTED or INFERRED that carries a part number still needs a VIN-based parts-catalog check at a dealer counter before money changes hands.

How to read the labels: **VERIFIED** means the researcher read it in a factory document, a parts-catalog fitment page, a vendor product page or a VCDS log; **REPORTED** means a forum post, marketplace listing, build video or search snippet whose page could not be opened; **INFERRED** means reasoning from the above. Prices are US dollars from US vendors unless marked CAD; shipping, duty and HST to Ottawa were not researched.

## The NMS donor is CDVB, and CDVC belongs to the Atlas

The project's working label needs fixing before any part is bought by engine code. VW's own Self-Study Program for the 2016 Passat prints the engine block as "Engine Code BWS … Bosch Motronic MED 17.1 … 280 hp (206 kW) at 6600 rpm … 258 lb/ft (350 Nm) at 2400 to 5300 rpm", which is itself a carried-over spec block (BWS is the 300-PS R36/CC code elsewhere) and should not be used to order parts (**VERIFIED**, [SSP 800153](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10121132-9999.pdf)). A separate VW of America 2016 Passat bulletin lists "3.6L VR6: CDVB" in its engine-code table (**VERIFIED**, [NHTSA MC-10168305-0001](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10168305-0001.pdf)), a verified Ross-Tech forum member states "Passat NMS 3.6 VR6 CDVB only comes in FWD DSG" (**REPORTED**) in a thread whose VCDS scan of a 2018 Passat GT identifies the ECU as "VR6 3.6l FSI" **03H 906 023 DC**, hardware 03H 907 309 A (**VERIFIED** log, [Ross-Tech forums 40349](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/40349/)), and a US used-engine seller lists "engine ID CDVB" for every 2012–2017 Passat 3.6 (**REPORTED**). Every source that places **CDVC** puts it in the Atlas: autofiles lists CDVC for the "Volkswagen Atlas 2018–2022" (**VERIFIED**, [autofiles](https://autofiles.com/engine-type/vag/ea390/36-vr6-fsi/)), mymotorlist dates CDVC to "2016–present, Atlas and Teramont" and never mentions the NMS Passat (**VERIFIED**, [mymotorlist](https://mymotorlist.com/engines/volkswagen/ea390/cdvc/)), and the firmware catalogue that gave this project its "03H 906 026 E, MED17.1.62" ECU number is an Atlas entry (**VERIFIED**, [c4ip.ru](https://c4ip.ru/en/vw/145555-vw-atlas-ca-3-6l-vr6-fsi-cdvc-206kw-280ps-med17-1-62-10sw059936-03h906026e-9970-stock-eeprom-bin)). One of the research notes (the electrical file) still carried the 03H 906 026 family forward for the NMS engine; that number is the Atlas ECU and is superseded here by the 03H 906 023 family (**INFERRED** from the sources above).

Why it matters: the NMS Passat ECU, the Atlas ECU and the B6 BLV ECU are three different Bosch families. The BLV runs MED9.1.1 in the **03H 906 032** family (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech wiki, Passat 3C](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/VW_Passat_(3C))); the CDVB runs MED17.1.6 in **03H 906 023** (Bosch lists sibling suffixes AQ/AC/AL for the Touareg 3.6, **VERIFIED**, [Bosch 0261S07622](https://www.bosch-repair-service.com/en/engine-control-unit/bosch/med17-1-6-0261s07622001/)); the Atlas runs MED17.1.62 in 03H 906 026. Immobilizer-defeat vendors, tuners and parts sellers all key off these numbers, so a "CDVC" search finds Atlas parts and services. The 2012–2018 NMS 3.6 was sold in Canada every year (Comfortline/Highline 2015, Highline-only 2017, a "very small quantity" of 2018 GTs) and was always DSG and front-drive (**REPORTED**, [autotrader.ca](https://autotrader.ca/research/volkswagen/passat/2015/); [RPM Canada](https://rpmcanada.ca/?p=6285); **VERIFIED**, [Wikipedia NMS Passat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Passat_(North_America_and_China))). Any 2012–2018 year is mechanically equivalent (same 280 hp / 258 lb-ft throughout), and the engine arrives with factory versions of two fixes Ninety4co had to retrofit on his BLV: a "one-part oil pump chain sprocket" and a "non-engaged chain tensioner" (**VERIFIED**, [SSP 800153](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10121132-9999.pdf)). Those are the CDVB's genuine advantages; the rest of this report explains why they are outweighed for a manual build.

## Every mount interface on the Jetta is a Golf part, so Ninety4co's chassis recipe transfers

The Mk6 Jetta (A6/NCS) is "partly based on the same PQ35 platform (slight adaptation of the Golf front axle)", with a 2,650 mm wheelbase against the Golf Mk6's 2,578 mm and no shared body panels (**VERIFIED**, [Wikipedia Jetta A6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Jetta_(A6))). What matters for the swap is that the factory catalog fits identical 1K0-prefix mount, dogbone, shift-cable, slave-cylinder and spring part numbers to both the Golf Mk6 and the Jetta Mk6, which is why Ninety4co's "bolted straight on, nothing modified" result in a 2010 GTI ([Part 3, 18:14](https://youtu.be/Z0YHrkM1E1Q)) should carry over. The Jetta-specific parts are the front subframe **5C0 199 313 N**, which the Jetta shares with the NMS Passat 3.6 and the Beetle A5 rather than the Golf (**VERIFIED**, [oemwolf 5C0199313N](https://oemwolf.com/oem-parts/5c0199313n.html)), the lock carrier **5C6 805 588 D** and the radiator **5C0 121 251 E** (**VERIFIED**, [oemwolf 5C6805588](https://oemwolf.com/oem-parts/5c6805588.html); [oemwolf 5C0121251E](https://oemwolf.com/oem-parts/5c0121251e.html)). The subframe sharing is a quiet piece of good news: the steering-rack, dogbone-bracket and control-arm envelope under the Jetta has carried a 3.6 VR6 in production (**INFERRED**). The two unmeasured clearances are hood-to-intake and firewall-to-rear-manifold; nobody has published either for a 3.6 in a Mk6 Jetta, and the only PQ35 data point is Ninety4co's "tight side to side" in a Golf-width bay (**REPORTED**).

Two details in that table are worth dwelling on. Second, the trans-mount choice is set entirely by the bolt pattern on the gearbox case, not by the chassis: all five PQ35 left mounts (AP, Q, N, CA, CH) share the same console, so a 2-bolt Mk4 02M takes AP and a 3-bolt TT/R32 02M takes Q or CH (**VERIFIED** shared console, **REPORTED** pattern per Ninety4co's doc, [parts list](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lUm4Pee3PA9zaG5dA_x5aLbu-WO3YJs_4todYXtXFYo/edit)). ## The 02M gearbox side is the most settled US$1,100 of the build

The GLI's own 02Q is useless for this swap: it is a 4-cylinder housing with an 8-bolt TSI flywheel, and FCP Euro's North American application list shows no VR6 under 02Q at all (**VERIFIED**, [FCP Euro MQ350 guide](https://www.fcpeuro.com/blog/definitive-guide-vw-audi-6-speed-manual-transmissions-mq350-02m-02q-0fb)). The only front-drive VR6-pattern manual sold here is the **2002.5–2005 Golf GTI 24V / 2002.5–2004 Jetta GLI 24V 02M**, roughly a three-model-year population now twenty years old; the 2004 R32 and 2008–09 Mk2 TT 3.2 02Ms are VR6-pattern but 4Motion, convertible to FWD with Epytec's kit 698 (adapter plate, seal 02M 409 189, flange 02M 409 356 A, shorter 2WD bolt), at the cost of shorter ~4.2/3.3 finals and, on the TT box, the 3-bolt mount pattern (**VERIFIED** Epytec, [kit 698](https://epytec.de/en/6-speed-manual-gearbox-conversion-kit-02m-4motion-vr6-r32-golf-mk1-2-3-4-turbo-698); **REPORTED** finals). Buy whatever sound FWD 24V box appears rather than waiting for a specific code; Ninety4co's cost **$400 including OEM steel shift forks** on Facebook Marketplace in 2023 (**REPORTED**, [Part 2](https://youtu.be/wTqhQoPT48I)). The 24V box's 3.94/3.09 dual finals are the tallest of the VR6 02Ms, which suits 350 Nm (**REPORTED** ratios, **INFERRED** suitability). VW rates the MQ350 at **258 lb-ft**, exactly the 3.6's torque, and FCP says the boxes handle 350–400 lb-ft "provided that the driver is not unnecessarily rough"; the known weak points are brass 1-2/3-4 shift forks, 2nd/3rd synchros, input-shaft play and the open diff (**VERIFIED**, [FCP Euro](https://www.fcpeuro.com/blog/definitive-guide-vw-audi-6-speed-manual-transmissions-mq350-02m-02q-0fb)).

Three verification items hide in that table. The K70287 root is South Bend's Mk4/Mk1-TT 6-speed application and is also sold in a 1.8T (6-bolt crank) form, so the order must be the **VR6 10-bolt** variant; the "-DMF" suffix most plausibly means "for a car that left the factory with a dual-mass flywheel, supplied with South Bend's single-mass flywheel", but South Bend should confirm before shipping (**INFERRED** from [UroTuning](https://www.urotuning.com/search?type=product&q=K70287) and [BMP](https://www.bmptuning.com/products/south-bend-stage-2-endurance-clutch-kit-6-speed-mk4-1j-mk1-8n)). The 3.6 donor's own "flywheel" is a DSG drive plate, so the flywheel must come with the clutch kit (**INFERRED**). And Ninety4co's first input-shaft and axle seals were the wrong size, so pull seal, fork and bearing numbers from ETKA by gearbox code, not by "Mk4 02M" (**REPORTED**, [Part 2](https://youtu.be/wTqhQoPT48I)). If the case is split for an LSD, that is the moment for forks, input bearings, all seals and diff bearings.

The secondary path, keeping the NMS Passat's own **02E DQ250**, is coherent only in one configuration: CDVB engine plus its own 02E, mechatronic and selector, into a GLI that was already DSG. The 6-cylinder DQ250 has "a star-shaped flange and a tripod joint" output unlike the 4-cylinder 02E and the manual boxes (**VERIFIED**, [Epytec ISP106](https://epytec.de/en/drive-shaft-vw-golf-mk2-mk3-corrado-passat-vr6-axle-conversion-02m-02q-dq250-dsg-6-speed-isp106)), Passat FWD axles are longer than Golf axles and one owner "fought with the axles" (**REPORTED**, [VWVortex 8929969](https://www.vwvortex.com/threads/3-6-4motion-6-speed-swap.8929969/)), the DSG mechatronic J743 "sends its query and authorisation request to J362" and so is itself an immobilizer participant (**VERIFIED**, [SSP 800153](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10121132-9999.pdf)), and the only PQ35 3.6+DSG cars that exist are HPA's and AW Racing's turbo Golf Rs, both of which went straight to a DQ500 because the DQ250 "had trouble with anything over 650 hp" (**VERIFIED** article, [Engine Swap Depot](https://engineswapdepot.com/?p=6448)). The chassis side is easier than it sounds: the NMS trans mount **1K0 199 555 CH** and dogbone **1K0 199 855 BF** are the Jetta's own numbers (**VERIFIED**, [oemwolf 555CH](https://oemwolf.com/oem-parts/1k0199555ch.html)), and BFI sells a 6-cylinder Stage 2 kit with "one complete 6-speed DSG transmission mount" for $665.99 (**VERIFIED**, [BFI Stage 2](https://blackforestindustries.com/products/bfi-mk5-r32-stage-2-mount-kit-fully-assembled-6-cylinder)). The gearbox code letters, drive plate, cooler lines and mechatronic revision for the NMS 3.6 02E were not found and should be read off the donor. For a first build, the DSG path adds a second immobilizer participant, an axle problem and zero precedent to a build that already has no CDVB precedent; it is a reason to pick a different recipient, not a reason to pick a CDVB.

## Cooling, A/C and exhaust are where the Jetta stops being a Golf

The Jetta's front end carries its own numbers, so Ninety4co's "stock Mk6 radiator fans / condenser" lines must be read as "the Jetta's own", and his Mishimoto radiator should not be assumed to drop in. The strongest cooling finding is that CSF catalogues one OEM-style radiator, **5K0 121 251 H** (CSF 3777, $180.45 clearance), for "Jetta 2.5L, Eos 3.2, CC/Passat 3.6 and TT", so a VR6-rated core that fits a Mk6 Jetta lock carrier exists as an OEM part (**VERIFIED**, [UroTuning CSF 3777](https://www.urotuning.com/products/csf-13-14-volkswagen-jetta-2-5l-oem-plastic-radiator-3777)). The GLI's own 2.0T radiator is the Jetta-specific **5C0 121 251 D/E** (CSF 3563), a different number and the small-core size (**VERIFIED**, [UroTuning CSF 3563](https://www.urotuning.com/products/csf-11-18-volkswagen-jetta-2-0l-oem-plastic-radiator-3563)); given Ninety4co's "these engines make a lot of heat", treat it as inadequate and move to the 2.5L/VR6 core (**INFERRED**). Ninety4co's **Mishimoto MMRAD-MK5-08** ($367.32 at Three Piece, whose fitment table includes 2012–2013 Jetta GLI rows) is contradicted by Mishimoto's own page ("direct fit for the 2008 Volkswagen R32") and by Summit ("R32 V6 AWD models only"), and the Jetta lock carrier is the 5C6 part, not the Golf's 1K0 (**VERIFIED** vendor pages, [Three Piece](https://threepiece.us/mishimoto-08-volkswagen-golf-r32-aluminum-radiator-mmrad-mk5-08); [Mishimoto](https://www.mishimoto.com/volkswagen-r32-aluminum-performance-radiator.html)); measure or ask before ordering. The Jetta's fans are the PQ35 1K0/1KM 959 455 family with the J293 control module inside the large fan, with a 2012 build-date split (**VERIFIED**, [IDParts](https://www.idparts.com/cooling-fan-large-oem-cbeacjaa-earlyckra-early-1k0959455fr-1k0959455n-p-12351.html)), so they should drive from either VR6 ECU as the Golf fans did for the BLV; verify the motor wattage class against the V6 application (**INFERRED**).

Ninety4co's OEM-style hose set (upper+lower radiator hose with temp sensor **1K0 121 049 CM**, radiator-to-after-run-pump **1K0 122 086 B**, heater upper **1K0 122 157 HE**, after-run pump outlet **3C0 121 063 E**, after-run pump **1K0 965 561 B**, and his advice that "the hoses are the same as a 2018 Passat GT") remains **REPORTED** only: none of those five numbers is indexed on any reachable page, and every dealer catalog blocked the researchers ([parts list](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lUm4Pee3PA9zaG5dA_x5aLbu-WO3YJs_4todYXtXFYo/edit)). The B6 return side uses a tee-with-check-valve assembly **3C0 121 086 M** that the Gray Fabrication swirl pot deletes (**REPORTED** dealer snippet, [Hendrick](https://vwparts.hendrickcars.com/parts/volkswagen-engine-coolant-hose-3c0121086m)); the Gray bottle is $280, built to order in 2–3 weeks, and comes in two versions keyed to whether the timing cover has a vacuum pump, so photograph the donor's cover first (**VERIFIED**, [Gray Fabrication](https://gray-fabrication.com/products/3-6l-vr6-swirl-pot)). His trick of trimming a factory Mk6 heater hose to fit the coolant flange should work with the Jetta's own 2.0T heater hoses, which will be a different length (**INFERRED**).

Air conditioning is the most likely custom-line item. The 3.6 accessory bracket puts the compressor lower than a TSI's, which is why Ninety4co's TSI suction line would not fit and he used the Eos 3.2/R32 PQ35 VR6 line **1K0 820 743 CE** with compressor **1K0 820 808 F** (**REPORTED**, [Part 3, 19:00](https://youtu.be/Z0YHrkM1E1Q)). The Mk5 Jetta/GLI shares the PQ35 1K0 820 743 suction-hose family (1K0 820 743 FG, "compressor to evaporator ... Eos, GTI, Jetta, Jetta GLI"), so the Eos line plausibly reaches the Jetta's evaporator block but is unverified in a Mk6 Jetta (**VERIFIED** family, [VW Nanuet](https://parts.vwnanuet.com/p/Volkswagen_2006_GTI/AC-Refrigerant-Suction-Hose/48272384/1K0820743FG.html)). The discharge line is worse: the Mk6 Jetta's **5C0 820 721 S** fits "1.8 & 2.0 2011–16 w/ gas eng.; does not fit 2.5, diesel or hybrid", a TSI-compressor-position part (**REPORTED**, [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/p/251506314)), so no OEM Jetta discharge line exists for a VR6 compressor position and the choices are an Eos 3.2/R32 1K0 820 721 line (if the Jetta condenser inlet matches) or a custom-crimped hose (**INFERRED**). Keep the Jetta condenser; the NMS 3.6 compressor number was not found (the B7 family shows 5N0 820 803 E), so use the donor's compressor or the PQ35 1K0 820 808 F, whichever matches the donor bracket (**INFERRED**). Have the system discharged at a shop before pulling the GLI engine, as Ninety4co did.

Fuel exposes the first real BLV-versus-CDVB difference. Ninety4co's "fuel pump module 3C0 906 093 A" is actually the **J538 fuel pump control module**, which fits Passat 2006–2010 and 2012–2017, CC and TT (**VERIFIED**, [FridayParts](https://www.fridayparts.com/fuel-pump-control-module-3c0906093a-v15710061-for-audi-tt-quattro-l4-2-0l-2008-2014-volkswagen-passat-cc-2009-2017); FCP $71–317, [FCP Euro](https://www.fcpeuro.com/Volkswagen-parts/CC/Fuel-Pump-Control-Module/)), paired with the Mk5-FSI 4.0-bar returnless filter/regulator **6Q0 201 051 J** (**REPORTED**). That architecture works because the BLV expects it. The NMS in-tank module **3C8 919 051 A** "includes pressure regulator" and spans Passat/CC 3.6 2006–2018 (**VERIFIED**, [Herko](https://store.herko.com/genuine-volkswagen-fuel-pump-module-3c8919051a-for-volkswagen-cc-passat-06-18.html)), so the CDVB expects the regulator in the tank, its low-side target was not found, and Ross-Tech warns that a "replacement fuel filter with the wrong bar rating (e.g. 4.0 bar installed where 6.0 bar is needed)" sets P310B on FSI engines (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech P310B](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/P310B/012555)). The GLI's own tank, pump and J538 stay in the Jetta in either case (the PQ46 Passat module is not a PQ35 tank drop-in, **INFERRED**); for the BLV the filter is Ninety4co's 4.0 bar part, for a CDVB the correct pressure must be read from the 2012–2018 Passat 3.6 repair manual first. The intake tells the same story: the BLV's MAF is Hitachi **06J 906 461 B** (**VERIFIED**, [ECS](https://www.ecstuning.com/b-hitachi-parts/mass-air-flow-sensor-maf/06j906461b~hit/)), the 2012–2018 Passat 3.6's is Bosch **03H 906 461 A** / 0280218260 (**REPORTED**, [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/175355678711)), so whichever engine is bought, its airbox, MAF tube and MAF come with it, and the big 3.6 airbox is reported to push the battery out of the bay on swaps (**REPORTED**); Ninety4co dodged that with a pre-existing trunk battery, and the Jetta build must either budget a relocation or test a compact Eos 3.2/R32 airbox with the donor MAF (**INFERRED**). Ninety4co chose the two-piece intake manifold so the upper half comes off in the car for valve-cover access; with the one-piece manifold a valve cover gasket "would need the front clip off" (**REPORTED**, [Part 2](https://youtu.be/wTqhQoPT48I)).

Exhaust is bolt-on at the manifold and custom at the midpipe. The 3.6 FSI manifold **03H 253 031 C** appears across R36, CC, Eos 3.6, Phaeton and Touareg (**REPORTED**, [eBay.de](https://www.ebay.de/itm/146581474255)); "BLV has the same angled manifold outlets as a Mk5 R32", which is why the **USP-32L-CAT1** high-flow cat downpipes (twin 2" into single 2.5", $799.99, race-only, listed for 2008 R32 and 2006–08 A3 3.2) bolted on with fitment "not the greatest" (**VERIFIED** vendor, [NGP](https://store.ngpracing.com/products/usp-high-flow-catalytic-converters-for-mk5-r32-and-a3-3-2l); **REPORTED** fit, [Part 3, 21:00](https://youtu.be/Z0YHrkM1E1Q)). For the NMS engine the factory layout is unresolved: MagnaFlow lists one direct-fit converter, 52938, "interchanges with OE part 561 254 500 D" (or B) for 2012–2018 Passat 3.6 at $2,182, versus two per-bank converters (3C0 254 504 BX / 3C0 254 502 TX) for the 2006–2008 BLV (**VERIFIED** [BMP](https://www.bmptuning.com/products/52938); **REPORTED** B6 numbers); the CARB version is CAD $5,310–5,543 special order in Canada (**REPORTED**, [Foreign Automotive](https://foreignautomotive.ca/products/magnaflow-12-13-volkswagen-passat-3-6l-direct-fit-converter)). Whether the NMS manifolds are the same 03H casting, and therefore accept R32 downpipes, is unverified. On a Jetta the single 2.5" R32-style outlet must be joined to the GLI cat-back's front flange by a custom midpipe section over a longer wheelbase (**INFERRED**). Oxygen sensors: BLV upstream **022 906 262 AN / AM** per Ninety4co (**REPORTED**); a dealer lists **1K0 998 262 C** as "3.6L, front" (**REPORTED**, [Hendrick](https://vwparts.hendrickcars.com/parts/volkswagen-oxygen-sensor-1k0998262c)). Keep all four donor sensors and both cats; Ontario no longer tests emissions, but the hardware argument is made in the compliance section below.

## Wiring is still the 200–300 hour job, and the Jetta's pin map is its own

Mechanically the swap is bolt-on; electrically it is a re-pin of the recipient's body harness plus a fuse-box transplant, and that is what Ninety4co spent **200–300 hours over about three weeks** on ([Part 4, 9:02](https://youtu.be/6Gg6oTTig4Y)). His architecture lesson transfers intact: the BLV ECU has a **T60** to the engine harness ("we didn't have to do anything to that") and a **T94** to the body harness through the firewall, and all the work is on the T94 plus the fuse box's **T40/T26** and the **T14** engine-to-body bridge ([wiring sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1RIhUj4_f6G1ZhraDKugaOQ0ZXPTq9BtquM9f8qgIkq4/edit)). The circuit groups that moved are known from his sheet: terminal 30/15, connection 87 and two ECM relays onto different fuse-box pins; a new after-run coolant pump circuit and relay; fan module and coolant-temp pins; EVAP leak-detection pump pins; fuel pump module control, low-pressure sensor and a fuel-pressure-regulator circuit; every TSI O2 wire out and four 3.6 sensors pinned fresh; TSI MAF out and 3.6 MAF in on four new pins; all six throttle-pedal pins; brake switch and (still unverified on his sheet) cruise. The GTI box lacked the **T26** connector the 3.6 harness needs, and running on the smaller box made a relay chatter and stick even key-out, so the B6/VR6 "high" fuse box **1K0 937 124 K** became mandatory, cut from a junkyard car with its cabling (**REPORTED**, [Part 4, 6:01](https://youtu.be/6Gg6oTTig4Y)). The manual-specific trap: the clutch switch landed on **T40 pin 14**, unassigned in the Passat box because the NA 3.6 was never a manual, so the ECU saw no clutch and gave no starter signal until a 15 A fuse (his fuse 39) was added ([Part 4, 12:03](https://youtu.be/6Gg6oTTig4Y)).

None of his pin numbers can be copied to a Jetta. The Mk6 Jetta cluster shares the Golf cluster's plug but not its pinout ("it won't function at all"), gateway location and even whether a separate gateway exists vary by trim, and Mk6 Jetta fuse assignments shift between 2011–2013 and 2014–2017 (**REPORTED**, VWVortex and fuse-diagram sites cited in the notes). The Jetta's engine-bay box is the PQ35 1K0 937 12x carrier under a 5C0 937 132 cover shared with the Beetle and NMS Passat, and listings mark the GLI's box as different from other Jettas' ("except GLI"), but nobody could open a catalog page to say whether any Jetta box carries the T26/T40 population a V6 needs (**REPORTED**). The safe plan is Ninety4co's: take the donor's E-box with its harness tail and engine-harness connectors attached, and buy **factory wiring diagrams for both VINs**. He used ElsaWeb at about $50 for 24 hours and found Mitchell/AllData/ProDemand "had wrong pin numbers, wrong colours and missing info"; North American erWin now lives at vw-us.erwin-store.com with timed "Info medium" access (wiring diagrams, fitting locations, repair manuals, SSPs), prices behind the login, most recently reported at **$35 for one day** with downloaded PDFs kept afterwards (**VERIFIED** site, [erWin](https://vw-us.erwin-store.com/erwin/showHome.do); **REPORTED** price, [IDParts blog](https://idpartsblog.com/2023/04/21/find-vw-repair-manual-online/)). Two one-day purchases, one per VIN, downloading the full diagram set and the "fitting locations" documents, is the single best-value line in the budget.

The electrical note argued that the CDVB is the easier ECU to integrate because the 2011+ Jetta, 2012 Beetle and 2012 NMS Passat were engineered on one Golf-Mk6-generation module set: MK60EC1 ABS with the identical label file, a BCM with the comfort system merged in, the same airbag module family, Steering Assist at address 44 and a Golf-5K-family cluster, whereas the B6 BLV sits a generation earlier (3C0 gateway, separate comfort module, MED9) (**VERIFIED** module lists, [Ross-Tech Jetta 16/AJ](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/VW_Jetta_(16/AJ)); [Ross-Tech NMS](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/VW_Passat_(NMS/A3))). That is a real advantage and it deserves to be stated plainly: the power-distribution and sensor-pin mismatches Ninety4co fought are likely smaller with a CDVB (**INFERRED**). But "same generation does not mean same pinout": no MED17.1.6 body-side pin map exists publicly, the Jetta's body harness and E-box population are engine-specific, and the CDVB brings three problems the BLV does not, namely missing-TCU messages on powertrain CAN that the ECU, ABS and other modules will "start complaining" about (**VERIFIED**, Ross-Tech's Uwe, [forums 40349](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/40349/)), a manual-transmission software variant that two Ross-Tech members doubt exists anywhere, and the LIN alternator. Those three are software problems, and software is where the CDVB has no vendor.

## Tuning support decides the engine: the BLV has a swap file, the CDVB has a shrug

The immobilizer is a solved, commodity problem for both engines; the swap calibration is not, and that asymmetry is the whole decision. On the immobilizer: in a Jetta there is no J362 that knows the Passat ECU, so a stock donor ECU will crank and stall within a second or two, exactly the symptom in the one CDVC swap thread found ("fires up, stays running for a few seconds, then cuts out", **REPORTED**, [MHH Auto](https://mhhauto.com/Thread-2018-VW-Atlas-SE-3-6-immo-off-Help)) and in a Mk6 Jetta cluster/BCM swap thread. Bench immo-off services for MED9 and MED17 cost **$135** (06A Technik, lists "3.6 VR6" but not the 03H 906 families, and showed sold out until mid-2025), **$250 + $100 for MED17.1** (Speedo Solutions, "Off Road / Race / Diagnostic Applications ONLY") and **$175** for a MED17.1.62 identity clone (VinnieBuilt), with eBay mail-in MED17 immo-off at $99–195 (**VERIFIED** vendor pages, [06A Technik](https://06atechnik.com/products/med9-med17-immobilizer-defeat); [Speedo](https://www.speedosolutions.com/VAG-MED17-EDC17-Immobilizer-Defeat-Service_p_156.html); [VinnieBuilt](https://www.vinniebuilt.net/products/audi-rs-med17-1-62-clone-and-programming-service-miami)). The alternative, adapting the donor ECU to the Jetta's own Immo IV cluster with both PINs, is theoretically supported for "VWZ"-serial components but not for "VWX"/challenge types, dealers no longer see PINs (GeKo), and nobody has reported trying it on a 3.6 (**VERIFIED** procedure, [Ross-Tech Immobilizer](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/Immobilizer); [Immo IV ECU swapping](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/Immobilizer_IV_ECU_Swapping)). Ninety4co never shows how his immobilizer was handled; Reflect Tuning's tune at the end "eliminated codes" and left "zero lights on the dash" ([Part 5](https://youtu.be/-DIJFHw3njE)), which is consistent with an immo-off folded into the swap file (**INFERRED**).

On the swap calibration the two engines diverge completely. For the **BLV/MED9**, United Motorsport publicly sells "Swap Tuning" whose scope is "changed or deleted instrument cluster, missing VSS (rev limiter sorted), change of exhaust config (VR6 applications), change/missing/new automatic transmission, deleted or missing ABS", with an application table that includes **"3.6L FSI VR6 — Passat '06 to '08"** (**VERIFIED**, [United Motorsport](http://unitedmotorsport.net/products/united-motorsport-swap-tuning/)); UM's files are sold in Canada and the US through NGP, which handles "swap cars … through custom tuning requests" (**REPORTED**, [NGP](https://www.ngpracing.com/4003/)); Reflect Tuning (Ian, North Carolina, takes mailed-in ECUs) finished Ninety4co's car; a Mk5 GTI BLV + 02M build sold with an ECU "calibrated by United Motorsports" (**REPORTED**); and APR and Unitronic both have MED9 3.6 performance files, Unitronic's listed at **CAD $550** through a Canadian dealer with engine codes BHK/BLV/BWS/BHL (**REPORTED**, [Foreign Automotive](https://foreignautomotive.ca/products/unitronic-volkswagen-passat-3-6-fsi-stage-1-91oct)). Every documented naturally aspirated PQ35 3.6 swap in North America is a BLV on MED9 with an 02M (**INFERRED** from the precedents survey).

For the **CDVB/MED17.1.6**, the only sourced statement on manual conversion is from a Ross-Tech thread about putting a 6-speed in a 2018 Passat GT, where administrator Uwe writes that transmission type lives in ECU coding Byte 1, offers a theoretical manual coding, and adds "I am quite skeptical that your ECU will accept this. My guess is you will have to talk to a tuner"; verified members add that the ECU software must be a manual-transmission version and that the NMS 3.6 "basically has no manual options at all", and one R32 owner "had ABS issues until he fitted a manual engine ECU" (**VERIFIED** quotes, [Ross-Tech forums 40349](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/40349/)). Commercial MED17 3.6 files are performance-only: APR's "ECU 3.6L EA390 MED17" covers Passat 12–18, CC, Touareg and Cayenne at **$449.95–459.95** ($366.95 plus $150 install at one dealer) and mentions no swap, immo or manual option (**VERIFIED**, [APR](https://www.goapr.com/products/software/ecu_upgrade/gasoline/6/vr6/parts/ECU-36L-EA390-MED17); [Kies](https://www.kiesmotorsports.com/products/apr-ecu-upgrade-3-6l-ea390-vr6-med17)); Malone's $549 "FSI VR6 280" has unconfirmed fitment; UM's swap table stops at the MED9 years; Eurodyne is 4-cylinder only; Reflect's site was unreachable and shows no 3.6 product; Integrated Engineering shows none. A CDVB manual build therefore needs a custom WinOLS job covering immo-off, the Byte-1 or software-variant change, suppression of the J217/J743 CAN timeouts and the ABS faults that follow, a clutch-switch strategy the software never had, and the O2/EVAP/aux-pump settings (**INFERRED**). The APR file proves the 03H 906 023 ECU is a flashable OBD target (**INFERRED**), so a tuner *could* do it; the point is that none has, none advertises it, and a first-time builder would be the test case.

Three smaller factors cut the same way. First, the "hybrid" of a CDVB long block under BLV electronics is not a shortcut: the newer 3.6 block is reported to have a different oil-filter flange and fuel rails, with the fuel-pressure sensor moved to the valve cover (**REPORTED**, [VWVortex 9399581](https://www.vwvortex.com/threads/swapping-a-newer-3-6-vr6-long-block-into-an-older-3-6-passat-or-touareg.9399581/)), and the MAF differs, so the BLV intake, rail and Schimmel spin-on housing (sold for "24V VR6 engines, 2.8L and 3.2L", 3.6 not mentioned, $286, **VERIFIED**, [vr6parts.com](https://vr6parts.com/sp-24v-vr6-oil-filter-housing.html)) are unproven on a CDVB. Second, the CDVB's electrical advantages are speculative while its software disadvantage is documented. Third, the DSG mechatronic is an immobilizer participant, so the one configuration where the CDVB is coherent (keeping its own 02E) doubles the immobilizer work.

The BLV's own weaknesses are real and must be priced in. It is a 2006–2010 engine; the oil-pump sprocket bolt backing out is a documented owner complaint ("damaged the timing chain cover, timing chain and a number of other parts", **REPORTED**, [CarComplaints](https://www.carcomplaints.com/Volkswagen/Passat/2006/engine/engine-3.shtml)), fixed by the one-piece sprocket and new 12.9 bolt Ninety4co fitted; and Ninety4co's 171,000-mile BLV, rebuilt top-end and timing only, **spun a rod bearing four months after its first drive** ([I FOUND WHAT'S WRONG](https://youtu.be/JBCkB3V5hrw)). The pre-install list is therefore the same for either engine, in priority order: the transmission-side timing set while the engine is out (chains 066109503C $79.99 or Febi 25404 $76.99, upper chain 03H 109 503 B, upper tensioner 066109507D $33.31, oil-pump chain 021109465B $62.99, both tensioner and guide rails; ECS's basic kit ES2784782 is $245.94; FCP's Atlas 3.6 kits run $397–1,056 but state no engine code, so confirm each line against the VIN) (**VERIFIED** listings, [FCP Euro Atlas timing](https://www.fcpeuro.com/Volkswagen-parts/Atlas/Timing/); [ES2784782](https://eurosporttuning.com/basic-timing-chain-kit-es2784782/)); rod and main bearings on any 150,000-km-plus core; the one-piece oil-pump sprocket and bolt on a BLV; water pump (belt-driven, passenger side), thermostat, belt, tensioner and idlers; valve cover and PCV (03H 103 429 family); head gasket, head bolts and cam-adjuster bolts if the head comes off; and a new OEM engine harness if connectors are brittle, which Ninety4co found "really cheap at the dealer" ([Part 2](https://youtu.be/wTqhQoPT48I)). The timing is on the transmission side and "impossible with engine and trans in the car", so it is now or never.

### Ontario donors cost CAD $1,700–6,000, and the province no longer tests emissions

Donor supply shapes the budget. One Copart Ontario sale of a 2013 Passat SEL 3.6 (front damage, run-and-drive, 112,996 mi) closed at **CAD $1,700** in February 2023 (**REPORTED**, [stat.vin](https://stat.vin/cars/1VWCM7A39DC090446)); current Ontario Kijiji asks are a 2012 Passat 3.6 SEL at 269,504 km for **$2,500 as-is** and a 2011 CC 3.6 for $6,495, with other recent asks at $6,000–8,999 (**VERIFIED** listing page, [Kijiji](https://www.kijiji.ca/b-cars-trucks/ontario/passat-3.6/k0c174l9004)). Copart's Ontario yards do not sell to the public, so a broker is needed (**REPORTED**). B6 BLV donors are thinner on the ground in the searches: a Toronto-area dismantler has listed a 2008 Passat 3.6 BLV with its 09M automatic (**REPORTED**, [dasparts.ca](https://dasparts.ca/arrivals/cardetails/477)) and a Hamilton 2009 3.6 4Motion wagon was offered as a $2,200 parts car (**REPORTED**). That scarcity is the best argument for the CDVB and it should be weighed honestly: if a complete NMS 3.6 lands for CAD $1,700 and no BLV appears, the tuning question has to be answered before buying, not after. The B6 donor also brings a conventional automatic you discard, whereas the NMS donor brings the 02E that the secondary path would use.

Compliance is simpler than most swap builders assume. Ontario removed the Drive Clean light-duty emissions test on **1 April 2019**, while "emission control systems are still required to be present and operating" (**VERIFIED**, [ERO 013-3630](https://ero.ontario.ca/node/478)); the provincial regulation that briefly contained an explicit "motor replacement" rule, O. Reg. 457/19 s. 8, was revoked by O. Reg. 183/22 with the regulation gone on 1 July 2022 (**VERIFIED** e-Laws source documents, [O. Reg. 183/22](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/r22183)); and a Safety Standards Certificate is triggered only by transfer, out-of-province registration or an unfit-to-fit status change, valid 36 days, from a DriveON centre (**VERIFIED**, [Ontario.ca SSC](https://www.ontario.ca/page/safety-standards-certificate)). Federal anti-tampering and the HTA's O. Reg. 169/22 were not checked, which is reason enough to keep both cats, all four O2 sensors and the EVAP/LDP hardware and to run an MIL-free tune. The paperwork step that matters most is the insurer: in *Aviva v. Cahill* (2026 ONSC 4975) an owner who added a supercharger, headers, clutch and cooling kit without telling Aviva lost the duty to defend and had exposure capped at **$200,000**, the judge noting "any reasonable insurer would consider that as a significant factor" (**VERIFIED** legal news, [Law Times](https://www.lawtimesnews.com/practice-areas/personal-injury/man-who-modified-corvette-breached-insurance-contract-superior-court/393526)). A 3.6 in a Jetta is at least as material; disclose it in writing before the first drive.

## Prioritized shopping and verification list

The list is ordered by what has to be true before the next dollar is spent. Labels show how firm each line is; a VIN-based dealer-counter check (one 2008 Passat 3.6 VIN, one 2016 Jetta GLI VIN, and a 2008 Eos 3.2 or R32 VIN for the A/C line) closes most REPORTED gaps in one visit.

**Stage 0: decisions and paper, before any purchase (under US$150)**
1. Lock the engine choice to the BLV for a manual build (reasoning above). If a CDVB donor appears first, get a written, fixed-price commitment from a tuner (United Motorsport via NGP custom request, or an independent WinOLS tuner) for a MED17.1.6 manual/swap/immo-off file *before* buying; without it, pass. **INFERRED** from the tuning findings.
2. Call United Motorsport/NGP (1-877-German1) and Reflect Tuning for the BLV swap file: price, whether immo-off is included, whether the car or a bench harness is needed, and what VSS/ABS/cluster deletes they want to know about. UM asks for the ECU box code via a VCDS screenshot. **VERIFIED** scope, price unknown ([United Motorsport](http://unitedmotorsport.net/products/united-motorsport-swap-tuning/)).
3. Buy erWin "Info medium" access for one day per VIN (reported $35/day) and download the complete wiring diagrams, fitting locations and component locations for the donor and the GLI. **VERIFIED** source, **REPORTED** price.
4. Tell the insurer in writing. **VERIFIED** case law.
5. Message @colin.cusack (the only 3.6 Mk6 Jetta known) and @chobester (Ninety4co) about immobilizer handling and the T14 jumper he could not recall. **REPORTED** leads.

**Stage 1: the donor and what to cut out of it (CAD $1,500–4,000 for a complete car)**
6. Complete donor with engine, ECU, engine harness, **E-box with its harness tail and T-connectors**, body-harness ECU connector cut at the firewall, throttle pedal, MAF and airbox, all four O2 sensors, both cats, EVAP hardware, fuel pump control module J538, after-run pump and relay, alternator, A/C compressor and lines, and the factory service manual for the BLV in the B6 Passat if it can be found. For a BLV the box is **1K0 937 124 K**. **REPORTED** (Ninety4co's cut list).

## Conclusion

The research changes the project in two ways. It renames the donor, which is not pedantry: "CDVC" searches return Atlas ECUs (03H 906 026, MED17.1.62), Atlas immo services and Atlas tech tips, none of which apply to a 2012–2018 Passat, whose CDVB runs a different Bosch family (03H 906 023, MED17.1.6) that no tuner has ever configured for a manual gearbox. And it moves the hard part of the build from the engine bay to the laptop. Every mechanical question that worried this project resolves in the Jetta's favour: the mounts, dogbone, cables, slave cylinder and springs are the same 1K0 parts as the Golf that Ninety4co already proved, the subframe is literally the NMS Passat 3.6's, and a VR6-rated OEM radiator for the Jetta carrier exists. What does not resolve is software. For a first-time builder in a home garage on a budget, the engine with a purchasable swap file, a tuner who has done this exact car, a pin-by-pin wiring sheet and every documented PQ35 3.6 precedent behind it is the BLV, and the recommendation is to build Ninety4co's car in a Jetta: **BLV + MED9 + B6 "high" fuse box + Mk4 24V FWD 02M + South Bend Stage 2 + AR/AP mounts + Jetta 2.5L/VR6 radiator + Gray bottle + Eos suction line + USP downpipes + UM or Reflect swap tune**, with rod and main bearings added to his rebuild list because his engine spun one. The CDVB is the engine to want in five years, when someone has published a manual MED17.1.6 file; today it is a research project wearing a parts list.

Two things would flip that call, and both are cheap to test before any purchase. If United Motorsport or an independent MED17 tuner quotes a fixed price for a CDVB manual/swap/immo-off calibration in writing, the CDVB's newer core, factory oil-pump fix, same-generation electrics and CAD $1,700 Ontario donors make it the better engine and this report's chassis, cooling and exhaust sections still apply unchanged. And if the recipient turns out to be a DSG GLI rather than a 6-speed, the only coherent drivetrain is a CDVB with its own 02E, which trades the 02M's settled $1,100 for an undocumented axle, mechatronic-immobilizer and CAN problem set; that is a different project and should be scoped as one. Either way, the next concrete step is not a part order but two phone calls to tuners and two days of erWin access, which together cost less than the clutch.
