# Leave the A3 stock, build the Jetta

There is no OEM parts set that turns a 2015–2020 Audi A3 8V into a VR6 car, and nobody has published a build. Every finished VR6 swap on VW's MQB platform is a professional Golf with a dual-clutch gearbox. None uses an Atlas engine, none is an A3, and none is both manual and all-wheel drive. If the A3 is built anyway, the most realistic drivetrain is a **2020–2021 Atlas or Atlas Cross Sport 3.6 (CDVC) with its own Bosch MED17.1.62 ECU (03H 906 026 family), its Aisin 8-speed 09P (AWD codes QVK/TYF) and transfer case, driving the A3 quattro's own Haldex 5 rear axle**. It ranks first because it is the only VR6 drivetrain VW engineered on the same electrical system as the A3. Even so, it is an automatic. Its left mount has nothing to bolt to in an A3, its axles and probably its prop shaft must be custom-made, and its ECU needs an immobilizer-off flash plus a swap calibration that no tuner sells. Three pedals plus AWD would mean a twenty-year-old VR6 02M 4Motion and a pedal box from a manual Golf, because no North American A3 8V was ever sold with a manual. HPA's machined DQ381 keeps the A3's own driveline, but it is only available inside a US$49,000-plus shop program, and a PQ35 02E combines the drawbacks of both. Built DIY, the A3 lands at roughly **CA$30,000–56,000 net** (INFERRED). That compares with **CA$25,000–40,000** for the Mk6 Jetta GLI plan (BLV + Mk4 24V 02M) and **CA$15,888–21,990** for a stock Ontario S3, whose 292 hp beats the swapped car's 276. For a 19-year-old first-time builder who wants a VR6 he can finish in a home garage, the answer is the Jetta. If all-wheel drive matters more than the VR6, buy a stock S3 and swap nothing. Do not swap the A3. At 6'6", sit in both bodies before buying either: third-party specs put the A3 sedan about 1.5 in short of the GLI in front headroom.

How to read this report. **VERIFIED** means the researcher read it in a factory document, a parts-catalog fitment page, a vendor product page or a VCDS Auto-Scan. **REPORTED** means a forum post, marketplace listing, press article, encyclopedia entry or search-engine snippet whose page could not be opened. **INFERRED** means reasoning from those sources. Any REPORTED or INFERRED part number needs a VIN-based catalog check or a physical test fit before money moves. Prices are US dollars from US vendors unless marked CA$. CAD conversions use about 1.37 plus 15–20% for HST and shipping, the same assumptions as the Jetta estimate. Two research notes conflict with the rest and are corrected here:

- The cooling note calls the 2012–2018 NMS Passat engine "CDVC". VW's own 2016 Passat bulletin lists it as **CDVB** (**VERIFIED**, [NHTSA MC-10168305-0001](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10168305-0001.pdf)), so "CDVC" in this report always means the Atlas.
- The transmission note prefers 2018–2019 Atlas donors because of "11.4:1" compression. That figure traces back to the NMS Passat's spec sheet, so the preference is replaced by the 2020–2021 recommendation below.

## Every MQB VR6 swap so far is a pro-shop Golf with a DSG

**Nobody has published a VR6 in an A3 8V.** Searches for one returned only the 2003–2009 A3 8P, whose 3.2 VR6 was a factory engine, plus threads on older platforms (**REPORTED**, [Audizine](https://audizine.com/threads/vr6-swap-questions.816381/)). Every documented VR6 in an MQB car is a Golf, and every finished one came out of a professional shop with a dual-clutch gearbox:

- **vd Veer Engineering (Netherlands)** put a 3.6 into a diesel Golf 7 GT with a DQ500. Its job list reads "Wiring modification from EDC17 to MED17.1.6", "MQB DSG Shifter canbus conversion to PQ DQ500" and "PQ ECU MED17 Canbus signal conversion to MQB". In other words, an engine ECU from the older PQ platform (the Passat generation) was made to talk to an MQB body through converter boxes (**VERIFIED**, [vd Veer](https://www.vdveer-engineering.nl/en/projects/2-golf-7-r36)).
- **HGP (Germany)** built a bi-turbo 3.6 Golf 7 R around an Audi RS3 gearbox. It reported that "none of the mounts match, because the MQB platform was not designed for this engine except on the Atlas" (**REPORTED**, [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/golf-7-r-gets-36l-bi-turbo-v6-and-rs3-gearbox-from-hgp-113029.html)).
- **HPA's VR550T program** fits the Chinese-market 2.5-litre turbo VR6 (DDKA) to 2018–2019 Golf Rs, using a "CNC adaptation of DQ381 Gearbox" and "Full UDS canbus integration". Golf R packages "begin at $49,000 USD" including labour, and the Alltrack version starts at **US$64,300** (**VERIFIED**, [HPA Golf R](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/pages/hpa-vr550t-2-5l-vr6-program-for-golf-r); [HPA Alltrack](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/pages/hpa-vr550t-2-5l-vr6-program-for-sportwagen-alltrack)). In 2023 The Drive quoted US$40,000, excluding the donor car (**REPORTED**, [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/vw-tuner-is-building-turbo-vr6-swapped-mk7-5-golf-rs-with-550-hp)). HPA's page now lists the car as "Available in DSG & 6-Speed Manual", while The Drive said donors had to be DSG cars. Neither source explains the manual hardware.
- **The only manual MQB VR6 on record** is a front-drive South African Mk7 with a 3.2 and a six-speed. Its builder plans to add a DSG and AWD later, and its electronics are undocumented (**REPORTED**, [Engine Swap Depot](https://engineswapdepot.com/?p=132118)).

None of these used an Atlas engine, none is an A3, and none combines a manual with all-wheel drive. The nearest thing to a DIY lead is a Mk7 Golf R owner on VWVortex. He wrote that "the Atlas VR mount bolts straight into the same location as my 2.0t". Another member replied: "It's the coding that is extremely difficult … It may literally be impossible to get it to work cleanly with all of the factory amenities". No finished build followed (**REPORTED**, search snippets, [VWVortex 9532121](https://www.vwvortex.com/threads/mk7-r-vr6-swap.9532121/)).

Five-cylinder swaps show what a real parts bin looks like, and why the VR6 lacks one. US shop Innovative Motorsports installs the RS3's DAZA engine "into any and all MQB-chassis cars". The package is the engine, the 7-speed DSG, the RS3 Haldex and the RS3 rear diff. Because the architecture is shared, no custom metalwork is needed. The hard part is electrical, because the bay is "essentially rewired" (**REPORTED**, [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/audi-five-cylinder-swapped-vw-golf-alltrack-is-just-better)). The A3 8V is the RS3's own body, so for this car a five-cylinder is close to a factory parts job. An Atlas VR6 has no parts set for MQB compact cars at all (**INFERRED**). That answers the builder's "as new as possible but still cheap and easy to VR6 swap": the cutoff is the platform, not the model year. HPA itself notes that "With the advent of the MQB platform, only a few North American model carried the 3.6L VR6" (**VERIFIED**, [HPA](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/pages/hpa-vr550t-2-5l-vr6-program)). On PQ35, by contrast, the VR6 engine mount **1K0 199 262 AR** is a catalog part on the Mk5 R32, the Eos 3.2 and every B6, B7 and NMS Passat 3.2/3.6 (**VERIFIED**, [oemwolf](https://oemwolf.com/oem-parts/1k0199262ar.html)), and Ninety4co's junkyard one bolted into his Mk6 GTI unmodified (**REPORTED**, his build series). The newest sedan on that platform is the 2015–2018 Mk6 Jetta GLI (**INFERRED**). Time also matters: VW built its last VR6 on 12 December 2024, so donor supply only shrinks from here (**REPORTED**, [The Drive](https://thedrive.com/news/the-vw-vr6-engine-is-officially-dead-in-america)).

A manual A3 is not available to start from either:

- Audi Canada launched the 2015 A3 sedan with a "6-speed S tronic transmission and quattro" (**REPORTED**, [Auto123](https://www.auto123.com/en/news/2015-audi-a3-sedan-pricing-unveiled/48284)).
- By 2017 the front-drive 2.0T had a seven-speed S tronic, the 220 hp / 258 lb-ft quattro kept the six-speed, and "no manual transmission is offered on any model A3" (**REPORTED**, [AutoTrader.ca](https://www.autotrader.ca/editorial/expert-reviews/audi/a3/first-drive-2017-audi-a3-s3/)).
- Audi Canada stopped selling manual cars altogether from model year 2019 (**REPORTED**, [Collision Repair](https://www.collisionrepairmag.com/audi-canada-no-longer-making-manual-transmission-vehicles)).
- Ross-Tech's A3/S3 8V page links the transmission control unit to the six-speed DSG **0D9** (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech A3/S3 8V](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/Audi_A3/S3_(8V/FF))). One parts seller says the facelift moved to the seven-speed DQ381 (**0GC**) (**REPORTED**, [vagparts.com.au](https://www.vagparts.com.au/collections/audi-8v-a3-s3-rs3-dsg-service-parts)), so read the code off the car.
- Every RS3 found for sale in Canada is a seven-speed automatic (**VERIFIED** listings, [AutoTrader.ca](https://www.autotrader.ca/cars/audi/rs%203/on/?rcp=15&srt=9&yRng=2017%2C2020&prx=-1&prv=Ontario&loc=Ottawa%2C%20ON)).

The best recipient on paper is a **2017–2020 A3 2.0T quattro sedan**. It is the cheapest 8V that already has the Haldex rear axle, at CA$12,998–19,998 in Ontario and CA$15,490 for a 107,000 km 2018 in Ottawa (**VERIFIED** listings, [Kijiji Ontario](https://www.kijiji.ca/b-cars-trucks/ontario/audi-a3-quattro/k0c174l9004); [Ottawa listing](https://www.kijiji.ca/v-cars-trucks/ottawa/2018-audi-a3-sedan-clean-car-fax-quattro-super-clean-car-low/1742551561)). Pulling its engine also destroys less value than gutting an S3 or RS3 (**INFERRED**).

## The Atlas CDVC is the only MQB-born VR6, and its ECU is the hard part

Three different 3.6s circulate under similar names, and only one belongs in an MQB car.

- **Atlas (CDVC).** Every VW of America document found, from the 2018 launch through the 2021 VIN range, calls the US Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport 3.6 **CDVC**. The 2022 EVAP bulletin lists its ECU software numbers by year: **03H 906 026 E/F/J/S** (2018), **R/AA/AH** (2019), **AG/AF** (2020) and **AJ** (2021) (**VERIFIED**, [VW TSB 01-21-09](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10210224-0001.pdf)). A firmware catalogue ties 03H 906 026 E to **Bosch MED17.1.62** (**VERIFIED**, [c4ip](https://c4ip.ru/en/vw/145555-vw-atlas-ca-3-6l-vr6-fsi-cdvc-206kw-280ps-med17-1-62-10sw059936-03h906026e-9970-stock-eeprom-bin)). Two Ross-Tech Auto-Scans of 2018 Atlases show the hardware as **03H 907 309 K** (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech forum 10675](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/10675/); [12372](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/12372/)).
- **NMS Passat (CDVB).** The 2012–2018 Passat runs MED17.1.6 in the 03H 906 023 family (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech forum 40349](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/40349/)).
- **B6 Passat (BLV).** It runs MED9 in the 03H 906 032 family (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech Passat 3C](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/VW_Passat_(3C))).

Tuning-file sites suggest an Atlas split between "2018–19 MED17.1.6 at 11.4:1" and "2020+ MED17.1.62 at 12.0:1". It falls apart when checked:

- 206 kW is both 280 PS and 276 hp, so the two power figures are one engine in two units.
- Dealer data list 12.0:1 for the 2018 Atlas ([dealer listing](https://www.autoparkhonda.com/used/Volkswagen/2018-Volkswagen-Atlas-e7504aeeac18381b30e2fd5aef1234c5.htm)).
- 11.4:1 is the NMS Passat figure from VW's own Self-Study Program ([SSP 800153](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10121132-9999.pdf)).

So every 2018–2021 US Atlas is the same engine and ECU family making **276 hp / 266 lb-ft** (**INFERRED**; output **VERIFIED**, [Unitronic](https://www.getunitronic.com/ecu-tuning/atlas-cross-sport-3.6-2020)). The code "CDVD" appears only in a third-party manual index and in reseller listings (**REPORTED**, [vwts.ru](https://vwts.ru/vw_teramont_0a.html)), so read the code on the donor's label. FWD and 4Motion Atlases share the same long block, because the transfer case bolts to the gearbox, not the engine (**INFERRED**).

**Buy a 2020–2021 Atlas or Cross Sport with ECU 03H 906 026 AG, AF or AJ.** VIN position 5 is "R" for the Atlas and "E" for the Cross Sport (**REPORTED**, [transend](https://transend.us/products/engine/cylinder-block-components/engine-complete-assembly/engines100179265)). Before a tuner reads the ECU, confirm the EVAP update to software 9973 or 1689 has been done (**INFERRED**). That year range skips four documented early problems:

- the 2018 launch software, which needed a misfire/P068A update (**VERIFIED**, [TB 01 18 14](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10149916-9999.pdf));
- the 2018 cold-start fault caused by a mis-set high-pressure-pump drive sprocket (**VERIFIED**, [Tech Tip 20-19-01TT](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10157341-9999.pdf));
- the 2018–2019 air-lock in the heater support pump V488 (**VERIFIED**, [Tech Tip 01-19-01TT](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10155420-0001.pdf));
- the 2018–2019 sunroof drains routed through the plenum (**VERIFIED**, [Service Action 60E2](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2024/MC-10250760-0001.pdf)).

The later years are not flawless. 2020–21 cars sit on a VW helpline list for low rail pressure (P0087, P0148, P053F) (**VERIFIED**, [NHTSA MC-10205521-0001](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2021/MC-10205521-0001.pdf)). A parts guide also says water from the sunroof and A/C drains corrodes Atlas ECMs (**REPORTED**, [Go-Parts](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-control-module-ecm-volkswagen-atlas-2018-2022)), so open the ECU plugs on a donor of any year. Owner reports add timing-chain stretch from around 60,000 miles (US$2,000–4,500 to fix) and leaking plastic thermostat housings (**REPORTED**, [au7o](https://au7o.io/known-issues/volkswagen-atlas?year=2023)). With the engine out, do the following (**VERIFIED** listings, [FCP Atlas timing](https://www.fcpeuro.com/Volkswagen-parts/Atlas/Timing/); [FCP Atlas cooling](https://www.fcpeuro.com/Volkswagen-parts/Atlas/Cooling-System/)):

- the timing set: iwis chain **066109503C** (US$79.99) or Febi **25404** (US$76.99), oil-pump chain **021109465B** (US$62.99) and upper tensioner **066109507D** (US$33.31); FCP's Atlas kits run US$397.10–1,056.38;
- water pump **03H 121 008 M** (US$212.99) and the thermostat housing;
- valve cover and PCV (03H 103 429 family);
- high-pressure fuel pump seals, marking the pump sprocket position as the tech tip describes.

**Shared MQB electronics are the A3 route's one real advantage over the Jetta.** Ross-Tech points both cars to the same Golf VII gateway and instrument-cluster pages. The Atlas Auto-Scans show the same Continental MK100 brake controller as the A3 8V, with the identical label file **5Q0-907-379-IPB-V1.clb**, plus an MQB body module, an MQB steering rack and a Haldex 5 controller (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech A3/S3 8V](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/Audi_A3/S3_(8V/FF)); [Ross-Tech Atlas](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/VW_Atlas/Teramont_(CA)); [Ross-Tech forum 10675](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/10675/)). The CDVC software was released into the same kind of CAN network the A3 has. On the PQ35 Jetta, the other modules were expected to complain that the engine ECU's torque messages were missing or implausible; on the A3 those faults should largely disappear (**INFERRED**). Shared electronics do not make the engine computer itself easier to fit, though. The A3 2.0T runs a Continental Simos 18 on 06K 907 425 hardware (**REPORTED**, [BMP Tuning](https://www.bmptuning.com/collections/vw/products/034-dynamic-tuning-ecu-software-upgrade-mk7-gti-gli-8v-a3)). That means every body-side ECU circuit has to be re-mapped onto a Bosch plug, and no MED17.1.62 pinout is public. Factory erWin wiring diagrams for both VINs are the only source (**VERIFIED** source list, [Ross-Tech](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/Official_Factory_Repair_Information)).

**The immobilizer is harder than on any PQ35 car.**

- MQB cars use Immobilizer 5, held in the instrument cluster (**VERIFIED**, [Abrites VN025](https://abrites.com/partial/vn025)).
- Ross-Tech states it "does not support Immobilizer 5 procedures at this time" (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech Immobilizer](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/Immobilizer)).
- VW's MQB procedure runs the "Adapt Immobilizer" test plan in ODIS (the dealer diagnostic software) with an online login. The cars covered include the 2018–2021 Atlas (**VERIFIED**, [Tech Tip 57-21-01TT](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10209098-0001.pdf)).
- An S3 owner who fitted an RS3 engine and harness had the ODIS online adaptation refuse the ECU as not correct for the car (**REPORTED**, [MHH Auto](https://mhhauto.com/Thread-Audi-S3-to-RS3-conversion-ECU-issue-IMMO-OFF)). An Atlas ECU on an Audi VIN should expect the same.

A one-time remote ODIS GeKo session costs **US$100–150 per task** (**VERIFIED**, [ECUTool](https://www.ecutool.com/products/odis-geko-login-online-coding-service)) and is worth one try. The realistic route is a bench immo-off:

- VinnieBuilt lists a MED17.1.62 clone at US$175 with an "IMMO Off" add-on; it showed as sold out on 8 October 2026 (**VERIFIED**, [VinnieBuilt](https://www.vinniebuilt.net/products/audi-rs-med17-1-62-clone-and-programming-service-miami)).
- eBay mail-in MED17.1.62 immo-off services start around US$27 (**REPORTED**, [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/str/clesauto72/Music/_i.html)).
- MHH members say owners have done it themselves "for several years" (**REPORTED**, [MHH Auto](https://mhhauto.com/Thread-2018-VW-Atlas-SE-3-6-immo-off-Help)).
- Abrites' MQB licences are the locksmith-grade route: €600 for Immo 5A/5B data or €1,800 for 5C, plus a separate parts-adaptation licence (**VERIFIED**, [VN026](https://abrites.com/partial/vn026); [VN025](https://abrites.com/partial/vn025)).

Neither car has SFD, the diagnostic lock VW applies to the A3 8Y and the 2024+ Atlas (**VERIFIED**, [Audi TSB 2069282/1](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10230921-0001.pdf); [Ross-Tech SFD](https://www.ross-tech.com/vcds/tour/sfd.php)). Some MQB steering-rack software can still be SFD-locked, so check the A3's power-steering module for an "SFD" suffix (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech EPS MQB](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/EPS_MQB)). Component protection only bites when a module moves between cars, which is the argument for keeping every A3 module (**INFERRED**).

**No vendor sells a swap calibration for this ECU.**

- The only shipping Atlas 3.6 tune is TuneZilla's Stage 1 ECU and TCU bundle (US$799, 302 hp / 303 lb-ft). It states that "TCU Tuning is required to run the Stage 1 ECU tune" (**VERIFIED**, [NGP](https://store.ngpracing.com/products/tunezilla-ecu-software-tune-vw-atlas-3-6l-vr6)).
- Unitronic lists the Atlas 3.6 as "Under Development" (**VERIFIED**, [Unitronic](https://www.getunitronic.com/ecu-tuning/atlas-cross-sport-3.6-2020)).
- APR's MED17 3.6 file covers the Passat, CC, Touareg and Cayenne but not the Atlas (**VERIFIED**, [APR](https://www.goapr.com/products/software/ecu_upgrade/gasoline/6/vr6/parts/ECU-36L-EA390-MED17)).
- No tuner advertises swap features for the Atlas ECU.

The gearbox choice decides how big that problem is. With the Atlas's own Aisin gearbox and its control unit, the ECU sees what it expects. A DSG or a manual leaves the ECU looking for an Aisin control unit that is not there. That means custom WinOLS work on the clutch-switch or DSG torque logic (**INFERRED**). The A3's brake, steering and Haldex controllers are coded from factory data for an EA888 car, so any VR6-specific settings must be hand-coded (**INFERRED**).

A standalone ECU does not escape this:

- Syvecs sells an MQB plug-in ECU that keeps "Dash, CAN communications, safety systems" working, but only for the EA888 (**VERIFIED**, [Syvecs](https://www.syvecs.com/?p=8091)).
- Its direct-injection S7D-6 costs about £3,700 ex-VAT (**REPORTED**, [reseller](https://www.garagewhifbitz.co.uk/?p=111553)).
- An HP Academy thread says MoTeC and Link support direct injection but not the factory CAN network (**REPORTED**, [HP Academy](https://hpacademy.com/forum/general-tuning-discussion/show/help-standalone-direct-injection)).

A custom direct-injection standalone that imitates the MQB CAN network would plausibly cost US$8,000–12,000. The stock-ECU route costs a few hundred dollars of immo-off plus a calibration nobody has quoted yet (**INFERRED**).

## Four gearbox routes exist, and only the Atlas automatic arrives as a matched set

**None of the A3's own gearboxes accepts a VR6.**

- The 8V's six-speed 0D9, seven-speed 0GC and the RS3's DQ500 all have four- or five-cylinder bellhousings.
- The MQB 0FB manual is a refined four-cylinder 02Q whose maker "refined the bellhousing spacing and design" (**VERIFIED**, [FCP Euro MQ350 guide](https://www.fcpeuro.com/blog/definitive-guide-vw-audi-6-speed-manual-transmissions-mq350-02m-02q-0fb)).
- The VR6 crank takes a 10-bolt flywheel, against 8 bolts on the TSI (same source).
- No VR6-to-EA888 adapter plate turned up in any search. One 2020 forum claim that the bolt patterns match contradicts the crank-bolt facts (**REPORTED**, [Classic Motorsports](https://classicmotorsports.com/forum/grm/vr6-rwd/53192/page1/)).

Five transverse gearbox families do carry a VR6 bellhousing from the factory:

- the Atlas Aisin **09P (AQ450)**, whose 3.6 AWD versions are coded **QVK/TYF** and FWD versions **QVJ/TYT** (**VERIFIED**, [factory manual index](https://vwts.ru/vw_teramont_0a.html));
- the six-cylinder **02E DQ250** DSG from the Mk5 R32, A3 8P 3.2, TT 3.2 and Passats, which has "a star-shaped flange and a tripod joint" (**VERIFIED**, [Epytec ISP106](https://epytec.de/en/drive-shaft-vw-golf-mk2-mk3-corrado-passat-vr6-axle-conversion-02m-02q-dq250-dsg-6-speed-isp106));
- a China-only DQ500 behind the Teramont 2.5T (**REPORTED**, [Autohome](https://www.autohome.com.cn/info/201803/913726.html));
- the **02M** six-speed manual in VR6 form, including 4Motion versions in the 2004 R32 and 2008–09 TT 3.2 (**VERIFIED**, [FCP Euro](https://www.fcpeuro.com/blog/definitive-guide-vw-audi-6-speed-manual-transmissions-mq350-02m-02q-0fb));
- the B6 Passat's Aisin 09G/09M.

Only the 09P was built for MQB electronics.

**The A3's rear axle is the fixed point all four routes plug into.**

- Its Haldex 5 controller J492 comes from the 0CQ 907 554 D/H/J family. HPA sells one replacement controller for "all VAG MQB vehicles with Haldex AWD", and its list includes both the **A3 quattro 8V 2015–2020** and the **Atlas 2018–2022** (**VERIFIED**, [HPA, US$1,099](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/products/gen-5-performance-haldex-controller)). The Atlas scan shows software 0CQ 907 554 H (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech forum 10675](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/10675/)).
- Haldex 5 sets clutch pressure from ABS wheel speeds, vehicle dynamics, pedal position and engine torque, all received over CAN (**REPORTED**, [TPS](https://tps.trade/blog/all-systems-go-as-haldex-put-under-the-spotlight)).
- The same controller family runs manual Mk7 Golf Rs, so it does not need a DSG (**REPORTED**, [VW newsroom](https://www.volkswagen-newsroom.com/en/the-new-golf-r-2432/4motion-all-wheel-drive-in-the-golf-r-2451); **INFERRED**).
- It does need believable MQB engine messages. The Atlas ECU provides them; an older-platform ECU does not. For that case, Syvecs' standalone Haldex controller "Works with any Engine management system" (**VERIFIED**, [Syvecs](https://www.syvecs.com/?p=6664)) and costs about NZ$3,890 (**REPORTED**, [Harry's Euro](https://www.harryseuro.co.nz/products/syvecs-haldex-controller-vag-mqb-gen5)).

**The prop shaft is the second fixed point.** The front flex disc **5Q0 521 127** is listed for both the A3 and the Atlas, so an Atlas transfer case plausibly has the same three-arm flange (listing **VERIFIED**, [FCP Euro](https://www.fcpeuro.com/filters/Volkswagen-parts/Atlas/Driveshaft/); fit **INFERRED**). Where that flange sits relative to the A3 tunnel, and how its ratio compares with the A3's rear diff, are undocumented. HPA needed a "DQ381 compatible drive shaft" just to change DSGs within one MQB car (**VERIFIED**, [HPA Alltrack](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/pages/hpa-vr550t-2-5l-vr6-program-for-sportwagen-alltrack)). A ratio mismatch would load the Haldex clutch all the time, so check it before anything is welded (**INFERRED**).

**The Atlas 09P route ranks first** because it is the only one that can be bought from a single wreck and that keeps the ECU, transmission control unit and Haldex speaking one language. The A3 supplies the prop shaft, the Haldex rear and its controller. Its weaknesses are all physical:

- The Atlas left mount **3QF 199 555 C** is, in BFI's words, "a bit different than all the other MQB mounts" and "doesn't require the battery tray brackets that most other MQB cars use". It has nothing to bolt to in an A3 (**VERIFIED**, [BFI](https://blackforestindustries.com/blogs/news/tis-the-season-new-bfi-products-christmas-2022)).
- The Mk7 Jetta's smaller Aisin AQ300 uses the standard compact mount **5Q0 199 555 R**, so "Aisin 8-speed" is not one mounting family (**VERIFIED**, [UroTuning](https://www.urotuning.com/products/transmission-mount-5q0199555r)).
- No source gives the 09P's length, its selector type or where its control unit sits, and nobody has documented a 09P in a Golf or A3.
- HPA could easily get Atlas parts and still chose to machine a DQ381. That hints at a packaging problem without proving one (**INFERRED**).
- Whether the Atlas transmission control unit takes part in the immobilizer is an open question. Abrites lists DSG control units for Immo 5 but no Aisin (**INFERRED** from [Abrites VN025](https://abrites.com/partial/vn025)).

Two parts data points: the 09P service kit (7 L Liqui Moly Top Tec 1800, filter, gasket) was US$279.99 at NGP before being superseded (**VERIFIED**, [NGP](https://store.ngpracing.com/collections/dsg-system/products/vw-audi-09p-aq450-8-speed-automatic-transmission-service-kit)). A used 4Motion 09P listing shows seller-entered numbers 09P300036A and 09P323571L (**REPORTED**, [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/198230786141)).

**The manual route is the only one that gives three pedals plus AWD, and it needs the most fabrication.**

- The VR6 4Motion manuals are twenty-year-old boxes from the 2004 R32 and 2008–09 TT 3.2. They are rated at 258 lb-ft, exactly the 3.6's torque (**VERIFIED**, [FCP Euro](https://www.fcpeuro.com/blog/definitive-guide-vw-audi-6-speed-manual-transmissions-mq350-02m-02q-0fb)).
- Their angle drive and flanges belong to the older PQ platform, so the front of the prop shaft and the axles must be custom. Epytec sells VR6 4Motion 02M driveshafts as conversion parts (**REPORTED**, [Epytec ISP105](https://epytec.de/en/drive-shaft-vw-golf-mk2-mk3-corrado-passat-vr6-axle-conversion-4-motion-02m-02q-6-speed-4wd-isp105)).
- A US A3 8V has no clutch pedal. The pedal cluster, master cylinder, line, shift box and cables must come from a manual MQB Golf, GTI or Golf R, and no source gave those part numbers (**INFERRED**).
- A VW/Audi-trained technician has converted a two-pedal A3 quattro to three pedals, but the article could not be opened to confirm which generation or which parts (**REPORTED**, [Audiworld](https://audiworld.com/?p=47081)).
- No off-the-shelf limited-slip diff fits it: Peloquin's 02M diff "Does not fit AWD models" (**VERIFIED**, [NGP](https://store.ngpracing.com/products/peloquins-limited-slip-diff-vw-mk4-6-speed-02m)).
- The Atlas ECU was never calibrated for a manual, so the clutch logic must be custom code (**INFERRED**).

**The other two routes fail for different reasons.**

- **HPA's machined DQ381** is the only route that keeps every A3 mount, axle, transfer case and the prop shaft as built. It is a one-shop service inside the US$49,000-plus program, not a part you can buy (**VERIFIED**, [HPA](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/pages/hpa-vr550t-2-5l-vr6-program-for-golf-r)). The only hint of a DIY path to the A3's own DQ250 is one social post. A US Golf R builder reported an interim set-up using "an MKV R32 DSG bell housing on the factory MK7 DSG with an APR tuned TCU and a custom TZ Engineering dual mass flywheel" (**REPORTED**, [VRSociety](https://vrsociety.tumblr.com/post/651662812635152384/oem-25l-vr6-turbo-in-a-mk7-golf-r-dewain-is)). Even if that works, it would run the A3's DQ250 at or near its rating with a 3.6, which Wikipedia gives as 350 Nm in its text and 400 Nm in its own table (**REPORTED**, [Wikipedia DSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-shift_gearbox)).
- **A PQ35 02E 4Motion** brings an older-platform mechatronic unit and earlier-Haldex expectations into an MQB car, along with star-flange axles and a custom prop shaft (**INFERRED**). For the PQ35 VR6 cars, NGP sells a "Haldex Gen2 service kit" (**REPORTED**, [NGP](https://store.ngpracing.com/products/haldex-gen2-service-kit-vw-mk5-r32-b6-passat-audi-8j-tt-8p-a3-3-2l)).

## The engine bolts to an A3 rail on paper; everything behind it is fabricated

On MQB cars, the right engine mount has two interfaces. Its body side bolts into a pocket on the frame rail, and its arm bolts to an engine-specific console on the block. Three clues suggest the Atlas parts could work on an A3 rail:

- CTS sells one MQB four-cylinder mount as a "direct bolt on replacement" for both the A3 8V and the Atlas 2.0T, which implies the two rail pockets match (**VERIFIED**, [UroTuning CTS](https://www.urotuning.com/products/cts-turbo-street-sport-engine-mount-60a-durometer-mqb-4-cylinder-cts-em-mqb-60d)).
- Wikipedia states that "All MQB cars share the same front axle, pedal box and engine positioning" (**REPORTED**, [Wikipedia MQB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group_MQB_platform)).
- The Atlas 3.6 uses body-side mount **3QF 199 262 G/H** on VR6 console **022 199 354 T**. FCP lists that console for the A3 8P 3.2 quattro, R32, TT and Passat as well as the Atlas, so the VR6 block face has not changed across platforms (**VERIFIED**, [FCP Atlas mounts](https://www.fcpeuro.com/filters/Volkswagen-parts/Atlas/Mounts/)).

Atlas mount plus console on the A3 rail is therefore the leading candidate (**INFERRED**). Nobody has documented a test fit. The left side and everything behind the engine is where the A3 stops being bolt-on.

**Engine length is probably not the limit; depth, height and nose weight are.**

- Audi describes the RS3's 2.5 TFSI as "Just 49 centimeters (19.29 in) in length" (**REPORTED**, Audi media text seen in a search excerpt, [Audi MediaCenter](https://www.audi-mediacenter.com/en/audi-quattro-the-evolution-of-a-successful-technology-2791/the-audi-rs-3-2825)).
- The 3.6's narrow 10.6° bank angle makes it "much shorter for transverse packaging" than an inline-six (**REPORTED**, [Mobility Engineering Tech](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/43113-sae-ma-02396)).
- What nobody has measured is firewall clearance at the rear-bank exhaust and hood clearance at the intake.
- Weight is the clearer issue. A 3.6 is listed at 188 kg (**REPORTED**, [mymotorlist](https://mymotorlist.com/engines/volkswagen/ea390/bws/)) against 160 kg for the RS3's DAZA (**REPORTED**, [Bar-tek](https://www.bar-tek.com/the-new-2.5l-tfsi-ea855evo)), and an RS3 sedan already carries 58.2% of its weight on the front axle (**REPORTED**, [auto motor und sport](https://www.auto-motor-und-sport.de/fahrbericht/audi-rs3-limousine-test-hockenheim-leistungsdaten/technische-daten/)).
- A VR6 A3 would therefore carry roughly 30 kg more over the front wheels than an RS3, so budget RS3-rate springs and RS3 front brakes (**INFERRED**).

Each system in that table needs a plan of its own:

- **Cooling: use the S3's stack.** Main radiator, right auxiliary radiator, and the left corner as a second coolant radiator. The S3 rejects heat from a 292 hp turbo engine, and a naturally aspirated VR6 has no intercooler to feed (**INFERRED**). The A3/S3 holds **10.0 L** of coolant (**VERIFIED**, [Audi capacity chart](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10127956-9999.pdf)). The Atlas holds about 20 L (**REPORTED**, [Bolidenforum](https://www.bolidenforum.de/portal/fahrzeugdaten/volkswagen-2019-atlas-sel-awd/?vd_lang=en)), but most of that difference is the long rear-heater circuit. Cap that branch and its valve, and vacuum-fill the system as VW's Atlas tech tip requires (**VERIFIED**, [Tech Tip 80-18-01TT](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/SB-10161207-9999.pdf)).
- **Cooling: avoid the RS3 parts.** RS3 cooling hardware is built around the five-cylinder, and one owner found aftermarket side coolers "would not fit at all" (**REPORTED**, [Audizine](https://audizine.com/threads/rs3-cooling-upgrades.956908/)).
- **Hoses and lines.** The engine-end radiator hoses, the compressor-end A/C hoses and the intake tract all have to be made. The VR6 compressor sits low at the accessory end of the block, so none of the A3's hoses reach it (**INFERRED**).
- **Battery.** Move it to the trunk on the pattern of the RS3, which already carries its battery there (**REPORTED**, [Audizine](https://www.audizine.com/threads/battery-replacement.778108/)).
- **Exhaust.** Everything between the manifolds and an S3 cat-back is custom: a Y/mid-pipe with four oxygen-sensor bungs, routed beside the prop shaft. The Mk5 R32 / A3 3.2 downpipe layout is the closest geometry reference (**INFERRED**, [NGP USP downpipes](https://store.ngpracing.com/products/usp-high-flow-catalytic-converters-for-mk5-r32-and-a3-3-2l)).
- **Emissions hardware stays.** Ontario dropped Drive Clean testing on 1 April 2019, but "emission control systems are still required to be present and operating" (**VERIFIED**, [ERO 013-3630](https://ero.ontario.ca/node/478)). Keep the Atlas converters, all four sensors and the EVAP path. Do not use race-only high-flow cats.

**The Jetta wins every row it competes in except all-wheel drive.**

- **Gearbox:** its gearbox side is a used Mk4 24V 02M for about US$400 plus a South Bend clutch.
- **Mounts:** they are the same 1K0 catalog parts that Ninety4co bolted into a Mk6 Golf.
- **Software:** a published swap file names the B6 Passat 3.6.
- **Running proof:** the swap is already running on the road ([Ninety4co Part 4](https://youtu.be/6Gg6oTTig4Y)).
- **What it shares with the A3:** wiring. Ninety4co spent 200–300 hours re-pinning his harness ([Part 4](https://youtu.be/6Gg6oTTig4Y)), and the A3 needs a full re-pin too, onto a different supplier's ECU plug.

**The A3 adds three problems the Jetta never has:**

- the immobilizer on a car whose module coding is tied to its VIN;
- a custom AWD driveline;
- a donor that costs several times a junkyard BLV.

It gets back only all-wheel drive and a newer body, and the result is still slower than a stock S3.

**The body itself is a problem at 6'6".** TrueDelta lists the 2018 A3 sedan at 927 mm (36.5 in) of front headroom, while a dealer listing quotes 1,006 mm (39.6 in) (**REPORTED**, [TrueDelta](https://www.truedelta.com/Audi-A3/specs-8-2018&r=3); [AutoNation](https://www.autonationhondamiamilakes.com/used/Audi/2018-Audi-A3-dd3ab339ac185b54f99274c2472c8c99.htm)). The 2017 Jetta GLI is listed at 38 in, and iSeeCars says the GLI "has more front head room" than the A3 (**REPORTED**, [Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/buy/2017-Volkswagen-Jetta-GLI__4dr_Sedan/specs/expert-review/); [iSeeCars](https://www.iseecars.com/compare/audi-a3-vs-volkswagen-jetta_gli)). Two 6'5" owners in an AudiWorld thread split: one reports "no issues" on the standard power seats, while the other says that with the sunroof his "hair will barely touch the headliner" (**REPORTED**, [AudiWorld](https://www.audiworld.com/forums/audi-a3-s3-rs-3-mkii-209/s3-tall-people-2889297/)). A panoramic sunroof is reported as standard on the US A3 sedan (**REPORTED**, [Yahoo Autos](https://autos.yahoo.com/exterior-design-dimensions-180000658.html)). The S3 and RS3 share this body, so a ten-minute sit test settles all three Audi options at once.

**Insurance will cost about as much as the parts.** Ontario drivers aged 16–19 typically pay CA$3,000–8,000 a year (**REPORTED**, [BrokerLink](https://www.brokerlink.ca/faqs/how-much-is-car-insurance-for-a-new-driver-in-ontario)). ## Conclusion

The A3 question is not really about which parts fit. It is about which problems can be solved with money. Every hard problem on the Jetta has a vendor or a precedent: a catalog mount, a US$400 gearbox, a swap file and a running car. The A3's three hardest problems have none: pairing an ECU through Immo 5 on a car whose coding is tied to its VIN, an Atlas ECU calibrated to run without its Aisin gearbox, and a custom AWD driveline. Solving all three produces a heavier car with less power than an S3 a buyer can drive home today. The newest car that is still cheap and easy to VR6-swap is the last PQ35 one. MQB's shared electronics only help someone who can already write calibrations.

So the recommendation is to **build the Jetta** as planned and accept front-wheel drive as the price of a manual VR6 that can be finished:

- BLV engine on MED9, with United Motorsport's or Reflect Tuning's swap file;
- Mk4 24V FWD 02M with a South Bend clutch;
- 1K0 199 262 AR and 1K0 199 555 AP mounts.

If all-wheel drive is the stronger want, **buy a stock S3** at CA$15,888–21,990 and leave it alone. If AWD plus a manual is non-negotiable, the best-known factory MQB car with both is the Mk7 Golf R six-speed (**REPORTED**, [FCP Euro](https://blog.fcpeuro.com/the-definitive-volkswagen-mk7-golf-r-buyers-guide)). Its Canadian availability and price were not researched and should be the next search. The RS3 is the best car of the four but the worst buy on this budget: about CA$49,000 in Ottawa, plus a DQ500 transmission control unit that one buyer's guide calls the car's "Achilles heel" (**REPORTED**, [Three Piece](https://www.threepiece.us/blog/8v-audi-rs3-buying-guide-400hp-sleeper-worth-65k-in-2025/)).

The A3 VR6 becomes a sensible project only when three things exist:

- a measured test fit of an Atlas 09P and its left mount in an MQB compact car;
- a written, fixed-price swap-calibration quote;
- a buyable route to the A3's own gearbox: HPA's DQ381 adaptation sold as a part, or the R32-bellhousing-on-DQ250 trick repeated and documented.

If those appear, the parts tables above are the starting point, and the donor should still be a 2020–2021 Atlas.

### Check these before spending money

1. **Sit test (free).** Sit in a 2017–2020 A3 or S3 sedan with the panoramic roof, seat fully down, and in a 2015–2018 Jetta GLI on the same day. If the Audi fails, the A3, S3 and RS3 questions all close at once.
2. **Insurance quotes (free).** Get VIN-specific quotes as a 19-year-old in Ottawa for a stock S3, a GLI, and a GLI with a disclosed engine swap, before choosing.
3. **A tuner's written quote (a phone call).** For the Jetta, ask United Motorsport (through NGP) or Reflect for the BLV swap-file price. For the A3, ask an MED17 tuner for a fixed price on an Atlas MED17.1.62 swap file (immo-off, missing-TCU handling, start-stop off). Ask HPA whether its DQ381 adaptation is sold separately. If nobody quotes the A3 job, stop there.
4. **One ODIS question (US$100–150).** Ask a remote GeKo service whether VW's online system will adapt an Atlas ECU to an Audi A3 VIN before you rely on it. The RS3-into-S3 precedent says it will refuse.
5. **A tape measure at a wrecker (cheap).** Compare the Atlas 3QF 199 262 G/H body flange with the A3's 5Q0 199 262 BK rail pocket. Measure the VR6 plus 09P length against the A3's rails and battery-tray area. Check the Atlas transfer-case flange against the A3's 5Q0 521 127 flex disc.
6. **VIN-based catalog pulls (partslink24 or a dealer counter).** For the A3: gearbox code (0D9 or 0GC), PR codes, transfer case, prop shaft and axles. For the Atlas: transfer case, 3QF 199 555 C, axle inner-joint type, exhaust manifolds, thermostat housing and compressor. These close most REPORTED part numbers.
7. **Donor checks before paying.** Confirm the engine label reads CDVC, the ECU is 03H 906 026 AG/AF/AJ with software 9973 or 1689 or later, and the gearbox code is QVK/TYF for 4Motion. Check that the ECU plugs show no water corrosion.
8. **Two erWin day passes (about US$35 each), bought only after items 1–7 pass.** Download, for both VINs: the ECU location and both connector pin counts, the alternator LIN routing, the fuse/relay carriers, and the connector fitting locations. Run an A3 Auto-Scan and check the steering module for an "SFD" suffix.
