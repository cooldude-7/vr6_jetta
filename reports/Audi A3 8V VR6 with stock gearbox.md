# Swap the bellhousing, keep the A3's driveline

The VR6 that goes onto a 2015–2020 Audi A3 2.0T quattro's own gearbox is the **Atlas 3.6 CDVC on its own Bosch MED17.1.62 ECU**, and the bolt-up happens inside the gearbox: the A3's 6-speed **0D9 DQ250** is split and rebuilt around the **front case (the DQ250's bellhousing, 02E 301 107) of a four-wheel-drive VR6 DSG** from a Mk5 R32, an A3 8P 3.2 quattro or a TT 8J 3.2 S tronic, with the **VR6 DSG dual-mass flywheel 022 105 266 AH/AK (LuK 415 0755 09)** on the crank. The A3 keeps its gearbox computer (the mechatronic), transfer case, prop shaft, Haldex 5 rear, axles, transmission mount and dogbone, while an **Atlas engine mount (3QF 199 262 G/H on console 022 199 354 T)** hangs the engine on the A3's right frame rail, as it does in TuneZilla's VR6 S3. That interchange is **likely but unproven at catalogue level**: the evidence is one 2021 report of an R32 DSG bellhousing on a Mk7 Golf R's MQB DQ250, plus MQB gearboxes that carry 02E-numbered cases and clutch packs, with no catalogue proof that the four-wheel-drive faces match, so a casting comparison worth a few hundred dollars decides the project before the big money moves, and the documented fallback is the **RS3/TT RS DQ500 with a machined bellhousing and VR6 adapter** at roughly CA$4,000–7,000 more. Four 8V-body cars already run VR6s and a fifth is planned, but every one is a turbo car on a DQ500, so this would be the first stock-power VR6 8V on its own gearbox. Control is the easier half, because the Atlas ECU already speaks the MQB network language the A3's DSG, stability control and Haldex expect; the builder's work is an immobilizer-off edit, a gearbox-type change, re-pinning, and lifting the DSG's **350 Nm** torque limit above the VR6's roughly **361 Nm**. Excluding the car, the build nets roughly **CA$10,100–24,000**, against roughly CA$17,000–38,000 on the same basis in the earlier estimate. That earlier "don't swap the A3" verdict rested on three assumptions the new evidence overturns: no precedent, an Atlas 8-speed with a custom AWD driveline, and a calibration that had to be bought.

How to read this report. **VERIFIED** means a researcher opened and read the source: a factory document, a parts-catalogue fitment, a vendor product page, or an index of factory manuals. **REPORTED** means a forum or build post, a video, a press article, a listing title, or a search-engine summary. **INFERRED** means reasoning from those sources. Prices are converted at US$1 = CA$1.37, €1 = CA$1.55 and £1 = CA$1.80, before Ontario's 13% HST and shipping unless a line says otherwise. Before money moves, any REPORTED or INFERRED part number needs a casting-number read, a VIN-based catalogue check or a test fit.

## Three wrong assumptions sank the earlier "don't swap" verdict

The first pass at this question ended with "Leave the A3 stock, build the Jetta", at a net DIY cost of CA$30,000–56,000 including the car. That verdict rested on three assumptions, and all three have failed.

**The first was that nobody had done it.** The earlier search found no VR6 in any A3, S3 or RS3 8V, because the builds were on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Audizine and drive2, which that search could not see. TuneZilla, a Canadian tuning company, "swapped out the stock EA888.3 2.0T for a 3.6L VR6 from a Passat R36, paired it with a DQ500 transmission from an RS3, and run it all on a Bosch MED17 ECU from a VW Atlas" (**VERIFIED**, [TuneZilla](https://tunezilla.com/blog/300/swap-tuning-1750114528)). That 2017 S3 8V first started in March 2025 and has raced since (**REPORTED**, [Road To 1000 Ep 3](https://youtu.be/05nRq4ComSI)). Three more 8V-body cars and one planned car turned up too (next section).

**The second was that the A3's gearbox could not take a VR6.** On that basis the earlier report ranked the Atlas's Aisin 8-speed first, which needed a fabricated left mount, custom front axles and possibly a custom prop shaft. It mentioned the R32-bellhousing trick only as one unrepeated social post. The new gearbox research found three things. The DQ250's "bellhousing" is a separate front case that can be exchanged. MQB-generation 0D9 gearboxes carry 02E-numbered cases, mechatronics and clutch packs. And a second route exists: the RS3's DQ500 with commercially sold adapters, which TuneZilla's and HGP's cars both use.

**The third was that a calibration had to be bought** from a tuner nobody could find. The builder owns tuning tools and will do the engine ECU and the DSG himself. TuneZilla's S3 proves that an Atlas MED17 can be made to run an 8V's modules, an MQB DSG and a Haldex rear.

Two smaller changes compound the big three. The builder will buy a loose engine with its ECU instead of a wrecked Atlas. That replaces a CA$7,000–13,000 Copart donor with a US recycler engine listed at **US$1,787–1,994** (about CA$2,450–2,730 before shipping and tax) (**REPORTED** listing, [Transend](https://transend.us/products/engine/cylinder-block-components/engine-complete-assembly/engines100179265)). And keeping the A3's own gearbox deletes the custom axle, prop-shaft and left-bracket lines, which carried CA$2,500–5,000 of unquoted fabrication. The result is roughly **CA$10,100–24,000 net excluding the car**. The earlier build-up came to roughly CA$17,000–38,000 excluding the car (INFERRED, from the same anchor prices).

Several earlier findings survive and reappear below. MQB's Immobilizer 5 still makes online pairing of an Atlas ECU to an Audi VIN unlikely. The body harness still has to be re-pinned onto a Bosch plug with no public pinout. Ontario still requires emission controls to be present and working. And a stock S3 still makes more power (292 hp) than a stock Atlas VR6 (276 hp), which is one reason the builder is starting from an A3 rather than gutting an S3.

## A 4WD VR6 front case converts the A3's own DSG

### The DQ250's bellhousing is half of the gearbox case

On the DQ250 the bellhousing is not a bolt-on adapter. The case is two castings: a front case or clutch housing, **02E 301 107**, and a rear gearbox housing, **02E 301 103**. Super-Parts sells both at €196.48 each (**VERIFIED**, [Super-Parts](https://www.super-parts.eu/02e301107-gearbox-housing-dq250-02e-dsg-6/)). The front case carries bearing bores. Maktrans sells a separate **four-wheel-drive** front case, "02E301107" / "**02E301107R**" (€207.90), and remanufactures 4WD cases with "Spun Bearing Bore Restored" (**VERIFIED**, [Maktrans](https://maktrans.net/02E4WD107)).

Torque goes from the crank to the dual-mass flywheel. Then "the splines of the dual mass flywheel on the input hub of the double clutch transmit the torque", so the flywheel is the only engine-specific rotating part (**VERIFIED**, [SSP 308](https://www.volkspage.net/technik/ssp/ssp/SSP_308.pdf)).

The VR6 bell casting differs from the four-cylinder one, though forum sources disagree on how. One says the VR6 DSG "has a different bellhousing bolt pattern and WILL NOT FIT" a four-cylinder (**REPORTED**, [VWVortex](https://www.vwvortex.com/threads/1-8t-20v-dsg-dq250-complete-project-with-photos-videos-and-racelogic-data.9353965/)). Another says it is "the same pattern, but at a different angle. The GTI engine leans backwards and the VR6 leans forwards" (**REPORTED**, [r32oc](https://www.r32oc.com/threads/mk5-r32-dsg-compatibility.274130/)).

The job therefore runs as follows. Split the A3's 0D9 and move its gearsets, differential, clutch pack, mechatronic, rear housing and angle drive onto a 4WD VR6 front case. Re-shim with the **02E 398 321** end-play rings, set the clutch end play to the 0D9 manual, and renew the clutch cover. Fit the VR6 DSG flywheel and the VR6 DSG starter. Refill with **7.2 L of G052 182** DSG oil, then run the DSG basic settings.

That makes it a gearbox rebuild, not a bolt-on. It is a job for a DSG rebuilder, or for a builder with the 0D9 workshop manual (sections 30 Clutch, 34 Housing, 35 Gears, 39 Final drive, **VERIFIED** index, [vwts.ru](https://vwts.ru/audi_a3_8v.html)) and the DQ250 clutch-setting tools (INFERRED).

### The evidence says likely, and the catalogues say nothing

Four facts favour the interchange. Dewain's car used it (**REPORTED**). A used Golf VII gearbox is listed with references "02e301103 02e301107" (**REPORTED**, [ecooparts](https://ecooparts.com/en/used-auto-part/gearbox/volkswagen/golf-vii-lim/17733787_gearbox-volkswagen-golf-vii-lim-sport-bluemotion.html)). MQB mechatronics are sold as "DQ250 02E 0D9 02E927770AN" and "02E927770AT", and the AT suffix is "used on immobilized units in MQB cars" (**REPORTED**, [Sheng Hai](https://www.shenghaiautoparts.com/shop/tcu/02e927770at-dq250-02e-0d9-transmission-mechatronic/); [Tosen](https://www.tosenparts.com/dq250-vs-dq200-mechatronic/)). And the clutch pack **02E 398 029 C** lists "Audi A3 8V, Quattro: 2.0T" among its fitments (**VERIFIED**, [vagparts](https://vagparts.com.au/products/02e398029c-clutch-service-kit)). No source anywhere names a "0D9 301 107" part, which fits the idea that the 0D9 reuses 02E case numbers.

Against it, a Chinese article (seen only as a search summary) calls 02E and 0D9 separate families whose clutches, 02E 398 029 and 0D9 398 029 A, are "not compatible". The same summary claims the MQB clutch housing "was new", which may be the summariser's addition (**REPORTED**, [zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/tardis/jm/art/2074298562670761525)). A machine-written catalogue page says AWD DSG6 cases carry a 0D9 prefix, but Maktrans's 4WD 02E 301 107 listings contradict it (**REPORTED**, [Autoparts-24](https://www.autoparts-24.com/oem/02E-301-107/)).

The real unknown is the four-wheel-drive hardware. The VR6 02E's own outputs use "a star-shaped flange and a tripod joint" (**VERIFIED**, [Epytec](https://epytec.de/en/drive-shaft-vw-golf-mk2-mk3-corrado-passat-vr6-axle-conversion-02m-02q-dq250-dsg-6-speed-isp106)). Quaife's 4WD DQ250 differential is sold "excluding Golf 5 R32", so the R32's differential differs too (**REPORTED**, [AwesomeGTI](https://www.awesomegti.com/shop-by-car/volkswagen/golf-mk6/differentials/quaife-atb-helical-lsd-differential-for-4wd-vag-dq250-dsg-02e-transmission-excluding-golf-5-r32-qdf25r/)). In this conversion the A3's own differential and flanges move across. The question is whether the VR6 4WD case's differential bores, right-hand output bore and seal, and angle-drive (transfer-case) face accept the 0D9's parts. Dewain's Golf R was a 4Motion car, but his post does not say he kept its transfer case unchanged. The honest verdict is **likely yes mechanically, unproven at catalogue level**, to be settled by the bench comparison in the final section before anything else is bought (INFERRED).

The donor case must come from a **four-wheel-drive VR6 DSG**: a Mk5 R32 DSG (the family Dewain used), an A3 8P 3.2 quattro S tronic, or a TT 8J 3.2 S tronic. One R32 owner says "Mk5 R32 plus Mk2 Audi A3/TT 3.2 V6 all use the same gearboxes with same codes" (**REPORTED**, [r32oc](https://www.r32oc.com/threads/mk5-r32-dsg-compatibility.274130/)). The factory 02E study guide covers the A3 8P and TT 8J angle drive (**VERIFIED** index, [vwts.ru](https://vwts.ru/audi_tt_8j.html)). A CC or Passat 3.6 qualifies only if it is confirmed as a 4Motion DSG. The NMS Passat 3.6 is front-drive, so its front case is the FWD casting and will not take the A3's angle drive (INFERRED).

No source ties a front-case suffix to any VR6 car, so read the cast number on whatever donor turns up. No loose VR6 case was found priced. Ontario yards were not checked, and US whole-car TT 3.2 automatics were listed at US$7,529–12,190 (**REPORTED**, [KBB](https://www.kbb.com/cars-for-sale/used/audi/tt/3-2)). Expect to buy a whole gearbox or a parted-out case.

### The rest of the A3 driveline stays as built

Keeping the A3's own mechatronic matters. Ross-Tech states that on the 0D9, "replacement of the transmission or mechatronics unit will result in P1701" (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech 0D9](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/6-Speed_Direct_Shift_Gearbox_(DSG/0D9))). Moving the A3's own unit onto the rebuilt case keeps its pairing with the A3 cluster, because the TCU lives inside the mechatronic and the pairing travels with it (INFERRED). The transfer case (S3/Golf R **0CN 409 053 AL**, **REPORTED**, [PartRequest](https://www.partrequest.com/catalog/transfer-cases/volkswagen)) carries over too, as does the prop shaft with its front flex disc **5Q0 521 127**, rear coupling **5Q0 521 307** and centre support **5Q0 521 441 B** (**VERIFIED**, [FCP Euro](https://www.fcpeuro.com/Audi-parts/A3-Quattro/Driveshaft/)). So do the Haldex 5 rear (0CQ 525 010 series) and its J492 controller from the **0CQ 907 554 D/H/J** family (**VERIFIED**, [HPA](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/products/gen-5-performance-haldex-controller)), and the front axles, including the left **5Q0 407 271 AP** listed for the 6-speed DSG quattro (**VERIFIED**, [UroTuning](https://www.urotuning.com/products/drive-axle-left-front-vw-audi-mk7-golf-r-8v-a3-s3-5q0407271ap)).

The DQ250's oil cooler "is incorporated in the cooling circuit of the engine" (**VERIFIED**, [SSP 308](https://www.volkspage.net/technik/ssp/ssp/SSP_308.pdf)), so the VR6's coolant plumbing must feed the A3's gearbox cooler.

Torque is tight but workable at stock power. VW rates the DQ250 at "Maximum 350 Nm (depending on engine)" (**VERIFIED**, [SSP 308](https://www.volkspage.net/technik/ssp/ssp/SSP_308.pdf)). TVS says "stock rated up to +/- 350 Nm" (**VERIFIED**, [TVS](https://tvsengineering.com/en/dsg-gearbox/dq250/)). MaxxECU puts the clutches at about 400–450 Nm before they slip (**VERIFIED**, [MaxxECU](https://maxxecu.com/webhelp/settings-dsg.html)). The CDVC's **266 lb-ft (about 361 Nm)** is about 3% over the 350 Nm limit that HPA quotes for the stock TCU. HPA also puts the OEM clutches' durability threshold at 400 crank horsepower, far above the VR6's 276 hp (**VERIFIED**, [HPA DQ250 tune](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/products/hpa-vw-audi-dq250-dsg-tune)). Raising the TCU's torque limit is therefore cheap insurance, not a requirement for clutch capacity (INFERRED).

### Read the gearbox code before buying the car

The North American A3 2.0T quattro kept its six-speed when front-drive cars moved to a seven-speed: "The 2017 A3 2.0 TFSI quattro continues with its 220 hp, 258 lb-ft four connected to a six-speed dual-clutch S tronic" (**REPORTED**, [AutoTrader.ca](https://www.autotrader.ca/editorial/expert-reviews/audi/a3/first-drive-2017-audi-a3-s3/)). The factory 0D9 manual index pairs the gearbox with the 2.0 TFSI 162 kW on both the 2013 cars and the 2017 facelift (**VERIFIED**, [vwts.ru](https://vwts.ru/audi_a3_8v.html)). 162 kW is 220 PS, which matches the North American rating (INFERRED). One Australian MY17 A3 quattro listing shows a seven-speed (**REPORTED**, [carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/cars/details/2017-audi-a3-s-line-auto-quattro-my17/SSE-AD-20641863/)), so check each car. Read the three-letter code on the data sticker (spare-wheel well or service booklet) or on the case, and confirm the TCU part number in module 02 begins with 0D9 rather than 0GC or 0CW. A seventh gear on the display rules the car out. No VR6 path exists for the 7-speed 0GC outside HPA's in-house machining.

The S3 rule follows the builder's own reasoning: take an S3 only if a 6-speed one costs about what an A3 quattro does. 2019–2020 North American S3s have the 7-speed DQ381, and the S3's own engine out-powers a stock VR6. Audi's 2016 US media kit lists the six-speed S tronic across the A3 and S3 line (**REPORTED**, [Audi media kit](https://www.audiworld.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/2016-audi-a3-s3-media-kit.pdf)). This research could not pin the exact changeover year, so any 2017–2018 S3 needs its code read. Ontario asks were **CA$12,998–19,998** for A3 quattros, with a 2018 at 107,000 km for CA$15,490 in Ottawa. S3s were **CA$15,888–21,990** (**VERIFIED** listings, [Kijiji A3 quattro](https://www.kijiji.ca/b-cars-trucks/ontario/audi-a3-quattro/k0c174l9004); [Kijiji S3](https://www.kijiji.ca/b-cars-trucks/ontario/audi-s3/k0c174l9004)).

An S3 would bring one technical bonus. Its engine is rated at 380 Nm in European trim (**REPORTED**, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_A3)), so its TCU most likely starts with more torque headroom than the A3's 350 Nm (INFERRED). The S3's own 0D9 code letters were not captured, so read them too.

## The Atlas ECU already speaks MQB, and six edits finish the job

**The builder's engine choice is the right one for this gearbox, because the A3 keeps MQB modules that expect MQB-format engine messages.** The Atlas CDVC runs Bosch MED17.1.62, software **03H 906 026 xx** and hardware **03H 907 309 K**. The software suffixes run E/F/J/S for 2018, R/AA/AH for 2019, AG/AF for 2020 and AJ for 2021 (**VERIFIED**, [VW TSB 01-21-09](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2022/MC-10210224-0001.pdf); [Ross-Tech forum](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/10675/)). Atlas Auto-Scans show the same Continental MK100 brake controller as the A3 8V, with the identical label file **5Q0-907-379-IPB-V1.clb**, plus a Haldex 5 controller (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech A3/S3 8V](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/Audi_A3/S3_(8V/FF)); [Ross-Tech forum](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/10675/)).

The 3.2 (ME7.1.1) and the NMS Passat 3.6 (MED17.1.6) are older PQ-generation ECUs. The only converter for them is vd Veer's €500 PQ-to-MQB box. It restores rpm, temperatures, warning lamps, gear display, power-steering enable and fans, but its published list has **no engine-torque, pedal, ESC or Haldex messages** (**VERIFIED**, [vd Veer](https://www.dsgcontroller.com/shop/can-convertor/can-convertor-pq-mqb/)). That gap matters because DQ250 clutch pressure is "set primarily by the torque demand the ECM sends" (**REPORTED**, [EcuTek](https://ecutek.atlassian.net/wiki/x/AoBNZQ)). A DSG-plus-Haldex car with missing torque messages has no working clutch control.

**Keep every A3 module**: the gateway, the cluster (which stays immobilizer master for the keys and the TCU), the ESC, the power steering, the Haldex controller, the fuel-pump controller, the battery monitor and the A3's own mechatronic. A foreign MQB TCU "must be flashed to remove the VIN lock" (**VERIFIED**, [Syvecs SyvTCM manual](https://www.northamptonmotorsport.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/syvecs-tcm-dq250-mqb-transmission-controller-user-manual-v1.pdf)). Used MQB DSG mechatronics carry "the immobilizer data of its donor car" (**REPORTED**, [VWVortex](https://www.vwvortex.com/threads/1-8t-20v-dsg-dq250-complete-project-with-photos-videos-and-racelogic-data.9353965/)). The A3's own unit avoids both problems. The builder's edit list follows.

**The immobilizer has to be edited out, not paired.** MQB cars use Immobilizer 5, held in the cluster (**VERIFIED**, [Abrites](https://abrites.com/partial/vn025)). An S3 owner who fitted an RS3 ECU had VW's online adaptation refuse it (**REPORTED**, [MHH Auto](https://mhhauto.com/Thread-Audi-S3-to-RS3-conversion-ECU-issue-IMMO-OFF)). TuneZilla's own method is "EEPROM editing, checksum correction" (**VERIFIED**, [TuneZilla](https://tunezilla.com/blog/300/swap-tuning-1750114528)).

An OBD connection reaches only the calibration. On MED17 the immobilizer data lives in EEPROM, so this edit needs bench or boot-mode access (**REPORTED**, [HP Academy](https://www.hpacademy.com/forum/general-tuning-discussion/show/med17-5-ecu-cloning-query-for-learning-purposes/)). None of the tool support lists seen in this research names MED17.1.62 explicitly; they name MED17.5, ME17.5.6 and MED17.8.x (**REPORTED**, [chipsoft](https://chipsoft.com.ua/download/docs/EN_Module_%5B51%5D_Bosch_MEDC17_-_Bench.pdf); [tuning-shop](https://tuning-shop.com/about-us/news/alientech-news-update-upgrade-162/)). So the builder's first job with his own tools is to prove they handle 03H 906 026 in bench or boot mode, including EEPROM, on a spare unit before he touches the donor's.

**Items 2 and 3 are where the real work hides.** On a MED17 3.6, Ross-Tech's administrator wrote that "Transmission type and number of gears is defined in the ECU's coding, Byte 1", doubted the ECU would accept a change by coding alone, and warned that "a number of other modules will start complaining about missing messages from the TCU" (**VERIFIED**, [Ross-Tech forum](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/40349/)). On an MQB DQ200-to-DQ250 swap, "the ECU has to be changed from seven gears to six" (**REPORTED**, [simos.app](https://simos.app/guide/dsg-swap)). No public damos or A2L exists for MED17.1.62, so finding the gearbox-type and Aisin-monitoring logic is reverse-engineering work. TuneZilla's weeks of "communication errors" were that work done undocumented. HPA's VR6 Alltrack shows why the variant expectations matter: its gearbox threw a fault because it "tried to connect to an electronic parking brake that wasn't there" (**REPORTED**, [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/swapping-a-537-hp-vr6-turbo-into-a-vw-golf-alltrack-is-the-right-choice)). Keeping the A3's own TCU and ESC keeps those expectations native to the car.

**One hard-wired signal is easy to miss.** "The DQ250 TCU requires a tacho signal as a backup engine speed source" on gearbox connector **1J0 927 320 pin 7**, with pins 10 and 15 carrying CAN (**VERIFIED**, [Syvecs SyvTCM manual](https://www.northamptonmotorsport.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/syvecs-tcm-dq250-mqb-transmission-controller-user-manual-v1.pdf)). Which MED17.1.62 pin can supply it, and what feeds it in the A3 today, are unknown. Factory erWin diagrams for the Audi VIN and the Atlas VIN, at about US$35 per day each, are the only source (**REPORTED**, [Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2018/09/03/audi-really-not-writing-position-statements-in-future-says-answers-in-formal-repair-procedures/)). The same diagrams answer the alternator question. The Atlas alternator is LIN-managed by the gateway, and whether the A3 gateway accepts it is unverified (INFERRED).

**The DSG and Haldex sides are light.** HPA's DQ250 Stage 2 file for the A3 8V raises the torque limit "from 350 Nm to 500 Nm" for US$965 with an HP Tuners MPVI4, or US$699 for owners of one (**VERIFIED**, [HPA](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/products/hpa-vw-audi-dq250-dsg-tune)). Retail MQB DQ250 files run US$399–599 (**REPORTED**, [BMP](https://www.bmptuning.com/collections/integrated-engineering/products/integrated-engineering-dq250-dsg-transmission-tune-mqb); [NGP](https://store.ngpracing.com/products/tunezilla-tcu-software-tune-audi-8v-8s-vw-mk7-mk7-5-dq250-fwd)). These prices are a benchmark for the builder's own edit, not a purchase. Clutch relearn needs P or N, the park brake on, the engine idling, DSG oil at 30–100 °C, the brake pressed and torque below 40 Nm (**VERIFIED**, [Syvecs SyvTCM manual](https://www.northamptonmotorsport.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/syvecs-tcm-dq250-mqb-transmission-controller-user-manual-v1.pdf)).

The Haldex 5 controller reads engine torque and rpm over CAN, and the Atlas ECU broadcasts them in MQB format, so no change is expected (INFERRED). If J492 refuses to cooperate, there are two fallbacks. HPA's Gen 5 controller (US$1,099) lists the A3 quattro 8V (**VERIFIED**, [HPA](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/products/gen-5-performance-haldex-controller)) and is described as a drop-in J492 replacement that relies on OEM CAN (**REPORTED**). Syvecs' MQB4 controller "works with any engine management system" at about NZ$3,890 (**REPORTED**, [Harry's Euro](https://www.harryseuro.co.nz/products/syvecs-haldex-controller-vag-mqb-gen5)).

If an ECU step stalls, TuneZilla sells immo-off and MED17 swap tuning through its ticket system without a published price (**VERIFIED**, [TuneZilla](https://tunezilla.com/blog/300/swap-tuning-1750114528)). Its Simos immo/clone service at US$499 and its US$799 Atlas ECU+TCU file are the closest price references (**VERIFIED**, [TuneZilla MQB](https://tunezilla.com/blog/224/swap-tuning-for-mqb-1730848888); [NGP](https://store.ngpracing.com/products/tunezilla-ecu-software-tune-vw-atlas-3-6l-vr6)).

## The RS3 DQ500 is the documented fallback

If the VR6 front case will not take the 0D9's internals, the A3 family's own heavy-duty gearbox is the next stop. The RS3 8V and TT RS 8S use the 7-speed **0DL DQ500**, which VW's study guide rates at up to 600 Nm (**VERIFIED**, [vwts.ru](https://vwts.ru/audi_tt3_fv.html)). It has a four- and five-cylinder bell: "Sadly, the DQ500 gearbox didn't come with the VR6 engines and does not fit bolt-on to the engine." The conversion machines the bell and adds VR6 adapter plates, "a 10-bolt flywheel" "and a custom transfer case bracket" (**VERIFIED**, [Krabifab](https://www.krabifab.ee/product/dq500-adapter/)), presumably because the VR6 block lacks the support points the transfer case uses on the five-cylinder (INFERRED). TuneZilla had theirs "machined to mate up to the VR6, cuz typically they don't mate to the VR6, they're made for the 2.5 L" (**REPORTED**, [Ep 2](https://youtu.be/40MiKU9WxwE)). HGP's Golf R ran the same RS3 gearbox with a reinforced 8-plate clutch and modified DSG software (**VERIFIED**, [HGP](https://hgp-turbo.de/en/products/vw-golf-7-r-3-6-biturbo-740ps-und-780-ps)).

The totals check out: lines 1–16 sum to CA$12,100 and CA$28,500, and the resale credit brings them to CA$10,100 and CA$24,000. Reconciling the lines against the research changes the edges but not the headline. New Atlas mount parts with HST come to about CA$730–890, so line 5's high end understates them by up to about CA$190. Line 4's low end assumes a loose case at the price of unspecified 02E 301 107 listings, and no listing ties a case to a VR6, so its high end is the more realistic planning figure. Line 12 assumes the battery has to move, which TuneZilla reported only for a turbo car. These adjustments roughly offset and sit inside the rounding of a CA$24,000 figure, so the net stays at **roughly CA$10,100–24,000** (INFERRED).

The list deliberately leaves out several costs. The verification purchases are not in it: a spare used MED17.1.62 to prove the ECU toolchain, and a core 4WD 0D9 if the A3's own gearbox cannot be opened for the bench comparison. Both are unpriced. DSG clutch-setting tools are not in it if a shop does not do the rebuild. Bigger front brakes are not in it; the earlier build-up carried CA$1,000–3,000 for RS3-grade brakes and springs, which the stock-power plan treats as optional. Nor is the A3 itself (CA$12,998–19,998 in Ontario), or insurance. Ontario drivers aged 16–19 typically pay CA$3,000–8,000 a year (**REPORTED**, [BrokerLink](https://www.brokerlink.ca/faqs/how-much-is-car-insurance-for-a-new-driver-in-ontario)). The swap must be disclosed: in *Aviva v. Cahill* (2026 ONSC 4975), an owner who did not report his modifications had his coverage capped at $200,000 (**VERIFIED**, [Law Times](https://www.lawtimesnews.com/practice-areas/personal-injury/man-who-modified-corvette-breached-insurance-contract-superior-court/393526)).

Two things move the number most. The bellhousing comes first: if the front case fails, the DQ500 fallback replaces line 4 and adds roughly CA$4,000–7,000. Engine mileage comes second, but only a little, because a cheap high-mileage CDVC and a low-mileage one both need the same timing and cooling refresh.

## Conclusion

The whole project now hinges on a few hundred dollars of measurement rather than tens of thousands of fabrication. Three cheap tests decide it: whether a CA$300 casting accepts the 0D9's bearings, differential and angle drive; whether a CA$480 flywheel engages the 0D9's clutch hub; and whether the builder's own tool can read and write a MED17.1.62's EEPROM. If all three pass, the build becomes a known list of parts and a lot of wiring, because the risky driveline work is gone. If the casting fails, nothing bought for the engine is wasted: the engine, mount, ECU work and cooling all carry over unchanged to the DQ500 fallback that TuneZilla has already proven in the same body. What changes is the budget, by CA$4,000–7,000, which is why the bench test comes before the engine purchase. It tells the builder which of two budgets he is committing to before the big cheques go out.

The precedents turn out to support this build more than they resemble it. Every 8V VR6 so far chose a DQ500 because turbo torque overwhelms the DQ250. The builder's stock-power choice is exactly what keeps the A3's own gearbox inside its rating. That same choice ties the car to near-stock torque, since the rebuilt gearbox still carries the DQ250's 350 Nm design limit. The hardest remaining unknown is not mechanical at all. It is the undocumented gearbox-type and Aisin-monitoring logic inside the Atlas ECU, the same place TuneZilla spent its weeks of "communication errors". A builder who maps that logic on a spare ECU before the engine arrives turns the longest-delay step into the first one finished.
