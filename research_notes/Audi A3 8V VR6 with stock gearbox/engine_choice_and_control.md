## 1. Engine options for this job (3.2, 3.6 BLV / CDVB / CDVC / BWS, 2.5T DDKA): output, ECU family, weight, availability and price in Ontario

### Takeaway
The engine choice is really a choice of **ECU generation**, because the A3 keeps MQB modules (DSG mechatronic, ESC, Haldex 5, gateway) that expect MQB-format engine messages. Only the **Atlas CDVC (MED17.1.62)** is an MQB-generation VR6 ECU sold in North America, and it is the one a Canadian shop (TuneZilla) has already run in an S3 8V with an MQB DSG and Haldex. The 3.2 (ME7.1.1) and the 3.6 BLV (MED9) / CDVB (MED17.1.6) are PQ-generation and need CAN translation. The 2.5T DDKA is effectively an HPA-only product. Used-engine supply in Ontario is thin for all of them: Kijiji Ontario showed no loose 3.2/R32 engine and one 3.6 Touareg listing on the research date, so the realistic source is a whole donor car (Copart via broker for Atlas).

### Inferences
- **INFERRED — ranking for an A3 that keeps its MQB DSG and Haldex 5:**
  1. **CDVC (Atlas, MED17.1.62):** best electrical fit (MQB message catalogue, same generation as the A3's 0D9 TCU, MK100 ESC and J492), a Canadian precedent in an S3 8V (section 3), donor supply via Atlas wrecks. Cons: Immo 5 ECU, no published pinout, ECU calibrated for an 8-speed Aisin, direct injection.
  2. **CDVB (NMS Passat, MED17.1.6):** vd Veer has already converted this ECU's CAN to MQB, but the converter's signal list lacks torque messages for the DSG/ESC/Haldex (section 2). Cheapest 3.6 donors (Passat V6 wrecks), but PQ CAN.
  3. **3.2 BUB/CBRA (ME7.1.1):** the lightest power (250 hp/320 Nm) but the cheapest and most hackable ECU (ME7 immo-off is routine), port injection, and the donor car (R32/A3 3.2/TT 3.2) also supplies the 02E VR6 bellhousing and flywheel the A3's DSG needs. PQ CAN, oldest, chain issues; donors are rare and collectible in Ontario.
  4. **BLV (MED9):** PQ CAN like the 3.2 with more power; MED9 is less hacked than ME7 and less documented for MQB swaps than MED17.1.6. Mid-pack.
  5. **DDKA:** not a DIY option; HPA does not publish an engine-only price and sells it as part of a US$49k+ program.
- **INFERRED:** The 3.2 vs 3.6 weight difference cannot be quantified from sources; the BWS figure (188 kg) suggests a 3.6 sits roughly 40+ kg above an EA888 (EA888 weight unverified).
- **INFERRED:** Whichever engine is chosen, buy a **whole donor car** (Copart/IAA via broker, or a private wreck) rather than a loose engine: Ontario has almost no loose VR6 engines listed, and the donor supplies the ECU, engine harness, ECU body-harness tail, mount and (for 3.2 donors) the VR6 DSG bellhousing.

## 2. Control strategy 1 — keep the donor's own PQ-generation ECU and translate its CAN for the MQB car

### Takeaway
One off-the-shelf product exists: **vd Veer Engineering's "CAN Convertor PQ->MQB" (€500 ex VAT, SKU CAN-CONV-PQMQB)**, which explicitly lists "PQ engines in Audi A3 8V". It restores cluster, warning lights, gear display, shift-lock, EPS enable and fan control, but its published signal list contains **no engine-torque, pedal or ESC/Haldex messages**. vd Veer's own MQB VR6 build avoided the problem by running a PQ DQ500 with its own PQ TCU rather than the MQB DSG. So "PQ ECU + converter + the A3's own MQB 0D9 + Haldex 5" is unproven; it may need a torque-message emulator (e.g., Syvecs SyvTCM, section 4) on top. No Polish, Russian or German gateway product for this was found.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED — what works with strategy 1 in an A3 that keeps its own 0D9:** cluster RPM/temps/lamps, gear display, shift-lock, EPS enable, fans (from the converter list); OBD/UDS diagnostics of the engine ECU through the gateway. **Unknown or likely broken:** DSG shift quality and clutch pressure (the MQB TCU sets clutch pressure from the engine's torque messages, which the converter does not list), launch control, ESC torque intervention/ASR (needs engine torque and an engine that accepts torque-reduction requests), Haldex 5 torque-based pre-load, cruise. Expect ESC/Haldex/TCU "implausible/missing message from engine" faults until vd Veer confirms torque-message translation.
- **INFERRED:** vd Veer's own MQB VR6 build kept a PQ gearbox and PQ TCU talking natively to the PQ ECU. For a PQ ECU, the internally consistent drivetrain is a PQ 02E 6-cyl DSG (R32/A3 3.2/TT 3.2), which brings star-flange axles, a PQ angle drive and a custom prop shaft (`transmission_driveline.md`). That defeats the "keep the A3's own driveline" goal.
- **INFERRED — cost if it does work:** converter €500 + DSG/cruise helpers €175–500 + PQ ECU immo-off (ME7 cheap; MED17.1.6 US$135–350 per background) + a PQ tune. This is the cheapest strategy on paper, but the torque-message gap is a show-stopper risk for a DSG-plus-Haldex car. Ask vd Veer in writing whether CAN-CONV-PQMQB firmware sends MQB engine-torque and pedal messages and whether it has been run with an MQB DQ250 + Haldex 5 before buying.

### Gaps
- vd Veer firmware release notes (which list the newest signals) were not found; whether any vd Veer customer has run a PQ ECU with an MQB 0D9 + Haldex 5.
- No "CANtronics"/Polish/Russian/German gateway product for PQ-engine-into-MQB found; none of the swap specialists publish prices beyond vd Veer.

## 3. Control strategy 2 — an MQB ECU (Atlas MED17.1.62) talking natively to the A3's mechatronic and Haldex

### Takeaway
This is the route with a real precedent in an 8V. **TuneZilla (Western Canada) runs a 3.6 VR6 in a 2017 S3 8V on a factory Atlas Bosch MED17 ECU** with their own software patches, an RS3 DQ500 (MQB) with machined bellhousing, and the Haldex rear retained. TuneZilla publicly sells immo-off and custom swap tunes for Bosch MED17-series ECUs, including "CAN bus tweaks for full gauge cluster integration", ordered through its ticket system; no swap-tune price is published. Since the builder will self-tune, the value of this precedent is proof that the Atlas MED17 can be made to coexist with 8V modules, an MQB DSG and Haldex, and a list of what had to change (section 8). It is not documented whether their DQ500 runs a TCU tune. HPA is the other shop with MQB VR6 "full UDS canbus integration", but only inside its US$49k+ programs. No IE, EQT, Unitronic or APR MQB swap calibration was found.

## 4. Control strategy 3 — standalone engine ECU with MQB CAN, standalone DSG TCU, standalone Haldex 5 controller

### Takeaway
No standalone engine ECU was found that drives an **MQB DSG + Haldex 5** off-the-shelf behind a VR6. The DSG half is solved only by **Syvecs**: a licensed, dealer-only "DQ250 / DQ500 MQB 2015+ Gearbox Control" firmware (needs a Syvecs S7I/S7Plus) and the separate **SyvTCM** module (Sept 2026 manual), which works with any engine ECU via generic CAN, emulates ABS and steering column to the TCU, and can estimate torque internally. **MaxxECU's DSG control is PQ-only** (TCUs from MED9.1-ECM cars; "does NOT support" SIMOS-ECM gearboxes, i.e. the A3's). ECUMaster, Haltech and Link have no documented MQB DSG control. Haldex 5 can be run by the Syvecs MQB4 AWD controller (~NZ$3,890), which "works with any engine management system". Prices for Syvecs DSG products are not published.

## 5. DSG software: does the A3's 0D9 (or 0GC) need a TCU tune for a VR6 torque curve, and who sells it

### Takeaway
On torque alone, a naturally aspirated VR6 (3.2: 320 Nm; 3.6: ~350–370 Nm) sits at or just under the DQ250's stock controller limit (sources: ~350–380 Nm limit; 400–450 Nm before clutch slip). A TCU tune is therefore **not strictly needed for clutch capacity**, but is advisable to raise the torque limit (so the TCU does not clamp or slip near peak), raise shift RPM, and add launch. The bigger requirement is that the engine ECU sends MQB-format torque values and has the gearbox type set to DSG. Off-the-shelf MQB DQ250 TCU tunes cost **US$399–965**. All are written for EA888 engines but flash the same 0D9 TCU; a VR6-specific map is custom work. A forced-induction VR6 needs a Stage 3-class tune and probably clutch packs, or a DQ381/DQ500.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED (vendor page)** — **HPA VW & Audi DQ250 DSG Tune**: fitment includes Audi **A3 8V/8Y 2014–2023 and S3 8P/8V 2010–2017**, Golf R Mk7, GTI; **Stage 2 US$965 with HP Tuners MPVI4 (US$699 if you own one)**: torque limit "raised from 350 Nm to 500 Nm", launch control, redline 7,150 rpm, auto-shift removed; **Stage 3 US$1,265 / US$999**: recalibrated shifts, clutch engagement curve and pressure, for >600 ft-lb; "OEM clutches have a durability threshold of 400 crank HP"; calibrated per VIN, ECU software and mods; "not approved for highway or street vehicle use"; listed as sold out / available to order at capture. — [HPA DQ250 DSG Tune](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/products/hpa-vw-audi-dq250-dsg-tune)

The A3 2.0T (350 Nm stock) TCU calibration has less headroom than the S3's (S3 torque figure not verified this run), so an **S3 is the better recipient** if buying.
- **INFERRED:** The off-the-shelf TCU tunes are keyed to the TCU software/VIN, not the engine, so they should flash an A3/S3 0D9 regardless of the VR6; the shift maps will still assume an EA888 torque curve. HPA's per-VIN calibration (Stage 2/3) is the only listed one that is "calibrated to … supporting modifications". It is US-based and MPVI4-delivered (HP Tuners works in Canada).
- **INFERRED:** Clutch relearn (VCDS basic setting: P/N, park brake on, engine idling, oil 30–100 °C, brake pressed, torque <40 Nm per the Syvecs manual) will be needed after the swap regardless.

## 7. Recommendation: simplest, cheapest-to-finish engine + control combination for a DIY builder in Ottawa

### Takeaway
**Atlas CDVC 3.6 (whole wrecked Atlas as donor) + its own MED17.1.62 ECU, self-tuned (immo off by EEPROM edit, DSG gearbox type, TCU-message monitoring, CAN/cluster integration; tools in section 8), driving the A3/S3's own 0D9 through a Mk5 R32 / A3 3.2 / TT 3.2 02E VR6 bellhousing, plus an off-the-shelf MQB DQ250 TCU tune; keep the OEM Haldex 5 J492 unchanged.** It has the only same-chassis precedent (TuneZilla S3), needs no translation box, and has the fewest extra modules. TuneZilla remains a paid fallback if a self-tuning step stalls. The 07K 2.5 I5 is the cheapest donor but is a PQ-ECU car with a torque-message gap and only 170 hp NA. An RS3 DAZA transplant is the factory-parts route but replaces the A3's DSG and is the most expensive donor (section 9). Fallbacks: Syvecs MQB4 for Haldex (~NZ$3,890) if J492 will not cooperate. The PQ-ECU + vd Veer route is cheaper on paper (€500 box, cheap ME7 ECU) but unproven with an MQB DSG and Haldex. The full standalone route is the most expensive and dealer-only.

- **INFERRED — why not the others:** a 3.2 (ME7) is the cheapest ECU and gives the bellhousing for free, but it adds a PQ→MQB box that does not list torque messages and only 250 hp. The CDVB (MED17.1.6) has the vd Veer converter precedent but the same torque-message gap. A standalone adds US$10k+ and dealer-only licences. The DDKA is HPA-only.
- **INFERRED — order of operations for the Ottawa builder (self-tuning):** (1) buy a used MED17.1.62 (03H 906 026 xx) on its own first, confirm the chosen tool can bench/boot read and write it including EEPROM, back up, and locate the gearbox-type and TCU-monitoring settings before committing to a donor car; also scan the A3/S3 (gateway installation list, 0D9 TCU part/software) to know what it expects; (2) source an 02E VR6 DSG bellhousing and confirm it bolts to the 0D9 case (Dewain REPORTED only); (3) buy the donor Atlas; (4) prefer an S3 (more TCU torque headroom, INFERRED) as recipient.

### Gaps
- Which tool currently reads/writes MED17.1.62 in boot/bench mode, including EEPROM (vendor lists seen this run named MED17.5 / ME17.5.6 and MED17.8.x, not MED17.1.62); whether the A3's gateway accepts the Atlas ECU without online SVM (background blocker).
- Whether an 02E 6-cylinder bellhousing bolts to an MQB 0D9 case unmodified (only Dewain's REPORTED interim setup); a source and price for that bellhousing in Ontario.
- Ontario U-Pull / LKQ / Kenny U-Pull / Copart.ca availability of R32, A3 3.2, TT 3.2, Eos 3.2 (bellhousing donors) and Atlas 3.6 (engine donors) not checked this run.
