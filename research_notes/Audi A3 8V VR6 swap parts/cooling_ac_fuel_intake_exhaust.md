# Plumbing for a VW Atlas 3.6 VR6 (EA390, CDVC) in a 2015–2020 Audi A3/S3/RS3 8V: cooling, A/C, fuel, intake, exhaust

Scope: which radiator, fan, coolant, A/C, fuel, intake and exhaust parts could serve a 2018–2023 North American Atlas 3.6 in an 8V A3 (preferably quattro) bay, with part numbers, fitment and sources, and where fabrication is unavoidable. Labels: **VERIFIED** = read on a parts-catalog/fitment page, factory document, or vendor product page fetched in this session; **REPORTED** = forum post, marketplace listing, or search-engine snippet whose page could not be opened (or was only seen as a snippet); **INFERRED** = my reasoning. No part number below is invented. Where a number came only from a snippet, it is labeled REPORTED.

Access limits: every Audi/VW dealer catalog tried (parts.audiusa.com, audiusaparts.com, audipartsdeal.com, partsouq, 7zap, Hendrick VW, Go-Parts, autopartspro) returned Cloudflare 403 pages to both WebFetch and curl. oemwolf.com answers by curl but its fitment data stops at about 2015 and has no 3QF (Atlas) or late 5Q0 numbers (5Q0121251GN, 5Q0121251HA and 3QF121251D all returned 404). FCP Euro category pages were readable and are the main VERIFIED source here, but FCP rarely shows years or engines, so "fits Atlas" on FCP can mean the Atlas 2.0T (EA888) rather than the 3.6. Background on PQ35 swaps (Ninety4co BLV-in-Mk6, Gray Fab swirl pot, USP R32 downpipes, Ross-Tech P310B) is in `../Mk6 Jetta CDVC VR6 swap parts/cooling_ac_fuel_intake_exhaust.md` and is not repeated.

**Key new fact:** a VW of America tech tip lists the **2018–2019 Atlas 3.6L engine code as CDVC**, the same code as the 2012–2018 NMS Passat 3.6 in the Jetta notes. — VERIFIED, [VW Tech Tip 80-18-01TT (NHTSA PDF)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/SB-10161207-9999.pdf)

## Cooling capacity: A3 / S3 / RS3 8V radiators, auxiliary radiators and fans vs the Atlas 3.6; is an S3/RS3 package the right base?

### Takeaway
The A3, A3 quattro, S3, Golf R and GTI share one main-radiator footprint (FCP fitment for Nissens 65302 / CSF 3922 / Mahle CR811000S; CSF's performance core is cross-referenced to **5Q0 121 251 GN**). The S3 adds bumper-corner coolers: a right-side auxiliary coolant radiator (**5Q0 121 253 H**, "secondary radiator") and a left-side DSG heat exchanger (**5Q0 121 251 HA**), fed through a Y-piece after the main radiator. The Atlas 3.6 radiator is reported as **3QF 121 251 D**, and the Atlas carries about 20 L of coolant against 10.0 L for the A3/S3. My judgment: use the S3 package (main radiator, right auxiliary radiator, and the left position as a second coolant cooler when the DSG cooler is not needed), with an aluminium main core as the upgrade. Without an intercooler the stack is less crowded, so I see it as a sound base for a ~280 hp naturally aspirated VR6. The RS3 package is less useful: its front end and side-cooler mounts are RS-specific, and owners report aftermarket side coolers do not fit.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Base package: an A3 quattro shares the S3's main-radiator footprint (same FCP fitment rows), so the recommended stack is: main radiator 5Q0 121 251 GN-footprint (CSF/Mishimoto aluminium preferred), right auxiliary coolant radiator 5Q0 121 253 H, and the S3 left-corner position. If the swap uses a manual or non-DSG gearbox, put a second coolant radiator in that left position rather than the DSG cooler 5Q0 121 251 HA. This needs the S3 Y-piece, quick-couplings (for example 5Q0 122 291 BL), bumper-corner brackets and ducts. Confirm whether an A3 2.0T quattro bumper has the corner openings and mounting points before buying. A non-S3 A3 likely has only the main radiator.
- **INFERRED** — Capacity: the S3 system rejects heat from a 292 hp EA888.3 whose integrated exhaust manifold is water-cooled. The EA390 has separate cast manifolds and no turbo or intercooler heat to reject, so a ~280 hp NA VR6 should not exceed what the S3 stack handles at road loads. Ninety4co's "these engines make a lot of heat" warning (Jetta notes) supports using the aluminium main core plus both side coolers. Deleting the intercooler frees the air path ahead of the radiator. The Atlas's ~20 L coolant volume largely reflects its long rear-heater circuit and is not a radiator-size target.
- **INFERRED** — The RS3 8V is a poor donor for this swap: its cooling hardware is RS-specific (8V0 main radiator, side coolers with unverified numbers, 07K pump), it needs RS3 bumper and ducts, and aftermarket side coolers are reported not to fit. If more capacity is needed, a thicker CSF/Mishimoto S3 main core is the simpler route.
- **INFERRED** — Fan control: both the A3 and the Atlas are MQB, so the CDVC ECU should drive an MQB fan with an integrated electronics module on a PWM line, as the EA888 ECU does. Prefer the highest-wattage 5Q0 121 203 variant that fits the A3 shroud (600 W "BK" per aftermarket data) over early low-output "DE" units. Verify connector and pin-out against the Atlas fan.
- **INFERRED** — The Atlas radiator (3QF 121 251 D) and shroud (3QF 121 207) are sized for the Atlas carrier and will not fit the A3 lock carrier. They are comparison data only.

## Atlas 3.6 coolant routing: thermostat, pumps, heater core, expansion tank, and A3 hose mating

### Takeaway
The Atlas 3.6 is a **CDVC** with a belt/chain-side mechanical pump (**03H 121 008 M**, shared with the Touareg). It also carries 3.6-only coolant-circuit valves, a heater circulation pump, and a rear-heater circuit with an **auxiliary heater core valve**. The A3 needs none of the rear-heat hardware. The only Atlas electric auxiliary pump number found is **5Q0 965 567 Q** (snippet, 3.6L applications). It is the same MQB 5Q0 965 567 pump family used on A3/Golf, which simplifies wiring. No Atlas thermostat-housing, hard-pipe or expansion-tank part numbers were found. The A3's radiator hoses use MQB quick-couplings at the radiator and EA888 outlets at the engine end, so I expect the engine-side radiator hoses to be custom or modified OEM hoses.

Procedure: pressure-test, "check the heater core lines after the thermostat to the rear heater core", replace the valve and **vacuum bleed** the cooling system. ### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Because the Atlas engine code is CDVC, the PQ46/NMS Passat CDVC hose research in the Jetta notes applies to the engine-side ports (thermostat outlet, return tee/check valve, heater feed). The Atlas's own hoses are Atlas-length (3QF) and include a rear-heater branch.
- **INFERRED** — For the A3: cap or delete the Atlas rear-heater branch and its auxiliary heater core valve. Keep the CDVC's heater circulation/after-run pump (5Q0 965 567 family) because the ECU and climate control expect it. Feed the A3 heater core from the CDVC heater outlets with modified A3 or Atlas heater hoses. Expect to trim and splice with MQB quick-connect ends. Vacuum-fill after assembly per the tech tip.
- **INFERRED** — Expansion tank: either relocate an A3 tank and run a new bleed line from the CDVC head/return, or use the Gray Fab swirl pot (its two variants depend on timing cover, so match it to the Atlas cover). The A3 tank sits on the right side of the bay, near the accessory end of a transverse VR6, so routing is plausible but unverified.
- **INFERRED** — Radiator hoses: the A3's upper and lower hoses end in EA888-specific engine fittings. Plan custom silicone hoses with MQB quick-connect radiator-end adapters, or hybrid Atlas engine-end and A3 radiator-end hoses joined with straight couplers. **Fabrication likely unavoidable.**

## A/C: Atlas 3.6 compressor and position vs the A3 EA888; A3 lines; custom lines; condenser reuse

### Takeaway
FCP lists the Atlas compressor as **7N0 816 803 E** (genuine, Nissens, Valeo), plus Mahle **7N0 820 803 E**, without engine split. Nissens 891695 is explicitly listed for the 2018 Atlas 3.6. A3 8V compressors are **5Q0 816 803 D**, **5Q0 816 803 H** and **5Q0 820 803 K**, and 2017 A3/S3 cars use **R134a (500 ± 15 g)**. The Atlas uses **R1234yf**. A3 8V lines appear as **5Q0 816 743 E / H** (used-parts listings). No source gives the CDVC compressor position on the Atlas bracket. The PQ35 precedent (Ninety4co) says the 3.6 bracket puts the compressor lower than on a TSI, so I expect at least the compressor-end hoses to be custom-crimped.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Compressor: use the compressor that bolts to the CDVC accessory bracket, i.e. the Atlas 3.6 unit (7N0 816 803 E family / Nissens 891695 per the listings; confirm by VIN). The A3's 5Q0 816/820 803 compressors mount to the EA888 bracket and will not bolt to the VR6. 7N0 816 803 E is also listed for the Beetle (EA888 only), which suggests a common compressor body across engines, but the FCP rows do not split engines, so this is unproven.
- **INFERRED** — Refrigerant: keep the A3's refrigerant as labelled (R134a for 2015–2017, R1234yf likely from 2018). Compressor block fittings are generally refrigerant-agnostic but the oil charge and quantity are not. Flush and charge to the A3 spec (500 g for R134a cars), with oil quantity matched to the compressor make. Never mix refrigerants.
- **INFERRED** — Lines: the A3's evaporator-to-compressor (suction) and compressor-to-condenser (discharge) hoses end at the EA888 compressor position. The VR6 compressor sits low at the front (accessory) end of the block, so both compressor-end hoses will be wrong. No OEM MQB line exists for a VR6 in an A3. **Custom-crimped hoses are unavoidable**: keep the A3 evaporator and condenser block fittings and splice to Atlas compressor block fittings (re-use Atlas hose ends). The A3 condenser-to-evaporator liquid line can likely stay.
- **INFERRED** — Condenser: reuse the A3 condenser, since it is sized for the A3 carrier and the A3 lines. The intercooler is deleted, so the condenser and radiator get cleaner air than in the turbo car.

## Fuel: Atlas 3.6 low-pressure system and J538; A3 8V quattro tank, pump and J538; can the A3 supply the CDVC; HPFP

- **INFERRED** — The HPFP 03H 127 025 S/D, rail and injectors come with the engine. The chassis joins at one low-pressure feed line from the A3 tank to the CDVC HPFP inlet. That line needs a VW quick-connect adapter or a modified A3 feed line, a small fabrication item. Keep the A3 EVAP canister and run its purge line to the CDVC purge valve.
