# VR6 swaps into MQB A1 cars, the North American Audi A3 8V as recipient, and the Ontario recipient/donor market

Research date: 2026-10-08. Prices are CAD unless marked USD. Listing prices were read from the Kijiji and AutoTrader.ca search-result pages' embedded data on the research date; they will move. Labels: **VERIFIED** = vendor page, factory/spec sheet, or listing page actually read; **REPORTED** = forum/build thread/social post, press article, or search snippet (page not opened, or third-party claim); **INFERRED** = the researcher's reasoning. VWVortex and golfmk7.com returned bot-block pages (2.6 kB / 43 B) to both WebFetch and curl, so every quote from those forums below is a search-engine snippet. Background (not redone): the earlier Jetta report establishes CDVB = NMS Passat 3.6 (MED17.1.6, 03H 906 023), CDVC/CDVD = Atlas/Teramont 3.6 (MED17.1.62, 03H 906 026), BLV = B6 Passat (MED9) — see `/home/user/vr6_jetta/reports/Mk6 Jetta CDVC VR6 swap parts.md`.

## 1. Has a VR6 (3.6 EA390, 3.2, or the Chinese 2.5T) been swapped into an MQB A1 car?

### Takeaway
No documented VR6 swap into any Audi A3/S3/RS3 8V, TT 8S, Jetta Mk7, Tiguan or Arteon was found; every MQB VR6 precedent is a VW Golf Mk7/7.5 (or Golf Alltrack/SportWagen), and the only finished, documented ones are European shop builds with DSG gearboxes (vd Veer 3.6 + DQ500; HGP 3.6 bi-turbo + RS3 DQ500), HPA's turnkey 2.5T VR6 program (US$40,000 conversion, DSG only), one South African 3.2 + 6-speed manual Mk7, and one in-progress US 2.5T Golf R. None used an Atlas CDVC engine, none is an A3, and none is AWD + manual.

So: factory Bosch MED17.1.6 (PQ-generation 3.6, i.e. the NMS/CC/Touareg family, not Atlas), PQ DQ500 7-speed DSG, recipient was a diesel (EDC17) Golf GT, CAN gatewaying between PQ engine/gearbox and MQB body. AWD/FWD, mounts, cost and problems not stated; photo filenames suggest March–May 2021. — **VERIFIED** (vendor project page) [vd Veer Golf 7 R36](https://www.vdveer-engineering.nl/en/projects/2-golf-7-r36)

### Inferences
- **INFERRED:** An Atlas CDVC/CDVD into an A3 8V would be a first-of-its-kind build. The closest analogue for the electronics is vd Veer's PQ-ECU-plus-CAN-converter approach; the closest for the drivetrain is the "R32 02E/DQ250 bellhousing on the Mk7 DQ250" trick, which matters because the NA A3 2.0T quattro already has a 6-speed DQ250 (section 5).
- **INFERRED:** Every finished MQB VR6 precedent kept a DSG; HPA explicitly refuses manual cars. No precedent exists for VR6 + manual + AWD in MQB; the only manual one (Team Nerdo) is FWD and its electronics are undocumented.
- **INFERRED:** The forum claim that the Atlas right-side engine mount "bolts straight into" the Golf 2.0T location conflicts with HGP's "none of the mounts match" — but HGP used an older PQ Passat 3.6 (different mount/bracket generation), so both can be true. The Atlas-mount-to-MQB-A1-rail fit remains a forum claim only.

### Gaps
- No build sheet, cost, ECU strategy or problem log for any MQB VR6 swap in North America; the Vortex and golfmk7 threads could not be read.
- No precedent for TT 8S, Jetta Mk7 (A1 in NA), Tiguan Mk2 or Arteon found; an MQB Tiguan/Teramont VR6 exists only as factory China/Russia-market product (not researched).
- vd Veer's 4Motion/FWD status, mounts and price for the R36 job unknown.

## 2. EA855 2.5 TFSI five-cylinder swaps into Golf R/S3/GTI as integration evidence

### Takeaway
RS3 (DAZA) engine swaps into MQB Golfs are common enough that a US shop (Innovative Motorsports) sold tiered packages and several European shops (SAR-Turbotechnik, MTR-Performance) have repeated them; the recipe is "take the whole RS3 drivetrain": engine, DQ500, Haldex, rear diff and often subframes/brakes, then rewire the bay. No documented DAZA swap into an S3 8V specifically was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Innovative Motorsports (US):** installs the DAZA "into any and all MQB-chassis cars"; Stage 1 "OEM+" package = RS3 engine + 7-speed DCT + RS3 rear diff + RS3 Haldex + custom downpipe + IMS exhaust; ~500 whp with optional tune; Stage 2 700+ hp, Stage 3 1,000+ hp; typical job ~5 weeks starting from a wrecked RS3 donor; shared MQB architecture means no custom metalwork; difficulty is electrical/software because the bay is "essentially rewired"; a 2017 Alltrack for Dubwerx makes ~450 whp on Stage 2. No price published. — **REPORTED** (press, search snippets, ~May 2022) [The Drive, 5-cyl Alltrack](https://www.thedrive.com/news/audi-five-cylinder-swapped-vw-golf-alltrack-is-just-better); [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/580667/vw-golf-r-inline-five/) (Motor1 returned HTTP 502)
- Motor1's Golf R build: "almost every major component was replaced, including the subframe"; result "a completely functional five-cylinder Golf R without any instrument cluster lights"; "the entire engine compartment is basically rewired". — **REPORTED** (search snippet) [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/580667/vw-golf-r-inline-five/)
- **MTR Performance + SAR-Turbotechnik (Germany), Golf R Mk7:** RS3 2.5 TFSI, TTE625 turbo, 600 PS / 700 Nm, kept "the DQ500 seven-speed DSG and 5th generation 4MOTION". — **REPORTED** [Engine Swap Depot](https://engineswapdepot.com/?p=72602)
- **AKA Performance (Ridderkerk, NL), Golf R Mk7:** DAZA from an RS3 installed by SAR-Turbotechnik, TTE/MTR700 turbo, DQ500, "rear RS3 subframe", tuning by MTR-Performance; claims 8xx hp / 9xx Nm, no dyno. — **REPORTED** [Engine Swap Depot](https://engineswapdepot.com/?p=25537)
- **Agron Coma build:** crashed RS3 + Spektrum Mk7 Golf R; "powertrain, subframes, and brakes all came from the RS3", DQ500 to RS3 rear differential, ~550 hp. — **REPORTED** [Engine Swap Depot](https://engineswapdepot.com/?p=106909)
- **Dutch Mk7.5 Golf R:** DAZA, TTE700, MTR software, 800+ hp / 800 Nm. — **REPORTED** [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/510673/vw-golf-r-rs3-engine/); [drives.today](https://drives.today/articles/1816/tuning/daza-swapped-dutch-950bhp-volkswagen-golf-r-mk7-5-with-the-ultimate-spec.html)
- Turbo Engineers sells a DAZA-to-8V.1 fitting kit (turbo, downpipe, charge pipe, intake) for LHD and RHD, "tweaks will be required depending on car model". — **REPORTED** (vendor listing snippet) [EuroAutoPro / TTE](https://www.euroautopro.com.au/product-page/the-turbo-engineers-audi-dvx-fitting-kit-for-rs3-8v)
- RS3 8V DQ500 mechatronic is called the car's "Achilles heel": limp-mode codes around 60–80k miles. — **REPORTED** (blog) [Three Piece](https://www.threepiece.us/blog/8v-audi-rs3-buying-guide-400hp-sleeper-worth-65k-in-2025/)
- Factory precedent: VW is reported to be preparing a five-cylinder Golf R for ~2027 (394 hp / 480 Nm), after Ingolstadt resisted in 2020. — **REPORTED** [carz.com.my](https://www.carz.com.my/2025/08/report-vw-golf-r-set-to-gain-5-cyl-heart); [Motor1 ES](https://es.motor1.com/news/770219/volkswagen-golf-r-motor-5-cilindros-rumores/)

### Inferences
- **INFERRED:** The five-cylinder swaps are "easy" for the same reason a VR6 would be hard: VW already built an MQB A1 car (RS3/TT RS) with that engine, so mounts, subframe, Haldex, axles, cooling, wiring and ECU software all exist as factory parts on the same CAN architecture. An A3 8V is the RS3's own body, so for that car a DAZA swap is close to a factory parts-bin job — whereas the Atlas VR6 has no MQB A1 parts set at all.
- **INFERRED:** The shops' universal habit of rewiring the bay and moving the donor's ECU/TCU together shows the MQB gateway/immobilizer is solved by keeping the full matched donor electronics set, not by reflashing the recipient's ECU.

### Gaps
- No Innovative Motorsports price; no S3 8V-specific DAZA thread; whether the A3/S3 8V body needs the RS3's specific front crash bar/radiator package for DAZA cooling was not established.

## 3. Bay-size evidence: EA390 vs EA855 vs EA888, and Atlas vs MQB A1

### Takeaway
The RS3 8V proves an MQB A1 bay carries a transverse five-cylinder (~160 kg) plus DQ500; the 3.6 VR6 is about 28 kg heavier (BWS figure, 188 kg) but is described as "much shorter" than an inline-6 for transverse packaging. All MQB cars share front-axle-to-pedal-box geometry and engine position, so the length question is favourable; no published side-by-side engine dimensions were found.

### Cited Findings
- All MQB cars "share the same front axle, pedal box and engine positioning, despite varying wheelbase, track and external dimensions." — **REPORTED** (Wikipedia) [Wikipedia MQB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group_MQB_platform); also "Only the distance between front axle and pedal box will be fixed" [Autozine](https://www.autozine.org/Archive/Audi/old/A3_2012.html)
- Atlas VR6: "narrow 10.6° cylinder-bank angle and single DOHC cylinder head make it nearly as narrow as an inline six but much shorter for transverse packaging in FWD-based architectures." — **REPORTED** (SAE-derived article snippet) [Mobility Engineering Tech](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/43113-sae-ma-02396). Atlas 3.6: 276 hp / 266 lb-ft, 10.6° [The Autopian](https://www.theautopian.com/its-finally-the-end-for-volkswagens-legendary-vr6-engine-heres-why-it-was-so-great/); CDVC = EA390, 3,597 cc, 276 hp [auto-data.net](https://www.auto-data.net/en/compare&carId1=30407&carId2=&carId3=) — **REPORTED**
- 3.6 VR6 weight: "Weight, kg 188" for code BWS (Passat/CC 2008–2015 3.6; whether dressed not stated). No Atlas CDVC weight found. — **REPORTED** (spec aggregator) [mymotorlist BWS](https://mymotorlist.com/engines/volkswagen/ea390/bws/)
- EA855 evo (DAZA): "Weighing in at 160 kg" (Bar-tek); aluminum crankcase replaced compacted graphite iron, 26 kg lighter than the CGI predecessor (PASMag says 39.7 lb ≈ 18 kg — conflict). — **REPORTED** [Bar-tek](https://www.bar-tek.com/the-new-2.5l-tfsi-ea855evo); [PASMag](https://pasmag.com/features/vehicle-features/evolution-tuning-audi-s-newest-five-banger?start=1); [Donkiespeed](https://donkiespeed.nl/continuation-cast-iron-version-2-5-tfsi/)
- RS3 8V sedan curb weight 1,630 kg / 3,594 lb vs S3 sedan 3,461 lb (~1,570 kg) vs A3 sedan 1,450 kg; 2015 A3 sedan 2.0T quattro "Weight" 1,525 kg. — **REPORTED** (TrueDelta/iSeeCars) [TrueDelta](https://www.truedelta.com/Audi-A3/specs-8-2018&r=3); [iSeeCars RS3](https://www.iseecars.com/car/2018-audi-rs_3-specs); [iSeeCars S3](https://www.iseecars.com/car/2018-audi-s3-specs); **VERIFIED** (spec page) [Guide Auto 2015 A3 quattro 2.0T](https://guideautoweb.com/en/makes/audi/a3/2015/specifications/4dr-sdn-quattro-2.0t-progressiv)
- Atlas: 5,037 mm long, 1,979 mm wide (VW) or 1,990 mm (Wikipedia), "the largest MQB model to date"; VR6 transverse. — **REPORTED** [VW newsroom](https://volkswagen-newsroom.com/en/atlas-3563); [Wikipedia Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Atlas)
- HGP: "the MQB platform was not designed for this engine except on the Atlas". — **REPORTED** [autoevolution](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/golf-7-r-gets-36l-bi-turbo-v6-and-rs3-gearbox-from-hgp-113029.html)

### Inferences
- **INFERRED:** RS3 (1,630 kg) minus S3 (~1,570 kg) ≈ 60 kg is the whole-car penalty of the 5-cyl + DQ500 + RS hardware; a dressed 3.6 VR6 (188 kg BWS figure) vs 160 kg DAZA suggests the VR6 swap would add roughly 28 kg more over the nose than an RS3 carries, i.e. a nose-heavy A3. Treat as order-of-magnitude only (different measurement bases).
- **INFERRED:** Because the VR6 is shorter (6 cylinders in a staggered 10.6° block) than the inline-5 that already fits, block length is unlikely to be the binding constraint; the likely constraints are the front-to-rear depth of the staggered block and its intake/exhaust manifolds against the firewall and radiator, the right-side mount bracket, the A/C compressor/alternator positions, and the DQ250/DQ500 bellhousing interface — none measured in public.

### Gaps
- No published EA888 Gen3 or Atlas CDVC dry weights or L×W×H dimensions found; "MQB37W"/"MQB A2" naming and any Atlas-specific front-structure differences (rail spacing, subframe) could not be confirmed — the only sourced statement is that all MQB share front-axle/pedal-box/engine position.

## 4. Swap vendors, standalone ECUs with MQB CAN integration, and tuners

### Takeaway
No vendor sells an MQB VR6 swap kit, harness or calibration for an A3; the only off-the-shelf MQB-VR6 product is HPA's US$40,000 VR550T (2.5T, Golf R DSG only). Pieces exist: vd Veer CAN converters and a €799 standalone Haldex controller, Syvecs plug-in ECUs that preserve MQB dash/CAN (but only for EA888), and generic standalones (MaxxECU, ECUmaster EMU Black, Haltech) whose MQB dash/DSG/ABS support is unproven.

### Cited Findings
- **Syvecs S7D-VW7.5RDF** "Golf Mk7.5 R / GTI TSI E888 GEN3 – S7D Plug in ECU": engine code CYFB "plus closely related engine codes", Golf R Mk7/7.5 and **Audi S3 (8V)**; "Dash, CAN communications, safety systems, diagnostics, and drivability are preserved"; live tuning of "signals sent to the DSG"; cruise can only clamp set speed; price not listed; no swap/VR6 support mentioned. — **VERIFIED** (vendor page) [Syvecs](https://www.syvecs.com/?p=8078); Mk7.1 variants [S7D](https://www.syvecs.com/?p=8038), [S7Plus](https://www.syvecs.com/?p=5016)
- **MaxxECU:** CAN analyzer can rebuild cruise on OEM switches; ESP and cruise otherwise lost (B5/B6 Audi context). — **REPORTED** [Audizine](https://dev.audizine.com/threads/best-new-standalone-ecu.714063/latest)
- **ECUmaster/Haltech + DSG:** a Mk2 Golf with DQ250 and Haltech IC7: "DSG does not move" on the Haltech dash; builder wrote a microcontroller translating ECUmaster CAN to MaxxECU protocol. EMU Black CAN speed must match (1 Mbps vs 500 kbps traps). — **REPORTED** [ECUmaster community](https://community.ecumaster.com/t/ecumaster-can-stream-dsg-and-haltech-dashboard/3900); [AEM EMU Black guide](https://documents.aemelectronics.com/techlibrary_ecumasterblack_qs.pdf)
- **vd Veer:** CAN Convertor RPM/DSG (no price), Haldex Gen1 standalone €799, Gen4 PnP wiring €65, VAG EHPS controller €300 (ex VAT). — **VERIFIED** [vd Veer](https://www.vdveer-engineering.nl/en/projects/2-golf-7-r36)
- **HPA:** VR550T conversion US$40,000 excluding car; HPA software handles CAN/TCU; HPA also sells "FTX VR6" kits referencing DQ500/MQ500. — **VERIFIED** [HPA](https://www.hpamotorsports.com/pages/hpa-vr550t-2-5l-vr6-program); **REPORTED** price [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/vw-tuner-is-building-turbo-vr6-swapped-mk7-5-golf-rs-with-550-hp)
- **APR** tuned the TCU in the VRSociety DDKA Golf R build. — **REPORTED** [VRSociety](https://vrsociety.tumblr.com/post/651662812635152384/oem-25l-vr6-turbo-in-a-mk7-golf-r-dewain-is)
- **Innovative Motorsports** is the US shop forum members point to for MQB swaps; **MTR-Performance / SAR-Turbotechnik** (Germany) do DAZA MQB calibrations. — **REPORTED** (see section 2)
- From the earlier Jetta report (not redone): APR's MED17 3.6 file is performance-only (US$449.95–459.95); immo-off services US$135–350; no tuner advertises a CDVB/CDVC swap or manual file. — **VERIFIED** in that report [APR EA390 MED17](https://www.goapr.com/products/software/ecu_upgrade/gasoline/6/vr6/parts/ECU-36L-EA390-MED17)

### Inferences
- **INFERRED:** For an A3 8V + Atlas VR6, the realistic electronics paths are (a) Atlas MED17.1.62 ECU with immo-off and a custom calibration plus CAN translation to the A3's gateway/DSG/Haldex (vd Veer-style, but Atlas-to-A3 is MQB-to-MQB, which may reduce translation compared with vd Veer's PQ-to-MQB), or (b) a standalone with a custom CAN map and a standalone Haldex controller, losing or rebuilding ESP/cruise/DSG integration. Both are custom work with no North American shop advertising it; HPA is the only shop that has shipped an MQB VR6 integration and prices it at US$40k with the car excluded.
- **INFERRED:** Integrated Engineering, Unitronic, 034Motorsport and EQT were not found advertising any MQB swap calibration; treat them as performance-file vendors, not swap integrators.

### Gaps
- No price for Syvecs S7D, vd Veer CAN converters, or Innovative Motorsports packages; no MaxxECU/Haltech/MoTeC MQB-specific CAN dash support page found; no quote for a CDVC-in-MQB calibration from any tuner.

## 5. North American A3 8V lineup 2015–2020 and the best VR6 recipient

### Takeaway
No A3/S3/RS3 8V sold in Canada had a manual: every NA car is S tronic DSG (2.0T quattro = 6-speed DQ250-family S tronic; 2017+ FWD 2.0T = 7-speed; RS3 = 7-speed with quattro). The best VR6 recipient on paper is a **2017–2020 A3 2.0T quattro sedan**: cheapest AWD 8V, already has the 6-speed S tronic that the R32-bellhousing trick applies to and the Haldex rear driveline, and is not a car whose value is destroyed by removing its engine (unlike S3/RS3).

### Cited Findings
- 2015 Canada launch: A3 sedan with "6-speed S tronic transmission and quattro all-wheel drive", four engine choices including 1.8 TFSI. — **REPORTED** (press) [Auto123](https://www.auto123.com/en/news/2015-audi-a3-sedan-pricing-unveiled/48284); Guide Auto: "No manual transmission" listed among cons — **REPORTED** [Guide Auto 2015 A3](https://www.guideautoweb.com/en/makes/audi/a3/2015)

- **INFERRED (manual):** Because no NA 8V had a manual, "AWD + manual" in an A3 means also converting the gearbox, pedal box, shifter and DSG-related CAN/ESP coding — a second swap on top of the engine swap with no MQB precedent. The A3's realistic VR6 configuration is DSG + quattro.
- **INFERRED (trim ranking):** (1) 2017–2020 A3 2.0T quattro sedan — cheapest AWD, DQ250 + Haldex already present, facelift electronics; (2) 2015–2016 A3 2.0T quattro sedan — same drivetrain, older; (3) S3 — same drivetrain family with more brake/cooling hardware, but it already makes ~300 PS and costs more, so swapping its engine destroys value; (4) RS3 — already has DQ500 (the gearbox both European VR6 builds used) and RS cooling, but at CA$39k+ it is irrational to remove its engine; (5) cabriolet — quattro 2.0T available 2015–2019 but heavier, less rigid and roof-limited; (6) FWD 1.8T/2.0T and Sportback e-tron — no quattro, unsuitable.

### Takeaway
