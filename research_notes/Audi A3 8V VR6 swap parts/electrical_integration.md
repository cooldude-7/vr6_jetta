# Electrical integration of a VW Atlas 3.6 VR6 (CDVC, MQB, 2018–2023) engine ECU into a 2015–2020 Audi A3/S3 8V (MQB)

Scope: CAN gateway, CAN architecture, component protection (CP), immobilizer (Immo 5), SFD, instrument cluster, ABS/ESC, steering, Haldex, fuse/relay carriers, body harness and ECU pinout, power management and auxiliaries, standalone-ECU alternative, wiring-diagram access. North American vehicles only. Donor = 2018–2023 VW Atlas 3.6 FSI VR6 (CDVC, Bosch MED17.1.62, 8-speed Aisin AQ450, FWD or 4Motion). Recipient = 2015–2020 Audi A3/S3 8V, quattro preferred (2.0 TFSI EA888 Gen3, 6-speed DSG 0D9 per Ross-Tech).

## Key question 1: Does "same MQB generation" make the swap easier?

### Takeaway
Yes, more than on the PQ35 Jetta, but only around the engine ECU. Ross-Tech points both cars at the same Golf VII (5G) gateway and cluster pages. Both run the same Continental MK100 IPB brake controller with the same label file, the same MQB body-module family, and an MQB-generation Haldex controller (VERIFIED for the Atlas, INFERRED for the A3). The Atlas CDVC ECU already talks to an MQB gateway, MK100 ESC, MQB EPS and Haldex on 500 kBaud powertrain CAN. It does not make the ECU swap itself easier. Immo 5 makes the donor ECU harder to pair than the PQ35 Immo 4 case, and the A3's EA888 ECU (Continental Simos 18) and the Atlas's Bosch MED17.1.62 are different suppliers and connector systems, so the body-side ECU wiring is a complete re-map.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — The real gain from "same generation" is the CAN message set. The A3's gateway, MK100 ESC, MQB EPS, Haldex and cluster all speak the MQB powertrain message catalogue, and the CDVC software was released into exactly that environment (Atlas). So the "ECU present, torque/rpm messages missing or implausible" class of faults expected on the PQ35 Jetta should mostly disappear. What remains is variant mismatch: the ECU expects an AQ450 torque-converter TCU, a different Haldex calibration and an Atlas-coded gateway; the A3 modules expect an EA888 ECU and a DQ250 DSG.
- **INFERRED** — "Same generation" makes three things harder than on PQ35. (1) Immo 5 is FAZIT-server-based and Ross-Tech/VCDS cannot do it at all. (2) MQB modules are long-coded through SVM/online data, so coding a "wrong" engine into an A3 VIN fights the factory database. (3) The two ECUs are different hardware families (Simos 18 vs MED17.1.62), so the PQ35 shortcut of re-populating a same-family Bosch connector is gone.

## Key question 2: A3 8V electrical architecture (gateway, BCM, cluster, ABS, EPS, Haldex, airbag, MMI, CAN buses, coding)

### Takeaway
A3 8V = Audi MQB (AU37X): MQB gateway J533 (5Q0 907 530 x in early cars, a 3Q0 907 530 AC reported on a 2020 car), BCM J519 at 09, Golf-VII-family cluster at 17 (analog, or 8V0 920 790 x virtual cockpit on later cars), Continental MK100 IPB ESC at 03 coded by SVM, MQB EPS at 44 with G85 inside the rack, AirbagVW20 at 15, DQ250 DSG at 02, Haldex controller at 22 on quattro. - **INFERRED** — The quattro A3's Haldex controller at 22 and its DQ250 TCU at 02 are the recipient-side modules most sensitive to engine torque messages. They are also the ones Ross-Tech's wiki page leaves undocumented.

## Key question 4: Immobilizer (Immo 5), component protection and SFD: the hard blockers

### Takeaway
Hard blocker 1: Immo 5. Pairing the Atlas ECU to the A3's immobilizer needs ODIS online/GeKo (FAZIT), which VCDS cannot do. A reported RS3-into-S3 swap shows ODIS refusing to adapt an ECU that does not belong to the VIN. The realistic routes are a bench immo-off or immobilizer-data edit on the MED17.1.62 (commodity service, off-road labelled), or a pro shop's proprietary solution. Hard blocker 2: online coding. ABS/EPS/Haldex coding comes from SVM/factory data for the A3 VIN, which describe an EA888, so "correct" coding for a VR6 cannot be fetched online. It must be hand-coded or left as is. Component protection is not the main issue as long as every A3 module stays in the A3. SFD does not apply to a 2015–2020 A3 8V or a 2018–2023 Atlas per Ross-Tech's US list, but some late MQB EPS software can be SFD-locked.

## Key question 9: Donor-vs-recipient electrical parts list and ranked hard blockers

### Takeaway
Bring only the powertrain electronics: Atlas ECU, engine harness, the body-harness ECU tail/fuse-relay strip, and engine-mounted sensors and actuators (including the vacuum pump). Keep every A3 module: gateway, cluster, BCM, KESSY, airbag, ESC, EPS, Haldex, DSG TCU, HVAC, MMI, J367/battery. Blockers in order: (1) Immo 5 pairing (solve by MED17.1.62 immo-off or immo-data edit, since ODIS online will probably refuse); (2) ECU body-side wiring between Simos 18 and MED17.1.62 with no published pinouts (solve with erWin for both VINs); (3) variant mismatch on powertrain CAN (CDVC calibrated for AQ450 + Atlas Haldex; A3 modules SVM-coded for EA888 + DQ250), which needs a swap calibration and hand coding and has no precedent; (4) gateway LIN energy management (Atlas alternator on the A3 gateway). CP and SFD are not blockers if no module crosses cars and both cars are pre-SFD.
