Research date: 2026-10-08. Baseline already documented locally and **not repeated here**: Ninety4co's BLV + 02M manual swap into a 2010 Mk6 GTI (`/home/user/vr6_jetta/research/ninety4co-vr6-mk6-swap-series.md`).

Label key used on every claim:
- **VERIFIED** = read directly from a vendor product page, a government/regulatory document, or an article page that was fetched.
- **REPORTED** = forum thread, build post, social post, or a search-engine summary of a page that could not be opened. Where a page could not be opened, the bullet says "(search snippet; page not opened)".
- **INFERRED** = my reasoning from the above.

Access limits that shaped this file: VWVortex serves a proof-of-work bot challenge (HTTP 202) and its licensed-crawler gateway returns HTTP 402, so **no VWVortex thread could be read in full**; golfmk6.com and mhhauto.com returned 403; e-Laws/CanLII pages are JavaScript-only (the e-Laws Word source documents were downloaded instead); the shared web-search budget was exhausted before the last planned searches (Ninety4co cost figure, 42 Draft Designs, Integrated Engineering, "Dubbed Performance", UM MED17 support, Mk6 Jetta 2.0T/AWD swaps). ## Key question 1: Documented 3.6 VR6 swaps into PQ35-family cars (Mk6 Jetta, Mk6 Golf/GTI/R, Mk5 Golf/GTI/Jetta/Rabbit, Beetle A5, Eos, wagons)

### Takeaway
Outside Ninety4co, the documented PQ35 3.6 swaps are: one Mk6 Jetta (Colin Cusack's naturally aspirated 3.6 with a Cayenne manifold, Instagram-only, no technical detail), two or three Mk5 GTI BLV + 02M manual builds on VWVortex (one sold unfinished with a United Motorsport tune and incomplete wiring), two turbocharged Mk6 Golf R builds that used a DQ500 7-speed DSG (HPA FT740 in Canada, AW Racing in Poland), and one German Eos with an R36 engine. No completed Mk5 Jetta/Rabbit, Beetle A5, or Mk5/Mk6 wagon 3.6 swap was found, and no build using a CDVC/MED17 engine in a PQ35 car was found.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Every documented naturally aspirated PQ35 3.6 swap in North America is a **BLV on MED9 with an 02M manual**; the two PQ35 3.6 + DSG builds that exist are high-power turbo cars that went straight to a DQ500, and both are pro-shop builds. The parts list for a CDVC + 02E into a Mk6 Jetta therefore cannot stand on a direct precedent; it stands on (a) BLV manual precedents for the chassis side and (b) factory NMS Passat 3.6/02E pairing for the drivetrain side.
- **INFERRED** — The recurring failure mode in the Mk5 GTI threads is the same as in Ninety4co's series: mechanicals are bolt-on, and builds stall or sell unfinished on **wiring** (two Mk5 GTI threads, the for-sale car, the Mk6 Jetta thread's "need both schematics").
- **INFERRED** — Colin Cusack's Mk6 Jetta is the only proof that a 3.6 physically fits the Mk6 Jetta bay; the Cayenne intake manifold is a cosmetic/space choice and tells us nothing about ECU or gearbox. Worth a direct message on Instagram before committing to a fuel/cooling layout.

## Key question 2: CDVC/MED17 ECUs in non-NMS cars (immobilizer and CAN) and 3.6 + DSG into PQ35 cars

### Takeaway
No documented, running CDVC/MED17 swap into a PQ35 car was found; the only CDVC swap thread found (an Atlas 3.6 into an unnamed car) was stuck at "starts then dies" with a swap tune unfinished. The MED17 immobilizer problem is well served commercially (bench immo-off or identity-clone services from $135–$350), while the CAN side (cluster, gateway, ABS, DSG mechatronics) has no off-the-shelf solution and no precedent. On the DSG side, the only real-world PQ35 3.6+DSG cars are the two turbo Golf Rs on DQ500; forum reports say a Mk5 R32's 02E bolts to a 3.6, Passat FWD axles are longer than Golf axles, and the DSG's dogbone and mechatronics wiring differ from an automatic's.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — For a CDVC/MED17.1.6x ECU in a Mk6 Jetta the realistic routes are, in order of precedent: (1) bench **immo-off** (06A Technik $135, Speedo $350 for MED17.1, both label it off-road/diagnostic use) plus a swap calibration from a tuner willing to work on MED17 3.6 files (APR and Unitronic have stock-car MED17 3.6 files but neither advertises swap or immo work; UM lists only the MED9 3.6); (2) **identity clone/VIN edit** so the donor ECU presents the Jetta's VIN (Speedo $300; VinnieBuilt $175), which still leaves Immo-4 key/cluster matching to be done; (3) **dealer/ODIS online adaptation** of the used ECU to the Jetta's Immo-4 cluster, which Ross-Tech's notes imply needs GeKo access and may be impossible for "Challenge"-type units. Nobody has published doing any of these for a CDVC in a PQ35 car.
- **INFERRED** — The CAN integration risk is larger than the immobilizer risk: the Mk6 Jetta cluster/BCM/gateway expects a 2.5/2.0T/1.4T ECU's message set, and the 02E mechatronics expects a Passat gateway and selector. The MHH "starts then dies" symptom is consistent with either an immobilizer miss or a gateway/cluster mismatch; the thread never resolved it.
- **INFERRED** — The CDVC-specific hardware differences reported (oil filter flange, fuel rails, fuel-pressure sensor relocated to the valve cover) mean Ninety4co's BLV parts list cannot be copied blindly for the oil filter stand, fuel rail/sensor and harness pigtails.
- **INFERRED** — Because the NMS Passat 3.6 02E is FWD and the Jetta is FWD, the axle problem reported for Passat-into-Golf swaps (length) applies; GLI DSG axles are the obvious first candidate to test, with Passat inner joints as the fallback suggested on VWVortex.

## Key question 4: Mk6 Jetta-specific swap knowledge (any engine swap into a 2011–2018 Jetta)

### Takeaway
No completed 2.0T, VR6, R32-drivetrain or 4Motion swap into a Mk6 Jetta with a write-up was found. What exists is electrical troubleshooting lore: the Mk6 Jetta cluster shares a plug but not a pinout with the Mk6 Golf cluster, gateway location and even whether a separate gateway exists varies by trim, and a cluster/BCM swap with re-routed CAN produced a start-then-stall. All of it says the Jetta is not a Golf electrically, which is why Ninety4co's T94/T40 pin map cannot be copied.

### Cited Findings
- **REPORTED (search snippet; page not opened)** — VWVortex "Mk6 cluster in a Mk4 or Mk5": "The connector plug is the same. The pinout on the MK6 Jetta cluster and MK6 Golf cluster is different. ## Key question 5: Ontario/Canada angle — donor availability and prices, and Ontario requirements for an engine-swapped car

### Takeaway
2012–2018 NMS Passat 3.6 donors are scarce in Ontario but cheap when they appear: one Copart Canada Ontario sale (2013 SEL 3.6, front damage, run-and-drive) closed at CAD $1,700 in 2023, and current Kijiji asking prices for high-km 3.6 sedans are CAD $2,500 (as-is) to $6,000–9,000 (running). Ontario has had no light-duty emissions test since 1 April 2019, and the provincial regulation that briefly contained an explicit "motor replacement" rule (O. Reg. 457/19 s. 8) was revoked in 2022, leaving a Safety Standards Certificate (needed only on transfer/registration events, not for an engine change) and insurer disclosure (a 2026 Ontario decision capped an insurer's exposure at $200,000 for undisclosed performance modifications) as the practical requirements.

**Ontario requirements for an engine-swapped light-duty car**
- **VERIFIED (Ontario government page)** — Safety Standards Certificate: required to register a rebuilt vehicle, transfer a used vehicle to a new owner (except to a spouse), register a vehicle brought from another province/territory/country, or change a vehicle's status from unfit to fit; valid **36 calendar days** after inspection; issued by an MTO-licensed **DriveON Vehicle Inspection Centre** and emailed; fee set by the station; the page does not mention emissions testing or engine changes. — [Ontario.ca SSC](https://www.ontario.ca/page/safety-standards-certificate)
- **REPORTED (search snippet)** — The current passenger/light-duty inspection standard is the MTO "Passenger/Light-Duty Vehicle Inspection Standard" (2022-03-18), which superseded Schedules 1 and 2 of R.R.O. 1990 Reg. 611. — [MTO standard PDF](https://ontario.ca/files/2022-03/mto-passenger-light-duty-vehicle-inspection-standard-en-2022-03-18.pdf), [Reg. 611](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900611/v3)
- **VERIFIED (Environmental Registry decision notice)** — The requirement for light passenger vehicle owners to obtain a Drive Clean emissions test was removed on **1 April 2019**; "Emission control systems are still required to be present and operating", with on-road enforcement. — [ERO 013-3630](https://ero.ontario.ca/node/478)
- **VERIFIED (e-Laws source document, O. Reg. 457/19 as filed)** — O. Reg. 457/19 (Vehicle Emissions) revoked O. Reg. 361/98 and came into force 1 January 2020. Its s. 8 "Motor replacement" read: "No person shall replace a motor in a motor vehicle unless the replacement motor, (a) is designed to meet emission standards at least as stringent as those achieved by the original motor with all its original systems and devices to prevent or lessen the discharge of any contaminant attached and functioning; and (b) has all the systems and devices to prevent or lessen the discharge of any contaminant usually installed, attached or incorporated by the manufacturer of the replacement motor." Its s. 9 "Tampering" barred "removing, bypassing, defeating or rendering inoperative" any emission system "including for greater certainty an OBD system" and barred modifying a vehicle "in any way that results in increased emissions"; it also barred operating a vehicle with an illuminated MIL and an active emission-related DTC. — [O. Reg. 457/19 (e-Laws .doc)](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/docs/R19457_e.doc), [e-Laws page](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/r19457)
- **VERIFIED (e-Laws source document, O. Reg. 183/22 as filed)** — O. Reg. 183/22 amended O. The motor-replacement, tampering and MIL rules in 457/19 therefore ceased to exist on those dates. — [O. **Insurance disclosure (Ontario)**
- **VERIFIED (legal news article)** — *Aviva Insurance Company of Canada v. Cahill, Litigation Administrator*, 2026 ONSC 4975: the insured added a supercharger, headers, performance clutch, cooling kit and racing tires to a 2011 Corvette in 2018 without telling Aviva; the court relied on OAP 1 s. 1.4.1 (duty to notify of significant changes affecting risk or willingness to insure), statutory condition 1 of O. Reg. 777/93, and s. 233(1) of the Insurance Act; Aviva had no duty to defend four civil actions and its exposure was limited to **$200,000 under s. 258(4)**; the judge said "any reasonable insurer would consider that as a significant factor" and found Aviva would have declined the risk under its rule 7.2.2. — [Law Times](https://www.lawtimesnews.com/practice-areas/personal-injury/man-who-modified-corvette-breached-insurance-contract-superior-court/393526)
- **REPORTED (search snippets)** — *ING v. Merino* (Ont. C.A.): Ontario auto insurers cannot void a policy ab initio; misrepresentation invalidates the claim, not the contract. A rates.ca piece reports an Ontario insurer denying a ~$35,000 claim over an undisclosed exhaust change. — [Canadian Underwriter](https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/legal/intact-loses-court-battle-over-voiding-auto-policy-for-misrepresentation), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/insurance-laws-and-products/805066/no-void-ab-initio-for-auto), [rates.ca](https://rates.ca/resources/what-can-void-your-car-insurance?amp)

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — A complete NMS 3.6 donor bought at Copart Ontario through a broker should land in the CAD $1,500–4,000 range before fees, based on the one Ontario sale and US comparables; a running high-km private-sale donor is CAD $2,500–6,000. Donor frequency is low (two Ontario Kijiji hits, one Ontario Copart record found), so a US donor imported for parts is a realistic alternative, and for a parts-only donor the Ontario brand is irrelevant.
- **INFERRED** — With no light-duty emissions test, with O. Reg. 457/19's motor-replacement rule revoked, and with the SSC triggered only by transfer/registration events, the swapped Jetta's provincial compliance exposure is (a) passing an SSC whenever the car changes hands and (b) the insurer. Keeping the CDVC's full emissions hardware (both cats, both banks of O2 sensors, EVAP/LDP) and an MIL-free tune remains prudent because federal anti-tampering and any HTA emissions provisions were not verified here.
- **INFERRED** — *Cahill* makes written disclosure of the engine swap to the insurer before the car is driven the single most important paperwork step; a 3.6 in a 1.4T/2.5 Jetta is at least as "material" as a supercharger on a Corvette.
