# Plumbing for a 3.6 VR6 (NMS Passat CDVC/CDVB) in a Mk6 Jetta GLI: cooling, A/C, fuel, intake, exhaust

Scope: which cooling, A/C, fuel, intake and exhaust parts fit a 2012–2018 NMS Passat 3.6 in a 2015–2018 Mk6 Jetta GLI (manual) bay, compared against the Ninety4co Mk6 GTI/BLV baseline. Labels: **VERIFIED** = read from a parts-catalog/dealer fitment page, a factory document, or a vendor product page; **REPORTED** = forum, build-thread, marketplace listing or search snippet whose page could not be opened; **INFERRED** = my reasoning from the above. Baseline items are from the local copy of Ninety4co's parts list (`/home/user/vr6_jetta/research/sources/ninety4co-swap-parts-list.md`) and are REPORTED unless re-verified here. No part number below is invented; where I could not find a number it is listed as a gap.

Access limits that shaped this file: every VW dealer parts catalog tried (parts.vw.com dealers on the RevolutionParts platform: Jim Ellis, Checkered Flag, Hendrick) and ECS Tuning returned Cloudflare challenge pages to both WebFetch and curl; eBay item pages returned 403; VWVortex could not be read (see the vendor file). So the Ninety4co hose numbers (1K0 121 049 CM, 1K0 122 086 B, 1K0 122 157 HE, 3C0 121 063 E, 1K0 965 561 B) and the Eos A/C line 1K0 820 743 CE could not be confirmed against a catalog from this environment and remain REPORTED. A dealer parts counter with a 2017–2018 Passat 3.6 VIN and a 2016 Jetta GLI VIN can close most of the gaps in one visit.

## Cooling: how the 3.6 routes coolant and which NMS Passat / "2018 Passat GT" hoses apply

### Takeaway
The only hard catalog facts found are that the NMS Passat 3.6 (engine code **CDVC**, and **CDVB** in VW's 2016 Passat engine-code table) is a close relative of the B6 BLV and that the B6 3.6 return side uses a Y/tee "upper hose with check valve" (3C0 121 086 M). Ninety4co's five hose numbers are all 1K0 (Golf 5 family) or 3C0 (Passat B6 family) prefixes, which is consistent with his statement that the 2018 Passat GT uses the same hoses, but I could not open a catalog page to prove any of them fit a 2017–2018 Passat 3.6. Treat his list as a strong REPORTED starting point and verify by VIN.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED (factory document)** — VW of America's 2016 Passat service bulletin engine-code table lists "3.6L VR6: CDVB" for the 2016 Passat (table rows "3.6L CDVB"); the same bulletin family covers 2016 Passat service-reminder reset. — [NHTSA TSB MC-10168305-0001 (PDF text extracted locally)](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2019/MC-10168305-0001.pdf)
- **VERIFIED (Ross-Tech wiki)** — Ross-Tech names "3.6L CDVC FSI engines" explicitly (P310B/P008B notes, G410 loose-pin tech tip TT 20-18-06TT), so CDVC is a real NMS 3.6 code; both CDVC and CDVB exist and the model-year split between them was not verified. — [Ross-Tech wiki P310B/012555](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/P310B/012555)
- **REPORTED (dealer catalog search snippet; page blocked)** — Hendrick VW lists **3C0 121 086 M** as "Engine Coolant Hose, Hose Assembly, Upper Hose, Incl. - **REPORTED (Ninety4co doc, local copy)** — His OEM-style hose set on the BLV in a Mk6 GTI: upper+lower radiator hose assembly **1K0 121 049 CM**; radiator outlet to after-run pump **1K0 122 086 B**; heater-core lower hose listed with the same **1K0 122 086 B** (he suspects a typo); heater-core upper hose **1K0 122 157 HE**; after-run pump outlet hose **3C0 121 063 E**; after-run pump **1K0 965 561 B**; he says "if you keep an OEM-style system, the hoses are the same as a 2018 Passat GT; look them up on parts.vw.com". ### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — The NMS Passat is a PQ46 derivative, so it is plausible that its 3.6 hoses carry 1K0 (PQ35) and 3C0 (B6) numbers exactly as Ninety4co lists; this is consistent with, but not proven by, the evidence above. A dealer lookup on a 2018 Passat GT (3.6) will settle it in minutes.
- **INFERRED** — Because the Gray swirl pot deletes the bottle and the 3C0 121 086 M tee/check-valve return assembly, the swap's remaining OEM hoses are the two-in-one radiator hose (1K0 121 049 CM), the after-run pump loop (1K0 122 086 B, 3C0 121 063 E, pump 1K0 965 561 B) and the two heater hoses. Those are the five numbers to verify first.
- **INFERRED** — The Mk6 Jetta heater core and firewall stubs are PQ35 (its Mk5 sibling used 1K0 122 157 xx heater pipes), so Ninety4co's "trim a factory Mk6 heater hose" approach should transfer, but the Jetta's hose lengths will differ because its bay is longer than the Golf's; buy the Jetta's own 2.0T heater hoses and expect to trim.
- **INFERRED** — Ninety4co's Gray Fab bottle choice sidesteps the question of where the NMS coolant bottle sits; keeping the OEM NMS bottle would require the NMS bottle bracket and bottle-to-head hard pipe, none of which were found in any catalog here.

### Gaps

- CDVC vs CDVB differences (if any) in coolant routing or after-run pump control: no source.
- After-run pump control on a CDVC MED17 ECU in a Jetta harness (relay/J496 wiring): not covered; belongs with the electrical researcher.

## Radiator, fan and condenser: Mk6 Jetta GLI numbers, Golf vs Jetta differences, R32/2.5 radiator fit, aftermarket options

### Takeaway
The Mk6 Jetta runs two radiator families: the 2.0T GLI/2.0L-AT/2.5 cars use the Jetta-specific **5C0 121 251 D/E**, while Valeo/Mahle list Golf-family **5K0 121 251 S** (2015–16 Jetta) and **5K0 121 251 AA** (2008–13 GLI). The large PQ35 radiator **5K0 121 251 H** is catalogued by CSF as one part for Jetta 2.5L, Eos 3.2, CC/Passat 3.6 and TT, which is the strongest evidence that an R32/2.5-size core fits a Mk6 Jetta front end; Mishimoto's MMRAD-MK5-08 fitment (via Three Piece US) lists 2011–2013 Mk6 Jetta rows including GLI, while Summit says R32-only. The Jetta's fans are the same 1K0 959 455 xx family as the Golf, with the J293 control module inside the large fan.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Since CSF sells one radiator (5K0 121 251 H) for the Jetta 2.5L and for the CC/Passat 3.6 and Eos 3.2, the Mk6 Jetta 2.5L radiator *is* a VR6-rated core and bolts into the Jetta lock carrier. The Mishimoto MMRAD-MK5-08 is Mishimoto's version of that same large-core footprint, which explains why Three Piece's fitment table lists Mk6 Jetta rows. Confidence that MMRAD-MK5-08 bolts into a 2015–2018 Jetta GLI: moderate; the GLI's own 5C0 121 251 D/E (2.0T) radiator has a different number, so the GLI lock carrier must accept both footprints only if the mounting points are shared. Measure or ask Mishimoto before ordering.
- **INFERRED** — The GLI's stock 2.0T radiator (5C0 121 251 D/E) is the small-core 2.0T size; given Ninety4co's "lot of heat" warning and that VW specified the large core for the 2.5L and the VR6s, the GLI radiator should be considered inadequate for a 3.6. The cheap OEM-style path is the Jetta 2.5L/VR6 radiator (CSF 3777 or OE 5K0 121 251 H); the aluminium path is MMRAD-MK5-08. No CSF all-aluminium race radiator for the Mk6 Jetta was found.
- **INFERRED** — Fan control: the CDVC/BLV ECU drives the fans through the J293 module inside the PQ35 large fan, the same arrangement as the Jetta's own 1K0/1KM 959 455 fans, so the Jetta's fans should work electrically as Ninety4co's Mk6 Golf fans did; the connector/pin-out difference between 2.0T and VR6 fan variants (PWM vs stepped) was not verified.
- **INFERRED** — The Jetta-specific radiator support (5C6 805 588 R) and the Jetta's own 5C0 A/C line numbers show the Mk6 Jetta front end is *not* identical to the Golf Mk6's, so Ninety4co's "stock Mk6 fans/condenser" lines should be read as "stock Mk6 Jetta fans/condenser", which the shared 1K0 fan family makes plausible.

## A/C: 3.6 compressor and position, Jetta Mk6 lines vs the 3.6 compressor, NMS lines, the Eos 3.2 suction line, condenser reuse

### Takeaway
The NMS Passat 3.6 uses 561-prefixed A/C lines (liquid line 561 820 741) and the B7-era compressor family (5N0 820 803 E appears for the Passat B7), while the Mk6 Jetta uses Jetta-specific 5C0 lines (liquid 5C0 820 741 A; discharge 5C0 820 721 S for 1.8/2.0 TSI only). Ninety4co's fix on the Golf was the PQ35 VR6 suction line 1K0 820 743 CE with compressor 1K0 820 808 F; the 1K0 820 743 xx family is the PQ35 compressor-to-evaporator hose family (Mk5 Jetta 2.0T used 1K0 820 743 FG, "Eos, GTI, Jetta, Jetta GLI, Jetta Wagon"), so the Eos line is plausible on a Jetta but unproven. Condenser reuse is REPORTED on the Golf and INFERRED for the Jetta.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — The 3.6's compressor sits on the VR6 accessory bracket (low, driver's side), so the compressor-end fitting of any PQ35 suction line and discharge line must come from a VR6 application. Ninety4co used the Eos 3.2/R32 suction line because the Golf/Eos/Mk5-Jetta evaporator fitting is common (1K0 820 743 family). The Mk6 Jetta's evaporator fitting is probably the same PQ35 block (its Mk5 predecessor was), but the Jetta's bay is longer, so the Eos line may reach but route differently; verify the firewall block before buying.
- **INFERRED** — Ninety4co's doc says only the suction line changed; his GTI's discharge line (compressor to condenser) evidently fit the 3.6 compressor. On the Jetta, the discharge line is Jetta-specific (5C0 820 721 S, 1.8/2.0 TSI compressor position only), so a Jetta discharge line for the 3.6 position does not exist as an OEM part; options are an Eos 3.2/R32 1K0 820 721 xx discharge line (if the Jetta condenser inlet matches the Golf's) or a custom-crimped hose. This was not verifiable here and is the most likely custom-line item.
- **INFERRED** — Use the CDVC's own compressor (the 2012–2018 Passat 3.6 compressor; number unresolved, B7-era 5N0 820 803 family appears) or the PQ35 VR6 1K0 820 808 F, whichever matches the CDVC bracket and the Jetta's R-134a lines; the Jetta GLI's own compressor is the TSI unit and will be wrong for the bracket.
- **INFERRED** — Condenser reuse: the Jetta condenser is a Jetta part sized for the Jetta carrier; reuse is reasonable (Ninety4co reused the Golf's) as long as line fittings match; no page gave its number.

## Fuel: CDVC low-pressure spec and J538, the GLI's pump and J538, the NMS pump module and tank flange, filter/regulator, EVAP/LDP

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Ninety4co's fuel layout = Mk5 BPY / B6 BLV architecture: a demand-controlled in-tank pump driven by J538 (3C0 906 093 A) with a return-less 4.0 bar filter/regulator (6Q0 201 051 J) acting as the pressure limit at the filter. That works because the BLV ECU expects that architecture. The CDVC (NMS) expects the regulator inside the pump module (3C8 919 051 A "includes pressure regulator") and a J538; whether the CDVC MED17 calibration targets 4 bar or ~6 bar on the low side was not found, and Ross-Tech's 4.0-vs-6.0 bar warning means this must be read from the CDVC repair manual before choosing a filter.
- **INFERRED** — The Jetta GLI (CPLA/CPRA TSI) already has a demand-controlled in-tank pump and a J538 under the rear seat, so the simplest path is to keep the GLI's tank, pump and J538, wire J538's control input to the CDVC ECU's pump-control output and feed the CDVC's HPFP; flow margin for a 300 hp NA engine at ~6 bar is unknown because the GLI pump's flow spec was not found. Fitting the 3C8 919 051 A module in the Jetta tank is unlikely without checking flange diameter and sender arm (B6/NMS tanks are PQ46; the Jetta tank is PQ35), so treat the GLI pump + CDVC J538 arrangement as the default and the NMS module as not a drop-in.
- **INFERRED** — Because the 3C0 906 093 A J538 fits both the B6 and the 2012–2017 NMS, it is the right module regardless of BLV vs CDVC; the Jetta GLI's own J538 (TSI, 1T0/5C0 family) may be electrically similar but its CAN/PWM protocol compatibility with MED17 3.6 calibrations is unverified.
- **INFERRED** — EVAP/LDP: Ninety4co kept the BLV's EVAP hardware intact from a second donor; the Jetta's charcoal canister and LDP sit at the tank, and the CDVC's purge valve is on the manifold, so the physical join is one purge hose; no location conflict found, but no source checked NMS vs Jetta canister/LDP part numbers.
