Donor engine 2012–2018 NMS Passat 3.6 (CDVC); gearbox Mk4 24V VR6 02M (FWD); recipient 2015–2018 Mk6 Jetta GLI 6MT (NCS/A6, VIN prefix "16-"). ### Takeaway
The Mk6 Jetta's engine-bay mounting interfaces (right engine mount, left transmission mount console, dogbone, shift cables, clutch slave family, front springs) carry the same 1K0-prefix PQ35 part numbers as the Mk6 Golf/GTI, so Ninety4co's Mk6-GTI mount recipe transfers; the Jetta-specific parts are the front subframe (5C0 199 313 K/N, which it shares with the NMS Passat 3.6 donor and the Beetle A5), the lock carrier (5C6 805 588 family), the radiator (5C0 121 251 E) and, on 2015+ cars, some Jetta-only axles/knuckles (5C0 407 271 BQ / 5C0 407 272 CP / 5C0 407 255 A).

## Q1. Is the Mk6 Jetta (NCS/A6) front structure the same PQ35 mounting geometry as the Mk5/Mk6 Golf? Subframe and engine-mount part numbers compared

### Takeaway
Yes at the mount interfaces: the Jetta Mk6 uses 1K0 199 262-family engine mounts (1K0 199 262 CP and CE) that are the same part numbers as the Mk6 Golf/GTI, Beetle A5, CC and NMS Passat, and the same 1K0 199 555 trans mounts and 1K0 199 855 dogbones as the Golf Mk6. The front subframe itself is a Jetta number (5C0 199 313 K → N) shared with the NMS Passat and Beetle A5, not with the Golf (1K0 199 313 H → AL).

Any PQ35 mount that bolts into a Mk6 GTI should bolt into a Mk6 Jetta GLI.
- INFERRED: The Jetta subframe differs from the Golf's in some dimension (the A6 has a wider body/track and VW gave it a new number), but it keeps the PQ35 mount/dogbone/steering interfaces, since the same 1K0 dogbones and mounts attach to it. Keep the Jetta subframe; do not swap in a Golf one.
- INFERRED: Because the NMS Passat 3.6 (CDVC donor) was built on the same 5C0 199 313 N subframe, the subframe-mounted envelope (steering rack position, dogbone bracket, control-arm pickup points) under the Jetta has carried a 3.6 VR6 in production. Firewall and lock-carrier clearance are a different question (Q9).

### Gaps

The AR mount is the factory right mount on the Eos 3.2, Mk5 R32 and all B6/B7/NMS Passat 3.2/3.6 cars; it is a 1K0 199 262-family part whose chassis footprint is the same console/pocket the Jetta Mk6's own 1K0 199 262 CP/CE mounts use, so it should bolt to the Jetta frame rail unchanged (REPORTED in a Mk6 GTI; INFERRED for the Jetta). BFI's 6-cylinder Mk5/Mk6 mount does not name the Jetta Mk6 but lists the Passat 3.6 and Mk5/Mk6 R32; 034's Mk5/Mk6 2.0T mounts explicitly list the Mk6 Jetta GLI, confirming the aftermarket treats GLI and GTI frame-rail geometry as identical.

## Q4. Transmission mount for a Mk4 24V 02M in a Jetta Mk6: 2-bolt vs 3-bolt patterns, Jetta GLI 02Q mount numbers, the 1K0 199 555 AP solution

### Takeaway
All PQ35 left-hand trans mounts (AP, Q, N, CA, CH) share the same chassis-side console (1K0 199 295 F / 296 F) and are fitted interchangeably across Golf Mk5/Mk6 and Jetta Mk5/Mk6 in the catalog, so the mount choice is set only by the bolt pattern on the gearbox case: Ninety4co's 2-bolt Mk4 02M took the 2.5L 5-speed/09G mount **1K0 199 555 AP**, and a 3-bolt 02M (Mk2 TT 3.2 / Mk5 R32 source) takes the 6-speed manual/DSG mount **1K0 199 555 Q** (or the Jetta's own CH/CA). The GLI's factory 02Q mount is 1K0 199 555 CH.

### Cited Findings
- REPORTED — Ninety4co: "A Mk5/Mk6 2.5L 5-speed transmission mount works with our 24-valve 02M. I did not have to modify a single thing"; his doc: 1K0 199 555 AP (PQ35 2.5L 5-speed / 09G Tiptronic) "works with his 2-bolt Mk4 02M. ## Q10. Suspension and brakes for the heavier engine: VR6 weight over the front vs the GLI's 2.0T; R32/Eos 3.2 front springs; Jetta-specific options

### Takeaway
The Eos 3.2's standard front springs (1K0 411 105 BC / BD, "761–795 kg" front-axle class, 12.8 mm wire) are PQ35 parts the factory catalog also lists for the Jetta Mk6, so an OEM "VR6-weight" front spring exists for the Jetta; the only swap-specific suspension data point is a Mk5 GTI 3.6 build on Mk6 GTI springs sitting ~20 mm lower in front, with advice to go 10–20% stiffer. No source gives the 3.6's installed weight versus the 2.0 TSI, and nothing was found on GLI brake adequacy.
