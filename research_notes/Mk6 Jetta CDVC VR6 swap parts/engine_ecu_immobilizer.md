## Key question 1: Which NMS Passat years/trims carry the 3.6, what it makes, Canadian availability, and what the engine code actually is

### Takeaway
The 3.6 VR6 FSI was offered in every NMS Passat model year 2012–2018 (US: top trims; 2018 GT and SEL Premium) and in Canada every year through 2018 (Comfortline/Highline/Execline depending on year, plus a handful of 2018 GTs), always with the 6-speed DSG; it was dropped for 2019 in both countries. VW's factory SSP rates the NMS engine at 280 hp at 6600 rpm and 258 lb-ft from 2400–5300 rpm on Bosch Motronic MED17.1. **The engine code is contested and the project's working label "CDVC" is probably wrong for the Passat**: VW's own SSP prints "BWS", a Ross-Tech forum regular and a used-engine seller say the NMS 3.6 is **CDVB**, and every source that places **CDVC** puts it in the **2018+ Atlas/Teramont** (with CDVD as a later Atlas variant). Read the code off the donor's engine sticker/VCDS before buying anything by code.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Weight of evidence: the NMS Passat 3.6 (2012–2018) is **CDVB**, running Bosch **MED17.1.6** in the **03H 906 023** ECU family; **CDVC** is the **2018+ Atlas** engine (MED17.1.62, 03H 906 026 family); **CDVD** is the later Atlas/Teramont variant (12.0:1 per tuning sheets, see Key question 3). VW's "BWS" in SSP 800153 looks like a carried-over spec block (BWS is the 300-PS B6 R36/CC code elsewhere) and should not be used to order parts. **Action: every parts lookup for the donor should be done by VIN, and the swap documentation should be re-labelled "CDVB (NMS) / CDVC (Atlas)" once the donor's sticker confirms it.**
- **INFERRED** — Because output (280 hp) and torque (258 lb-ft) are identical across 2012–2018 and the SSP says the 2016 facelift's 3.6 was carried over, any NMS year is an equivalent donor mechanically; the 2016+ cars have a different body-electrical set (Running Gear CAN, MIB II) that matters only for which body-side modules you might scavenge.
- **INFERRED** — Canadian donors exist for every year but are rarest for 2017 (Highline-only) and 2018 (GT "very small quantity"); 2012–2016 Comfortline/Highline/Execline V6 cars are the realistic Ontario salvage pool.

## Key question 2: How the NMS 3.6 (CDVB/"CDVC") differs from the B6 BLV (MED9.1.1): intake, PCV, injectors/HPFP, oil filter housing, mount bracket, exhaust, sensors, dimensions

### Takeaway
Hard, sourced differences are thin. VW's SSP lists the NMS engine's features (3.6-bar oil pressure, non-engaged chain tensioner, one-part oil pump sprocket, 89 °C thermostat, 32° exhaust cam adjuster, 7-bolt damper) and the ECU family change (MED17.1 vs the BLV's MED9.1.1 / 03H 906 032); parts listings show a new oil pan family (03H 103 601 AK/AD vs the CC-era 03H 103 601 J) and shared valve-cover/PCV numbers across 3.2/3.6. Intake manifold, injector/HPFP, O2-sensor count and MAF/MAP details for the NMS engine were not found in any catalog page this pass; treat them as open until a VIN lookup is done.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — The one-piece oil-pump sprocket and non-engaged tensioner that Ninety4co retrofitted to his BLV are factory fitments on the NMS engine per the SSP; the NMS donor does not need that upgrade, only inspection.
- **INFERRED** — The pan supersession (03H 103 601 J for CC to mid-2011, then 03H 103 601 AD/AK shared by Atlas/Passat CDVB/CDVC/CDVD) indicates the lower-end castings were revised around the 2011–2012 changeover and then held constant through the Atlas; it does not by itself prove the block's mount-bracket bosses changed. No source says the external dimensions or engine-mount bracket differ between BLV and CDVB; the FWD transverse layout and PQ46-derived NMS chassis make identical bracket bosses likely, but this is unverified.
- **INFERRED** — Because the NMS 3.6 is ULEV 2 with "three-way catalytic converter with lambda control" (SSP), expect the same two-bank, four-sensor O2 arrangement the BLV has (Ninety4co pinned B1/B2 sensor 1 and 2); the sensor part numbers and wideband/narrowband types for the NMS were not found.

### Gaps

- SSP 823603 ("3.2L and 3.6L Engines") would answer most mechanical-difference questions; only a retailer listing selling it was found, no accessible copy.

## Key question 3: Is the Atlas 3.6 (CDVC/CDVD, 2018–2023) the same long block, and could it wear the Passat's intake/harness/ECU?

### Takeaway
The Atlas 3.6 is the same EA390 family and shares the oil pan, timing chain and (per aggregator pages) the 11.4:1 / 89 × 96.4 mm architecture with the Passat engine; sources split the Atlas into a 2017–2019 variant (MED17.1.6, 11.4:1, 280 hp) and a 2020+ variant (MED17.1.62, 12.0:1, 276 hp), and VW's own ECU family for the Atlas (03H 906 026 x, MED17.1.62) differs from the Passat's (03H 906 023 x). No source documents an Atlas long block running under a Passat harness/ECU, and nothing itemises head/cam/injector differences, so an Atlas core is plausible but unproven as a drop-in for the Passat's electronics.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Block, crank (89 × 96.4), pan family and timing hardware are shared, so a 2018–2019 Atlas CDVC (11.4:1, MED17.1.6 per the tuning sheets) is the closest low-mileage core to the Passat CDVB; a 2020+ CDVD/12.0:1 engine likely has different pistons and calibration and is a worse match for the Passat ECU.
- **INFERRED** — The Atlas ECU (03H 906 026 x, MED17.1.62) is wired for a 4Motion/8-speed Aisin vehicle and is not a substitute for the Passat's 03H 906 023 x; the realistic Atlas path is **Atlas long block + Passat intake, harness, injectors/HPFP and ECU**, which no source has documented. Expect to verify injector part numbers, cam-adjuster/sensor types and HPFP before committing.

## Key question 5: Immobilizer generation and master module on the NMS Passat; what it takes to run the ECU in a Jetta

### Takeaway
VW's SSP describes the NMS as Immobilizer IV/KESSY-style: the Access/Start module J518 asks the Anti-Theft Immobilizer Control Module **J362** for terminal 15, J519 (BCM) reads the key and forwards transponder data to J362, J362 releases the steering lock J764 over Convenience CAN and "authorizes the ECM to start the engine"; **the DSG mechatronic J743 also sends its own authorisation request to J362**. Which physical box houses J362 is not stated (VW's TechConnect article says late Passats put it in J519 or J518; Ross-Tech's NMS page links its cluster to the Golf 5K family). Locksmith sources call it Immo IV with Megamos ID48 keys and a PIN read over OBD. For a Jetta swap the practical route remains an immo-off flash (or a clone onto a known-good ECU) because the Jetta has no J362 that knows the Passat ECU; adaptation via SKC is theoretically an Immo IV option but needs the donor PIN and a "VWZ"-type component.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED (factory SSP)** — "Starting Authorization Procedure … The Start System Button E378 is pressed. The Access/Start Authorization Control Module J518 processes the signal, wakes the convenience CAN-Bus and asks the Anti-Theft Immobilizer Control Module J362 [if] terminal 15 can be turned ON … The authorized key … sends its transponder data at a frequency of 433 MHz to J519. J519 forwards this transponder data to J362. J362 verifies the transponder data. If the key is authorized, J362 sends the command via the convenience CAN-Bus for release of the electric steering column to the Electronic Steering Column Lock Control Module J764. This same message is transmitted to the Vehicle Electrical System Control Module J519 and, at the same time, also via a discrete wire so that terminal 15 is turned ON … As soon as all of the data buses are awake, immobilizer communication across all buses can take place. The Anti-Theft Immobilizer Control Module J362 authorizes the ECM to start the engine if the data match is successful. If the DSG Transmission Mechatronic J743 is installed, it sends its query and authorisation request to J362." — [SSP 800153](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10121132-9999.pdf)
- **VERIFIED (VCDS log)** — 2018 Passat GT: Address 25 Immobilizer present, coding 000007, no faults; Address 05 Acc/Start Auth present. — [Ross-Tech forums 40349](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/40349/)
- **VERIFIED (Ross-Tech wiki)** — NMS module map shows 05 Access/Start, 09 BCM, 17 cluster (Golf 5K-family page), 25 Immobilizer, 2B steering column lock; BCM only talks to VCDS with all doors unlocked. — [Ross-Tech wiki VW Passat (NMS/A3)](https://wiki.ross-tech.com/wiki/index.php/VW_Passat_(NMS/A3))

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — In the Jetta there is no J362/J518 pairing for the Passat ECU, so a stock CDVB ECU will crank and immediately stall (the classic immobilizer symptom; see the MHH "starts then dies" CDVC thread in the precedents file). The two workable routes: (1) **immo-off in the ECU flash**, ideally done by the same tuner who writes the manual-swap calibration so it is one file; (2) **adapt the Passat ECU to the Jetta's own Immo IV cluster** with both PINs (Jetta cluster SKC + donor PIN) per Ross-Tech's Immo IV ECU-swap procedure, which only works if the ECU is a "VWZ"-type adaptable component and leaves the Jetta's anti-theft intact. Route 2 has no documented precedent on a 3.6 MED17 and is the one to test on the bench before cutting harnesses.
- **INFERRED** — For the DSG secondary case, the SSP shows the mechatronic J743 is itself an immobilizer participant; a transplanted 02E would need its own immo pairing or defeat in addition to the ECU's.
- **INFERRED** — The Passat's KESSY/J518 and steering-column lock J764 do not need to come with the engine; the Jetta's own key/column lock handles terminal 15, and nothing in the SSP suggests the ECM needs KESSY-specific CAN messages beyond the immobilizer release itself (which immo-off removes).

### Gaps
- Which physical module hosts J362 in the NMS (cluster vs BCM J519 vs KESSY J518): VW's SSP does not say; the TechConnect article and the Ross-Tech cluster-page linkage point in different directions.
- Whether the 03H 906 023 x ECU is an adaptable ("VWZ") Immo IV component that VCDS can re-pair to a Mk6 Jetta cluster: no report found either way.
- Whether a 2016+ NMS (post-facelift) uses a different immobilizer/key chip (the "ID88 hybrid" forum claim): unverified.

## Key question 7: What the NMS 3.6 ECU expects on CAN (gateway, ABS, steering, cluster, DSG), and common swap faults/fixes

### Takeaway
VW's SSP documents the NMS network: gateway J533; a 500 kbit/s Powertrain CAN carrying the ECM J623, DSG mechatronic J743, TCM J217, ABS J104, airbag J234, power steering J500, selector lever J587 and parking assist; a separate Instrument Cluster CAN; a 100 kbit/s Comfort CAN with BCM J519, KESSY J518, steering-column electronics J527, column lock J764 and the steering-wheel module; and an Extended CAN where **J533 controls alternator charging over LIN**. Removing the DSG makes the ECM, ABS and other modules log missing-TCU messages (Ross-Tech), and swap threads across VAG platforms show U0101 "no communication with TCM", "press clutch pedal to start", "ECU incorrectly coded" and gateway-list write errors as the standard failure set; the fixes are tuner-side message suppression, correct Byte-1 coding, gateway installation-list edits, and ABS coding. Mk6 Jetta-specific CAN behaviour toward a foreign ECU is undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- **VERIFIED (factory SSP)** — "The data bus diagnostic interface J533 is still the main Gateway … A – Diagnosis CAN, B – Powertrain CAN, C – Instrument Cluster CAN, D – Comfort CAN, E – Extended CAN, F – Infotainment CAN, G – K-wire. A-C = 500K Bit/s, D-G = 100K Bit/s." Powertrain CAN members: J623 ECM, J743 DSG mechatronic, J217 TCM, J104 ABS, J234 airbag, J500 power steering, J587 selector lever sensor, J791 parallel parking. Comfort CAN: J519 BCM, J518 KESSY, J527 steering column electronics, J764 column lock, J453 MFSW, J255/J301 HVAC, door modules, G397 rain/light, Virtual Pedal. Extended CAN: "J533 acts as the load management and controls the alternator charging via LIN-Bus." 2016 adds a Running Gear CAN for ACC/lane assist. — [SSP 800153](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10121132-9999.pdf)
- **VERIFIED (Ross-Tech forum, Uwe)** — With the transmission electrically disconnected and removed from the gateway installation list, "a number of other modules will start complaining about missing messages from the TCU." — [Ross-Tech forums 40349](https://forums.ross-tech.com/index.php?threads/40349/)

- **REPORTED (OBD reference)** — U0101 = no valid TCM data on CAN for a set period; suspects: TCM, CAN wiring, terminating resistors, engine ECU, software. — [Autodoc club U0101](https://club.autodoc.co.uk/obd-codes/u0101)

- Cross-reference (`precedents_vendors_salvage.md`, REPORTED there): Mk6 Jetta cluster shares a plug but not a pinout with the Golf cluster; a Jetta cluster/BCM swap with re-routed CAN produced a start-then-stall; MHH CDVC swap "fires up, stays running for a few seconds, then cuts out".

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — In a Mk6 Jetta GLI (also a PQ35-family car with MK60EC1 ABS, Golf-5K-family BCM and a cluster-based Immo IV) the Powertrain CAN message set the CDVB ECU expects is close to native except for three things: the DSG/TCM messages (must be suppressed in the tune), the immobilizer release (immo-off or re-pairing), and any NMS-specific KESSY/column-lock interplay (not required once immo is handled). ABS/ESP torque-request and wheel-speed messages, steering angle and brake switch are standard PQ35 Powertrain CAN traffic the Jetta already produces.
- **INFERRED** — The LIN-controlled alternator is a hidden trap: if the NMS alternator comes with the engine, its charging is commanded by the gateway over LIN; the Jetta's gateway/BCM must be able to drive it (Mk6 Jettas also use LIN-regulated alternators, so it is plausible but must be checked), otherwise fit the BLV-era alternator Ninety4co used (06F 903 023 F) or a conventionally regulated unit.
- **INFERRED** — Expect this fault set on first start and plan fixes in advance: U0101/"TCM missing" (tune suppression + gateway list), ABS/ESP "ECM implausible signal" (ABS coding for manual, VSS source), cluster "incorrectly coded"/"press clutch" prompts (cluster coding), EVAP/O2 (keep OEM hardware), and immobilizer-active (immo-off). The MHH and Jetta cluster-swap "runs a few seconds then dies" cases are immobilizer symptoms, not CAN faults.

### Gaps
- No documented list of the exact CAN IDs/messages the MED17.1.6 ECM requires versus tolerates; no swapper has published a CDVB-in-PQ35 fault log.
- Whether the Mk6 Jetta GLI BCM/gateway supplies LIN alternator control compatible with the NMS alternator.

## Key question 8: Must-replace/inspect items on a used NMS 3.6 before install, with part numbers where found; weak points and TSB history

### Takeaway
The factory NMS engine already carries the one-piece oil-pump sprocket and non-engaged chain tensioner, so the known BLV-era oil-pump-bolt fix is built in; what remains is the transmission-side timing set (two chains, two tensioner rails, two guide rails, upper tensioner; shared iwis/Febi chain 066109503C/25404 and upper chain 03H 109 503(B) are catalog-verified across Passat/CC/Atlas), the belt-driven water pump, the 89 °C thermostat, valve cover/PCV (03H 103 429 family), the oil pan family 03H 103 601 AD/AK, and the HPFP drive sprocket position VW itself flagged on CDVC. Bearing failures and carbon buildup have no VW document behind them; the only bearing data point is Ninety4co's spun BLV rod bearing after a top-end-only rebuild.

### Inferences
- **INFERRED** — Pre-install list for an NMS donor, in priority order: (1) full timing set while the engine is out (transmission side; chains 066109503C-equivalent/03H 109 503 B upper, lower chain, both tensioner rails, both guide rails, upper tensioner 066109507D-equivalent, oil-pump chain 021109465B-equivalent) and inspect the one-piece oil-pump sprocket bolt; (2) rod and main bearings (Ninety4co's failure argues for a bottom-end inspection on any 150,000+ km core); (3) water pump, 89 °C thermostat, serpentine belt, tensioner/idlers; (4) valve cover 03H 103 429-family with gasket and PCV diaphragm; (5) HPFP drive sprocket position check per the VW tech tip and new HPFP/injector seals; (6) oil pan gasket/pan if damaged (03H 103 601 AK family); (7) ECU connector condition and a new engine harness if connectors are brittle; (8) intake-valve carbon cleaning (direct injection; no VW document found, so treated as an inference).
- **INFERRED** — Kit shopping is unreliable by vehicle filter (FCP's Passat and Atlas kit pages do not state engine codes); order chains/tensioners by the OEM numbers above or buy the Atlas 3.6 kits (FCP KIT-00290/00299) after confirming each line item against the NMS VIN.

### Gaps
- OEM part numbers for the NMS 3.6 tensioner rails/guide rails, lower chain, oil pump, oil-filter housing, thermostat housing, water pump and HPFP were not retrievable from a VIN-filtered catalog page; the numbers above come from Atlas/CC listings that also name the Passat.
- No VW TSB on 3.6 bearings, oil-pump bolts, or chain stretch surfaced for the NMS years (the only VW documents found are SSP 800153 and the Atlas HPFP tech tip); CarComplaints entries are owner reports.
- Whether the Schimmel spin-on housing or the Gray Fabrication bottle fits the NMS engine's oil-filter flange and vacuum-pump cover: unverified (same gap as the precedents file).
